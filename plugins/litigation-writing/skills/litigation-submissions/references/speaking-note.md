# Speaking note

## What it is for

A private script for the advocate on their feet: what to say first, what to fall back to, what the other side will say and the scripted answer, and how far to give ground. It is never filed and never handed up. Because it is private, it may use the first person ("My primary submission is …") and telegraphic cue lines. It is still built on the record: every point carries the bundle reference the advocate will take the court to.

Write it for the person who will speak. If that is not the drafter (a solicitor-advocate or junior taking the hearing), spell out what the drafter knows and the speaker may not: the history behind a point, the trap in a document, what the client will accept.

## Structure (all systems)

1. **Housekeeping.** Anything that must be dealt with before the merits: an objection to a late paper (cite the direction it breaches by its date and paragraph number, and give the reasons, strongest first), documents the court should have, time estimates.
2. **A. Primary position.** One sentence. Then the supporting points, numbered, four or five at most, each with its reference.
3. **B. Fallback.** When to move to it (only if the court signals it is against the primary position), and the one sentence that introduces it. If the fallback is an order, have the order drafted (in the hearing note or skeleton) and cite its paragraph.
4. **C. If they say … → reply.** The points the other side is likely to make, two or three at most, each with the scripted answer and the reference that proves it. Keep the reply to one or two lines.
5. **D. Questions from the bench.** The questions the court is likely to ask, with short answers and references.
6. **E. Concessions.** The number to concede and the line not to cross ("Concede no more than [period] days; resist anything longer"), with the reason, so the advocate can decide on the spot.
7. **F. Order of argument and time.** Which point first, which last, and the time for each.
8. **G. What each outcome gives the client.** The practical result of each likely ruling and the next step it opens, so the hearing is argued with that step in view.

Follow the court's own structure if it has directed one ("the Court asked three questions"): use its headings and fold the rebuttals into each part rather than adding a separate rebuttal section.

**De-duplicate.** A speaking note assembled from the skeleton and the reply tends to repeat itself. Each point appears once, in the place it will be said.

## Differences in the US profiles

None in substance. Oral argument in US courts is often shorter and more question-driven, so put more weight on part D, and keep the record citations in the court's form (`ECF No. [number] at [number]`). Address the court as the judge prefers ("Your Honor"); the note may script the form of address.

## Skeleton

```
SPEAKING NOTE: [Plaintiff]
Hearing before [judge] on [date 1] at [time]

Housekeeping
1. Object to the [Defendant]'s supplemental paper dated [date 2]: filed without leave, after the deadline in paragraph [number] of the order of [date 3] [Bundle ref].

A. Primary position
2. My primary submission: the Summons should be dismissed. Four points:
   (1) … [Bundle ref]
   (2) … [Bundle ref]
   (3) … [Bundle ref]
   (4) … [Bundle ref]

B. Fallback (only if the Court is against me on A)
3. If the Court is minded to extend time: short extension only, on unless terms. Draft order at paragraph [number] of the hearing note.

C. If they say … → reply
4. If they say: the delay was caused by … → Reply: their own letter of [date 4] says otherwise [Bundle ref].
5. If they say: no prejudice → Reply: [number] months lost; trial date at risk [Bundle ref].

D. Questions from the bench
6. Why not costs in the cause? → The [Defendant] caused the hearing; costs should follow it [Bundle ref].

E. Concessions
7. Concede no more than [period] days. Resist anything longer. Do not concede the unless consequence.

F. Order of argument
8. Housekeeping (2 minutes); A (10 minutes); C as they arise; B only if pressed.

G. What each outcome gives the client
9. Dismissal: judgment can be sought at once. Short extension on unless terms: judgment follows automatically on default.
```

## Checklist

The checker tests: a primary position and a fallback are stated; anticipated points with replies (soft); a concession limit (soft). First person is allowed here and nowhere else in this skill. Numbered paragraphs are not required, but number the points anyway; it helps under pressure.

Check by eye:

- Every point has its reference.
- Each anticipated point has a reply of one or two lines.
- The concession is a number or a precise term, with the line not to cross.
- Nothing is repeated.
- Nothing in it would embarrass the client if it were seen; it is private, but notes go astray.
