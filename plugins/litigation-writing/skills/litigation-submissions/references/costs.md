# Costs submissions

## What it is for

Written submissions on costs: after a hearing where the court reserved costs or directed written submissions, on a summons to vary a costs order, or in answer to the other side's claim for uplifts. The court has a wide discretion and little patience. What persuades is a clear account of who won what, measured fairly, a plain concession of the order that is plainly right, and a short, separate fight about each uplift.

**Do not choose the law.** The costs rules, the basis of assessment, the test for indemnity costs and for a certificate for counsel come from the rules and authorities the user supplies (`[rule]`, `[authority needed: …]`).

## Structure: Hong Kong

**Caption.** Action number; the title with the direction it answers (`[Plaintiff]'S SUBMISSIONS ON COSTS PURSUANT TO THE DIRECTION OF [judge] DATED [date]`); the party line; a defined-terms line for the summonses.

**Sections.**

1. **A. OVERVIEW.** The direction, and the costs in issue, each with its defined term: "the costs of the [Defendant]'s summons dated [date] ("the Security Summons") and of …". Then the outcome of each, measured in a way the court can check: the issues won and lost, and the hearing time each took ("[number] hours of the [number]-hour hearing were spent on the issue the [Plaintiff] won"). Then the findings the court is invited to make in one sentence, and "The orders sought are set out at paragraph [number] below."
2. **B. LEGAL PRINCIPLES.** Short, from the user's authorities, one sub-section each: B1 the discretion and partial success; B2 the indemnity basis; B3 the certificate for counsel; B4 summary assessment or taxation, and payment forthwith, where they are in issue.
3. **C. [Defendant]'S SUMMONS** and **D. [Plaintiff]'S SUMMONS**, one section for each order in issue. Inside each:
   (a) concede the base order where it is right, in one sentence ("The [Plaintiff] does not dispute that the costs of … should be paid by the [Plaintiff] to the [Defendant] on a party and party basis.");
   (b) then the pivot: "The [Plaintiff] resists each of the uplifts sought:";
   (c) one sub-heading per uplift (indemnity basis, certificate for counsel, payment forthwith), each ending on a one-line answer ("A certificate for counsel is neither necessary nor proper.");
   (d) where the court made an adverse finding, quote it accurately, then confine it: say what it did not find.
4. **E. THE STATEMENT OF COSTS** (where quantum is in issue). The items challenged, by item number, each with the reason. Put the figures in an annexed comparison table, not in prose: columns for each statement and the difference.
5. **F. CONCLUSION.** The orders, lettered, one per summons, each with its basis, assessment and certificate; alternatives stated ("alternatively, no order as to costs").

**A costs-variation summons** (for example, on a sanctioned offer) uses the skeleton's structure with one framing sentence: "There is one issue: whether …". Then the cascade: the point that decides it alone; and even if the court undertakes the full analysis, the result is the same. An authority the court itself raised gets its own heading.

**Building the costs case earlier.** In a skeleton, the costs paragraph is drafted from page one: the basis sought, and for indemnity costs a numbered list of the conduct relied on, each item with its reference. See `skeleton.md`.

## Differences in the US profiles

The American rule means each side usually bears its own attorney's fees; fee-shifting needs a statute, rule or contract, which the user identifies. Costs in the narrow sense (filing fees, transcripts) usually go to the prevailing party. In federal court, costs are claimed in a bill of costs taxed by the clerk (FRCP 54(d)(1); 28 U.S.C. § 1920), and attorney's fees by motion within the time and with the content FRCP 54(d)(2) requires: the judgment, the basis of entitlement, and the amount or a fair estimate. California uses a memorandum of costs and a separate motion for attorney's fees (CRC 3.1700, 3.1702). In every profile, a fee motion is supported by a declaration with time records and rates (drafted with litigation-evidence), and argues reasonableness from the user's authorities. Sanctions for frivolous conduct are a separate application (for example 22 NYCRR 130-1.1 in New York). The structure above still works: overview, entitlement, the base claim, each disputed item or uplift under its own heading, and the proposed order.

## Checklist

The checker tests: numbered paragraphs; a CONCLUSION heading; orders sought in the last third; costs terms (basis, assessment or taxation); a concession of the base order (soft); no bullets.

Check by eye:

- The measure of success can be checked against the record (issues, hearing time, the order made).
- The base order is conceded where it is plainly right, before the fight.
- Each uplift has its own heading and its own one-line answer.
- Every figure in the text matches the annex and the costs statements.
- The orders cover every summons in issue, with basis, assessment, certificate and timing.
