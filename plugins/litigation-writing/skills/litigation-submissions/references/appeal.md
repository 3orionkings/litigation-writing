# Appeal submissions (substantive)

## What it is for

The skeleton for the hearing of an appeal, for the appellant or the respondent. The appeal court reads the judgment below and the grounds; it wants to know which findings or rulings are wrong, why, and what order should replace them. It does not want the trial re-run. The strongest ground goes first, because the court may stop when it is persuaded and because a weak first ground colours the rest.

## Structure: Hong Kong

**Caption.** Appeal number, title (`SKELETON SUBMISSIONS OF THE [Appellant]`), hearing line (`For the hearing on [date] at [time]`; the bench is normally not named), and the legend, which declares the decision tag and the appeal bundle scheme.

**Sections.**

1. **A. OVERVIEW.** One paragraph that gives the whole appeal: `It is the [Appellant]'s case that:` with each proposition followed by its ground number in brackets, in strength order. No rhetorical question, no slogan.
2. **B. BACKGROUND.** Two or three paragraphs, then the agreed chronology as an annex. Quote the judge's own findings where they are the evidence for the error.
3. **C. GROUND [strongest]: [the proposition as a sentence].** Grounds may be taken together where they overlap ("GROUNDS 2 AND 4: …"). Each ground:
   (a) opens with the error and its tag: "The Judge erred in holding that … [Decision/§n].";
   (b) states the law, from the user's authorities, as numbered propositions;
   (c) applies it: "Applying those principles:" then First, Second, Third;
   (d) closes with the consequence, citing the same paragraph of the decision: "It follows that the Judge erred in …".
4. **The standard of review.** Where the decision below was discretionary or a finding of fact, say once, near the start or inside the ground it affects, why the appeal court can interfere (an error of principle, a finding without evidence), from the user's authority.
5. **D, E … the remaining grounds,** in strength order. A ground with nothing to add is better abandoned than argued thinly; ask the user.
6. **Respondent's notice or cross-appeal** in its own section, if any.
7. **CONCLUSION.** One characterising sentence. Then the orders: the appeal allowed; the order below set aside; the order substituted, drafted in full; costs here and below.

**For the respondent.** Take the appellant's grounds in the appellant's order (the court will), each ending on why the judge was right. Answer the appellant's skeleton by its labels (`[A§n]` for the appellant's paragraphs) inside each ground. Ask for the appeal to be dismissed with costs.

## Differences in the US profiles

The profiles cover trial courts, so flag the appellate court's own rules for the user. In a federal court of appeals the brief follows FRAP 28(a): disclosure statement, tables of contents and authorities, jurisdictional statement, statement of the issues, statement of the case, summary of the argument, the argument with the standard of review for each issue, a short conclusion stating the precise relief, and a certificate of compliance with the length limit under FRAP 32. State appellate courts have their own equivalents. US appellate briefs are not usually in numbered paragraphs; follow the court's rule, and tell the user if the checker's numbering test then reports. Record citations follow the court's appendix or record scheme.

## Skeleton

```
[case number]

SKELETON SUBMISSIONS OF THE [Appellant]
For the hearing on [date 1] at [time]

Legend: [A12/§3] = Bundle A, page 12, paragraph 3; [Decision/§n] = paragraph n of the judgment of [judge] dated [date 2]

A. OVERVIEW

1. It is the [Appellant]'s case that:
   (a) the Judge applied the wrong test to … (Grounds 2 and 4);
   (b) on the right test, the finding at [Decision/§40] cannot stand (Ground 1); and
   (c) … (Ground 3).

B. BACKGROUND

2. … [Bundle ref]. The background is set out in the Agreed Chronology annexed.

C. GROUNDS 2 AND 4: THE JUDGE APPLIED THE WRONG TEST

3. The Judge erred in holding that … [Decision/§35].

4. The correct test is [authority needed: the test the Judge should have applied]: First, …. Second, ….

5. Applying those principles: First, …. Second, … [Bundle ref].

6. It follows that the conclusion at [Decision/§35] cannot stand.

F. CONCLUSION

7. The Decision rests on a test the authorities do not support. The [Appellant] respectfully invites the Court to order that:
   (1) The appeal be allowed.
   (2) The order of [judge] dated [date 2] be set aside and in its place it be ordered that ….
   (3) The [Respondent] do pay the [Appellant]'s costs here and below, with a certificate for counsel, to be taxed if not agreed.

Dated this [date 3]

[counsel-name]
Counsel for the [Appellant]
```

## Checklist

The checker tests: numbered paragraphs; grounds identified; a CONCLUSION heading; orders sought in the last third; error language (soft); tables in the US profiles (soft); no bullets.

Check by eye:

- The overview maps every proposition to its ground number.
- Grounds run in strength order, and each opens and closes with the same decision tag.
- No new evidence and no new point that was not run below, unless the user has leave or a reason.
- The substituted order is drafted in full.
