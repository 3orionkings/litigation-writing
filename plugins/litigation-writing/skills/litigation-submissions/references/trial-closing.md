# Closing submissions

## What it is for

The written closing after the evidence: it tells the judge what findings to make, on what evidence, and what weight to give each witness, and then applies the law to those findings. A judgment is built from findings. So the closing is organised around the findings sought, and every one of them points to the transcript or the documents. Law takes less room than in an opening; the evidence takes more.

## Structure: Hong Kong

**Caption.** As for the opening, with the title `CLOSING SUBMISSIONS OF THE [Plaintiff]` and the date the closing is to be heard or lodged. Add the transcript to the legend: the user's scheme for day, page and line (or, for untranscribed evidence, the witness, the date, the day of trial and the session).

**Sections.**

1. **A. INTRODUCTION.** What closings are being exchanged and whether the other side's has been seen. The issues still live, cross-referring to the opening's list. What changed at trial: concessions in cross-examination, a witness not called, a document withdrawn.
2. **B. COMMON GROUND.** What is now agreed, with transcript references.
3. **C. THE EVIDENCE: THE [Plaintiff]'S WITNESSES.** One sub-section per witness: what they established, the points the other side attacked, and the answer. Concede the client's weak points first and plainly (a witness's limited education, the lapse of time, an inconsistency on a detail), then explain why they do not touch the core.
4. **D. THE EVIDENCE: THE [Defendant]'S WITNESSES.** One sub-section per witness, and inside it one heading per topic. For each topic, in this order: what the witness could know first-hand (not present, one occasion only, hearsay); internal contradictions; contradictions with the documents and with the witness's own side; then the weight submission in one line ("The Court is invited to give little weight to [witness]'s evidence on this topic."). Adverse inferences for documents or witnesses not produced go where the gap matters.
5. **E. EXPERT EVIDENCE.** Which expert to prefer, and why, by reference to the joint statement and the cross-examination.
6. **F. FINDINGS SOUGHT.** A numbered list of the findings of fact the court is invited to make, each with its references. This is the closing's core: a judge writing the judgment can lift it.
7. **G. THE LAW APPLIED.** Issue by issue, short, from the opening's statement of the law (cross-refer rather than repeat).
8. **H. QUANTUM.** Cross-refer to the opening where nothing changed; update the arithmetic where the evidence changed it; explain any difference from the pleaded figure.
9. **I. REPLY TO THE [Defendant]'S CLOSING.** If closings are exchanged first, answer by paragraph label (`[D§58]`), inside the sections above where possible, here only for what does not fit.
10. **J. CONCLUSION.** The judgment sought, interest, and costs.

## Differences in the US profiles

In a bench trial the written closing is often a post-trial brief, or proposed findings of fact and conclusions of law where the court orders them (FRCP 52(a) governs findings in a federal bench trial). Proposed findings are numbered, one fact each, each with its record citation (trial transcript by page and line, `Tr. [number]:[number]`, and exhibits by number); conclusions of law follow, numbered, with the authorities. Before a jury, the closing is oral; this variant then produces the speaking-note material, and jury instructions belong to a separate process. Check the profile and the trial order for length and timing.

## Checklist

The checker tests: numbered paragraphs; findings sought; weight submissions (soft); the evidence organised by witness (soft); a closing request (soft); no bullets.

Check by eye:

- Every finding sought has a transcript or document reference.
- Every attacked witness ends each topic with a weight submission.
- The client's own weak points are conceded and answered, not ignored.
- The other side's closing is answered by its paragraph labels.
- Quantum figures agree with the opening or the difference is explained.
