# New York

This profile covers civil actions in the New York Supreme Court, including its Commercial Division. The governing rules are the Civil Practice Law and Rules ("CPLR"); the Uniform Civil Rules for the Supreme Court and the County Court (22 NYCRR Part 202), including the Commercial Division Rules at 22 NYCRR 202.70(g); and the Rules on Costs and Sanctions (22 NYCRR Part 130). Citation follows the Official New York Law Reports Style Manual (2022 edition with the 2024 electronic update). CPLR 2106 was amended by L 2025, ch 564, effective November 21, 2025. Researched October 2026. Check the assigned justice's part rules before filing; they can add to or vary everything below.

## Document names

| Document type | New York name |
|---|---|
| Parties | Plaintiff and Defendant |
| Claim pleading | Complaint (CPLR 3011) |
| Defence | Answer, which may include a counterclaim against a plaintiff and a cross-claim against a defendant |
| Reply | Only to "a counterclaim denominated as such"; an answer to a cross-claim only if the cross-claim "contains a demand for an answer" (CPLR 3011). No other pleading unless the court orders |
| Counterclaim and others | Counterclaim; cross-claim; third-party complaint; interpleader complaint |
| Amendment | Amended complaint (or answer); on a motion for leave, the proposed pleading "clearly showing the changes or additions" (CPLR 3025(b)) |
| Evidence instrument | Affirmation under CPLR 2106, or affidavit; an attorney's affirmation for procedural matters |
| Witness statement | No separate form; evidence on a motion is an affidavit or affirmation of a person with knowledge |
| Skeleton | Memorandum of law |
| Hearing note | No equivalent; the nearest short paper is the Commercial Division pre-motion letter (Rule 24) |
| Application papers | Notice of motion or order to show cause, memorandum of law, supporting affidavits or affirmations and exhibits |
| List of authorities | Table of authorities in the memorandum (excluded from the word count) |
| Request for particulars | Demand for a Bill of Particulars (CPLR 3042), answered by a Bill of Particulars |

## Pleading standard

CPLR 3013: "Statements in a pleading shall be sufficiently particular to give the court and parties notice of the transactions, occurrences, or series of transactions or occurrences, intended to be proved and the material elements of each cause of action or defense."

- **A hybrid standard.** Plead the transactions and every material element of each cause of action. Pleadings "shall be liberally construed" and defects that prejudice no substantial right are ignored (CPLR 3026). The test on a motion to dismiss is New York's own, not federal plausibility (see "Background").
- **Form (CPLR 3014).** Plain and concise statements in consecutively numbered paragraphs, each containing as far as practicable a single allegation; separate causes of action and defenses separately stated and numbered ("First Cause of Action"); alternatives and inconsistent causes allowed; a writing attached to a pleading is part of it.
- **Corporate status.** Where a party is a corporation, the complaint says so and, where known, under whose laws it was created (CPLR 3015(b)).
- **Conditions precedent** need not be pleaded (CPLR 3015(a)).
- **Particular claims (CPLR 3016).** Libel or slander: the particular words. Fraud, misrepresentation, mistake, wilful default, breach of trust, undue influence: the circumstances "stated in detail". An action on a judgment: how far it has been satisfied. A claim or defense based on the law of a foreign country: "the substance of the foreign law relied upon shall be stated", so Hong Kong law relied on is pleaded in substance.
- **Relief (CPLR 3017).** A demand for the relief claimed, which may be in the alternative; the court may grant other relief appropriate to the proof except on default. A declaratory judgment demand specifies the rights to be declared and any consequential relief. In personal injury and wrongful death actions, no amount is stated; the defendant may request a supplemental demand, answered within 15 days.

## Answering a pleading

CPLR 3018(a): "A party shall deny those statements known or believed by him to be untrue. He shall specify those statements as to the truth of which he lacks knowledge or information sufficient to form a belief and this shall have the effect of a denial. All other statements of a pleading are deemed admitted, except that where no responsive pleading is permitted they are deemed denied or avoided."

- **Paragraph by paragraph.** Admit, deny, or deny knowledge or information sufficient to form a belief. No reasons and no positive case are needed inside a denial; the positive case goes in the affirmative defenses or a counterclaim. There is no Hong Kong "save and except" general traverse.
- **Commercial Division: interlineate (Rule 6(d)).** For every responsive pleading, reproduce each allegation of the pleading answered, keeping its content and numbering, with the response beneath it. The party who prepared the pleading must on request supply it in its word-processing format.
- **Affirmative defenses (CPLR 3018(b)).** Plead every matter that would otherwise take the other side by surprise or raise issues of fact not on the face of an earlier pleading, such as arbitration and award, collateral estoppel, culpable conduct in diminution of damages, discharge in bankruptcy, illegality, fraud, infancy or other disability, payment, release, res judicata, statute of frauds and statute of limitation. The list is not closed. Number each ("First Affirmative Defense").
- **Conditions precedent and signatures.** A denial of performance of a condition precedent is made "specifically and with particularity" (CPLR 3015(a)). Each signature on a negotiable instrument is admitted unless specifically denied (CPLR 3015(d)).
- **Verified complaint, verified answer.** Where the complaint is verified, the answer must be (CPLR 3020(a)). Some answers must be verified whatever the complaint (fraud on creditors or other fraud charges, a corporation sued on a note), and so must a defense not involving the merits (CPLR 3020(b)-(c)).
- **Counterclaims** are permissive on the text of CPLR 3019(a). No reply is served to affirmative defenses; issue is joined on the answer.

Answer formulae:
- "Admits the allegations of paragraph [number] of the complaint."
- "Denies the allegations of paragraph [number] of the complaint."
- "Denies knowledge or information sufficient to form a belief as to the truth of the allegations of paragraph [number] of the complaint."

## Particulars

- **Fraud and related wrongs.** "Where a cause of action or defense is based upon misrepresentation, fraud, mistake, wilful default, breach of trust or undue influence, the circumstances constituting the wrong shall be stated in detail." (CPLR 3016(b)). The detail need not amount to proof; facts permitting a reasonable inference of the conduct suffice (see "Background").
- **Bill of particulars (CPLR 3041-3043).** Any party may require another to give a bill of particulars of its claim, or a copy of the items of an account (3041). The demand is in writing and states the items on which particulars are wanted; the bill is served within 30 days and answers each item, except items objected to, for which the reasons are stated "with reasonable particularity" (3042(a)). A bill may be amended once as of course before the note of issue (3042(b)). The court may compel, preclude or strike on wilful failure, and may relieve against improper or unduly burdensome demands (3042(c)-(e)). In personal injury actions CPLR 3043 lists nine particulars that may be required, and a supplemental bill for continuing special damages may be served without leave up to 30 days before trial.
- **Goods and services.** In a verified complaint the plaintiff may set out and number the items of the claim with the value or price of each; the defendant's verified answer must then say which items it disputes and on what ground (CPLR 3016(f)).
- **More definite statement.** A pleading "so vague or ambiguous that a party cannot reasonably be required to frame a response" may be met by a motion under CPLR 3024(a).

## Verification and evidence

- **Signature and certification (22 NYCRR 130-1.1a).** Every pleading, motion and paper is signed by an attorney (or the unrepresented party) with the name printed or typed directly below. Signing certifies that the paper and its contentions are not frivolous and, for an initiating pleading, that its matter was not obtained illegally. Frivolous conduct includes asserting "material factual statements that are false" (130-1.1(c)); sanctions are capped at $10,000 for a single occurrence (130-1.2). Part 130 has no 21-day safe harbor.
- **Verification of pleadings is optional, then contagious.** A verification is a statement, subscribed and affirmed under the penalties of perjury in accordance with CPLR 2106, that the pleading is true to the knowledge of the deponent, except as to matters alleged on information and belief, which the deponent believes to be true (CPLR 3020(a)). Once a pleading is verified, each later pleading is verified. It is made by the party; for a domestic corporation by an officer; and in the cases listed in CPLR 3020(d) by an agent or the attorney, who then states the grounds of belief and why the party did not verify (CPLR 3021). A defectively verified pleading is treated as unverified, and the adverse party may treat it as a nullity on prompt notice (CPLR 3022).
- **Affirmation under CPLR 2106** (as amended by L 2025, ch 564). The statement of any person, wherever made, subscribed and affirmed to be true under the penalties of perjury, may be used in place of and with the same force as an affidavit, a verification of a pleading, a bill of particulars and other sworn statements, except a deposition, an oath of office, or an oath required to be taken before a specified person other than a notary. The statutory form:

> I affirm this ___ day of ______, ____, under the penalties of perjury under the laws of New York, which may include a fine or imprisonment, that the foregoing is true, except as to matters alleged on information and belief and as to those matters I believe it to be true, and I understand that this document may be filed in an action or proceeding in a court of law.
>
> (Signature)

- **The older forms are out of date.** Until January 1, 2024 the rule was limited to certain professionals and to persons outside the United States; from that date any person could affirm, in a shorter form; the 2025 amendment added the information-and-belief words and the list of uses. Use the current form.
- **No notary is needed** for a CPLR 2106 affirmation; the same form serves a deponent in Hong Kong. Who may take an affidavit, if one is used instead, was not researched.
- **Foreign-language papers.** An affidavit or exhibit in a foreign language is accompanied by an English translation and an affidavit by the translator stating qualifications and that the translation is accurate (CPLR 2101(b)); the translator may affirm under CPLR 2106 instead.
- **Summary judgment evidence.** The supporting affidavit is by a person having knowledge of the facts and recites all the material facts (CPLR 3212(b)).

## Amendment

- **Without leave (CPLR 3025(a)).** Once, within 20 days after service of the pleading, or at any time before the period for responding to it expires, or within 20 days after service of a pleading responding to it.
- **By leave or stipulation (CPLR 3025(b)).** At any time; "Leave shall be freely given upon such terms as may be just including the granting of costs and continuances." The motion is accompanied by "the proposed amended or supplemental pleading clearly showing the changes or additions to be made to the pleading." In practice that is a blackline against the current pleading, filed with a clean copy. Supplemental pleadings, for later transactions or occurrences, take the same route.
- **To conform to the evidence (CPLR 3025(c)),** before or after judgment, on just terms.
- **Response.** An answer or reply to an amended pleading is required if one was required to the original, within 20 days (CPLR 3025(d)).
- **Verification.** If the original was verified, verify the amendment (CPLR 3020(a)).
- **Commercial Division.** No rule specific to amended pleadings was found; CPLR 3025(b) governs, and the Rule 24 pre-motion conference applies to a motion for leave.
- **Bill of particulars.** Amend once as of course before the note of issue (CPLR 3042(b)).

## Formal rules for papers

- **CPLR 2101.** Durable white paper, 11 by 8½ inches; legible, in black ink; the name printed beneath each signature; type no smaller than 10-point (a summons 12-point). Every paper begins with a caption naming the court, the venue, the title of the action, the nature of the paper and the index number; a summons, complaint or judgment names all parties. Every paper is indorsed with the name, address and telephone number of the attorney (or party in person). A defect in form that prejudices no substantial right is disregarded, and an objection to form is waived unless the paper is returned within 15 days with particular objections.
- **22 NYCRR 202.5(a).** The index number, the county of venue, a brief description of the paper and, where assigned, the judge's name appear to the right of the caption on the cover and first page. Double spacing except quotations and attorneys' names and addresses; one-inch margins; print no smaller than 12-point, footnotes no smaller than 10-point; writing on one side only. Proof of service is annexed. E-filed memoranda, affidavits and affirmations above 4,500 words carry bookmarks (202.5(a)(2)). Confidential personal information is redacted (202.5(e)).
- **Commercial Division Rule 6.** Double spacing, 12-point print, one-inch margins; text in briefs and affidavits, footnotes included, in a proportionally spaced 12-point serif typeface; bookmarks; a hyperlink to the NYSCEF docket entry for every previously filed document cited (Rule 6(c)).
- **Word limits, Supreme Court generally (22 NYCRR 202.8-b).** Attorney affidavits or affirmations and memoranda of law in chief: 7,000 words each. Reply papers: 4,200 words, responding only to the papers in chief. Lay and expert evidentiary affidavits are not limited. Excluded from the count: caption, table of contents, table of authorities, signature block. Typed or handwritten papers: 20 pages, reply 10. A certificate by counsel stating the word count and compliance goes on a page attached to the end (202.8-b(c)). Sur-replies only with permission in advance (202.8-c).
- **Word limits, Commercial Division (Rule 17).** Briefs and memoranda 7,000 words; reply memoranda 4,200; affidavits and affirmations 7,000, fact affidavits included. The same exclusions and the same end-page certification.
- **Figures.** No New York rule requires figures rather than words.

## Applications and motions

- **The papers.** A notice of motion (or an order to show cause), a memorandum of law, and affidavits or affirmations with exhibits, within the 202.8-b or Rule 17 limits.
- **Commercial Division.** The exact relief sought is specified in the notice of motion or order to show cause and in a concluding section of the memorandum (Rule 16(a)). No proposed order is submitted with motion papers on a dispositive motion (Rule 16(b)). A pre-motion conference, requested by a letter of no more than two pages, precedes any non-exempt motion (Rule 24).
- **Motion to dismiss (CPLR 3211).** One pre-answer motion, combining every ground (3211(e)). The grounds include a defense founded on documentary evidence (a)(1), lack of subject-matter jurisdiction (a)(2), limitation and the other bars in (a)(5), and failure to state a cause of action (a)(7). Grounds (a)(1) and (3) to (6) are waived unless raised by the motion or in the answer. Either side may submit evidence, and the court may treat the motion as one for summary judgment on notice (3211(c)). A defense may be attacked under 3211(b). Scandalous or prejudicial matter is attacked within 20 days under CPLR 3024(b)-(c).
- **Summary judgment (CPLR 3212).** After issue is joined; no later than 120 days after the note of issue unless the court sets a date (no earlier than 30 days after it). Supported by an affidavit of a person with knowledge, a copy of the pleadings and other available proof. Partial summary judgment is available, and on denial the court may specify facts deemed established (3212(e), (g)). In the Commercial Division the court may direct a statement of material facts in numbered paragraphs (Rule 19-a); the response restates each paragraph and answers it; each statement cites the evidence; paragraphs not specifically controverted are deemed admitted. The statewide equivalent, 22 NYCRR 202.8-g, has been repealed.

## Citation style

The Official Reports Style Manual. Citations sit in the text, in parentheses; brackets replace parentheses inside them. The bracketed items show the shape of a citation only; in a draft every authority comes from the user (see `authorities.md`).

- Court of Appeals: `([Case name], [volume] NY3d [page], [pin] [[year]])`. "v" without a full stop; no full stops or spaces in "NY3d", "AD3d", "Misc 3d"; no court name where the reporter identifies it.
- Appellate Division: `([Case name], [volume] AD3d [page], [pin] [[number] Dept [year]])`.
- Supreme Court: `([Case name], [volume] Misc 3d [page], [pin] [Sup Ct, [court] County [year]])`.
- Page ranges in full: "351-352", not "351-52".
- Short forms: `(id. at [pin])`.
- The CPLR is cited without "§": "(CPLR 3211 [a] [7])"; in running text "CPLR 3211 (a) (7)". Other statutes take "§": "(Insurance Law § 5102 [d])".
- Court rules: first citation "(Uniform Rules for Trial Cts [22 NYCRR] § 202.5)", later "(22 NYCRR 202.5)".
- The Bluebook form, if the user prefers it, is `[Case name], [volume] N.Y.3d [page], [pin] ([year])`. Use one style throughout.

## Forms that exist only here

- **Demand for a Bill of Particulars and the Bill of Particulars** (CPLR 3041-3043), the nearest US equivalent of the Hong Kong request and answer.
- **The interlineated answer** of the Commercial Division (Rule 6(d)).
- **The verified, itemised goods and services claim** and the item-by-item verified answer (CPLR 3016(f)).
- **The CPLR 2106 affirmation**, which also verifies pleadings and bills of particulars.
- **The demand for an answer to a cross-claim** (CPLR 3011) and the supplemental demand for damages in personal injury actions (CPLR 3017(c)).
- **The pleaded substance of foreign law** (CPLR 3016(e)).
- **The licence allegation** required of a plaintiff suing a consumer on a licensed-business claim in the counties named in CPLR 3015(e).
- **The Rule 19-a statement of material facts** and the Rule 24 pre-motion letter (Commercial Division).
- **The word-count certification** on a separate last page.

## Background: where the standards come from

Background only. Cite these in a draft only if the user supplies or verifies them.

- *Leon v Martinez*, 84 NY2d 83, 87-88 (1994): on a CPLR 3211 motion the pleading is liberally construed, the facts alleged are accepted as true with every favourable inference, and the question is whether they fit any cognizable legal theory; affidavits may remedy defects in the complaint.
- *Pludeman v Northern Leasing Sys., Inc.*, 10 NY3d 486, 492 (2008): CPLR 3016(b) may be met by facts sufficient to permit a reasonable inference of the alleged fraud; it does not demand proof.
- *Alvarez v Prospect Hosp.*, 68 NY2d 320, 324 (1986): the movant's prima facie showing on summary judgment, and the shift of the burden to the opponent.
- *Zuckerman v City of New York*, 49 NY2d 557, 562 (1980): the opponent must produce evidentiary proof in admissible form; conclusions and unsubstantiated assertions are not enough.
