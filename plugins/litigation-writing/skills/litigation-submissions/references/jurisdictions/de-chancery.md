# Delaware Court of Chancery

This profile covers proceedings in the Delaware Court of Chancery. The governing rules are the Court of Chancery Rules ("Ct. Ch. R." or "Rule"), restyled in 2023 and 2024 to track the restyled federal rules, with Chancery additions: Rules 7 and 10 effective September 25, 2023; Rules 8, 9, 11, 13, 15 and 23.1 effective June 14, 2024; Rule 56 effective June 1, 2026. Former Rule 171 (briefs) is repealed and its content now sits in Rules 7 and 10. Citation follows the Bluebook as modified by the Delaware Uniform Citation (Rule 10(c)). Researched October 2026. Check the presiding judicial officer's practices before filing. The Superior Court has its own profile (`de-superior.md`).

## Document names

| Document type | Chancery name |
|---|---|
| Parties | Plaintiff and Defendant |
| Claim pleading | Complaint, verified by the party (Rule 3(c)) |
| Defence | Answer, which restates each allegation above its response (Rule 7(a)(3)) |
| Reply | As the federal list: an answer to a counterclaim; a reply to an answer only if the court orders (Rule 7(a)) |
| Counterclaim and others | Counterclaim; crossclaim (Rule 13(e)); third-party complaint; all verified (Rule 3(c)) |
| Amendment | Amended complaint (or other pleading), filed with a document showing how it differs (Rule 15(a)(3)); verified if the original had to be |
| Evidence instrument | Affidavit (Rule 56(c)(4)); the verification of a pleading also counts as record material on summary judgment (Rule 56(c)(1)) |
| Witness statement | No separate form |
| Skeleton | Opening brief, answering brief, reply brief (Rule 7(c)) |
| Hearing note | No equivalent; the nearest short paper is a letter to the court, capped at 1,000 words (Rule 7(d)(3)) |
| Application papers | Written motion with a form of order (Rule 7(b)); briefs for the motions listed in Rule 7(c)(1) |
| List of authorities | Table of authorities in the brief (Rule 7(c)(2)(B)) |
| Request for particulars | None. A motion for a more definite statement (Rule 12(e)) |
| Derivative actions | The complaint pleads demand or demand futility with particularity (Rule 23.1) |

## Pleading standard

- **Rule 8(a)** tracks the restyled federal rule: a short and plain statement of the claim showing entitlement to relief, and a demand for the relief sought. Allegations are stated in numbered paragraphs (Rule 7(a)(3)); exhibits are part of the pleading for all purposes (Rule 7(a)(5)).
- **The motion-to-dismiss standard** in Delaware's state courts is reasonable conceivability, lower than federal plausibility (see "Background"; not verified from a primary source).
- **Fraud and mistake (Rule 9(b)).** "In alleging fraud or mistake, a party must state with particularity the circumstances constituting fraud or mistake. Malice, intent, knowledge, and other conditions of a person's mind may be alleged generally." Unlike the Superior Court, negligence is not added.
- **Derivative complaints (Rule 23.1(a)).** The complaint must "(1) state with particularity: (A) any effort by the derivative plaintiff to obtain the desired action from the entity; and (B) the reasons for not obtaining the action or not making the effort; and (2) allege facts supporting a reasonable inference that the derivative plaintiff has standing to sue derivatively under the law governing the entity." Within 10 days the derivative plaintiff files an affidavit about compensation (Rule 23.1(b)).
- **Alternatives** and inconsistent claims are permitted as under the federal rule (Rule 8(d)(2)-(3)). Special damage is specifically stated and conditions precedent pleaded as under the federal rule (Rule 9(c), (g)).

## Answering a pleading

- **Restate, then respond (Rule 7(a)(3)).** "An answer to a complaint, counterclaim, or crossclaim must repeat the allegations of the pleading to which it is responding and then set forth the response below each such allegation."
- **No general denial (Rule 8(b)(3)).** "A party must either specifically admit or deny each allegation. A general denial is not permitted, except by a nominal party or any other party joined only to ensure that full and complete relief can be granted." There is no Hong Kong "save and except" traverse. Lack of knowledge or information is stated as under the federal rule and operates as a denial.
- **No reasons inside the denial**, but each affirmative defense carries its basis: "The pleading must provide a short and plain statement of the basis for the affirmative defense." (Rule 8(c)(3)).
- **Affirmative defenses (Rule 8(c)(1)).** The federal list with "discharge in bankruptcy" and "unclean hands" added.
- **Answers are not verified.** Rule 3(c) lists claim-type pleadings only.
- **Time.** Answer within 20 days (Rule 12(a)(1)(A)).
- **Counterclaims.** Compulsory and permissive as in the federal rule (Rule 13(a)-(c)); crossclaims at Rule 13(e).

Answer format (each allegation reproduced, then the response):

```
[number]. (The allegation, reproduced exactly as pleaded.)

[Defendant] admits the allegations of paragraph [number]. / [Defendant] denies the allegations of paragraph [number]. / [Defendant] lacks knowledge or information sufficient to form a belief about the truth of the allegations of paragraph [number] and therefore denies them.
```

## Particulars

- **Rule 9(b)** for fraud and mistake, and **Rule 23.1(a)** for derivative claims, are the particularity rules.
- **More definite statement (Rule 12(e)).** A motion before the responsive pleading; an order is complied with within 10 days.
- Chancery has no counterpart of the Hong Kong request for particulars; anything more is obtained in discovery.

## Verification and evidence

- **Verification (Rule 3(c)).** "(1) The following papers must be verified: (A) complaints; (B) counterclaims; (C) crossclaims; (D) third-party complaints; and (E) amendments or supplements to those pleadings. (2) Each party filing the paper must verify under oath or by affirmation that the matter contained in the paper is true and correct to the best of the party's knowledge, information, and belief. An authorized person must verify a paper filed by an entity or association." This is the nearest US counterpart of the Hong Kong statement of truth, but it is sworn or affirmed by the party, and it applies to claim pleadings only. No prescribed form of words was found; draft the verification to state the content of Rule 3(c)(2) and leave the oath or affirmation block for the officer who takes it.
- **The signature certifies (Rule 11).** Rule 11 mirrors the restyled federal rule, including the 21-day safe harbor for sanctions motions. Delaware counsel signs or receives service of all papers (Rule 170(d)).
- **Affidavits on summary judgment (Rule 56(c)(4)).** Made on personal knowledge, setting out admissible facts, and showing competence; certified exhibits may be attached. A party "may not rest upon unverified allegations or denials in its pleadings" and must cite particular parts of the record (Rule 56(c)(1)).
- **Not researched:** who may take the oath or affirmation; whether an unsworn declaration may replace an affidavit or verification; translations.

## Amendment

- **As of course (Rule 15(a)(1)).** "At any time before a responsive pleading is served", with no 21-day cap.
- **Otherwise (Rule 15(a)(2)),** with leave, which the court freely gives when justice so requires.
- **The comparison is mandatory (Rule 15(a)(3)).** "A party must file an amended pleading with the Court, even if the Court has granted a motion for leave to file the amended pleading. A party filing an amended pleading must also file a document indicating plainly how the amendment differs from the pleading that it amends." File a clean amended pleading and a comparison (blackline); no Hong Kong colour conventions.
- **Amend or lose (Rule 15(a)(5)).** A plaintiff facing a motion under Rule 12(b)(6) or 23.1 must amend, or seek leave to amend, before its response to the motion is due; otherwise a dismissal under either rule is with prejudice as to the named plaintiff, unless the court for good cause orders otherwise.
- **Verification.** An amendment to a complaint, counterclaim, crossclaim or third-party complaint is verified (Rule 3(c)(1)(E)).
- **Relation back and supplemental pleadings.** Rule 15(c) (with 120 days in place of the federal Rule 4(m) period) and Rule 15(d) (response within 10 days).

## Formal rules for papers

- **Caption (Rule 10(a)).** The Court's name, the names of the parties, the civil action number, and a title.
- **Font (Rule 10(b)(1)).** One of: Times New Roman 14-point with 13-point footnotes; Century 12-point with 11-point footnotes; Century Schoolbook 12-point with 11-point footnotes. Line spacing at least twice the point size (10(b)(2)); one-inch margins (10(b)(3)).
- **Certification (Rule 10(d)).** Signing certifies compliance with Rule 10. The signature block of any motion, brief or letter states (1) the word limit that applies and (2) the number of words in the filing subject to it.
- **Word limits.** A motion supported by a brief: 500 words. A motion not supported by a brief: 3,000 words; opposition 3,000; reply 2,000 (Rule 7(b)(3)). An opening or answering brief: 14,000 words; a reply brief: 8,000 (Rule 7(c)(3)). A letter to the court: 1,000 words, signed by a Delaware lawyer (Rule 7(d)(3)).
- **Briefs have no numbered paragraphs.** "A brief may not contain numbered paragraphs." (Rule 7(c)(2)(C)). Motions, by contrast, are in numbered paragraphs (Rule 7(b)(2)).

## Applications and motions

- **The written motion (Rule 7(b)(2)).** In this order: title, introduction, background, argument, and "a conclusion stating the relief sought", in numbered paragraphs, with a form of order.
- **When briefs are used (Rule 7(c)(1)).** Only for motions under Rules 12, 23, 23.1, 41(b), 56 and 65, for settlement and fee applications, and for pre-trial and post-trial briefing.
- **The brief (Rule 7(c)(2)(B)).** A table of contents; a table of authorities; an introduction; a statement of facts with references to the record (in an opening or answering brief); the argument; and a conclusion stating the relief sought.
- **Rule 12 motions.** Rule 12 mirrors the restyled federal rule with Delaware timings: matters outside the pleadings convert a motion into one for summary judgment (Rule 12(d)); a motion to strike "any insufficient defense or any material that is redundant, scandalous, immaterial, or not pertinent" is made within 20 days where no response is allowed (Rule 12(f)). Remember the amend-or-lose rule above.
- **Summary judgment (Rule 56(a)).** "The Court must grant summary judgment if the movant shows that there is no genuine dispute as to any material fact and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law." Cross-motions are deemed a stipulation for decision on the record (Rule 56(i)).
- **Security for costs.** "A non-Delaware resident may be required to post security for costs." (Rule 3(h)).
- **Meet and confer** was not researched.

## Citation style

Rule 10(c): "Citations and quotations must conform to the Uniform System of Citation (the Blue Book) published and distributed from time to time by the Harvard Law Review Association as modified by Delaware Uniform Citation (the Yellow Book), published and distributed from time to time by the Delaware State Bar Association." The bracketed items show the shape of a citation only; in a draft every authority comes from the user (see `authorities.md`).

- Reported cases: the Atlantic Reporter. `[Case name], [volume] A.3d [page], [pin] (Del. Ch. [year])`; the Supreme Court is "(Del. [year])".
- Unreported cases, online database first, without the docket number: `[Case name], [year] WL [number], at *[pin] (Del. Ch. [date])`, optionally followed by the judicial officer in parentheses.
- Memorandum or letter opinions not in a database: `[Case name], C.A. No. [case number], at [pin] (Del. Ch. [date])`.
- Transcript rulings are cited like memorandum opinions with "(TRANSCRIPT)", pinpointed page:line; table decisions with "(TABLE)".
- Short forms: `[Case name], [volume] A.3d at [pin].`; `Id. at [pin].`; `[Case name], [year] WL [number], at *[pin].`
- Statutes: "[number] Del. C. § [number]", for example "10 Del. C. § 3901". Rules: "Ct. Ch. R. [number]", with no date for the current rule. Cite the brief rules as Rules 7 and 10, not Rule 171.

## Forms that exist only here

- **The verified complaint** and the verification of counterclaims, crossclaims, third-party complaints and their amendments (Rule 3(c)).
- **The answer that reproduces each allegation** above its response (Rule 7(a)(3)), with no general denial (Rule 8(b)(3)).
- **The statement of the basis of each affirmative defense** (Rule 8(c)(3)), and "unclean hands" in the list.
- **The amend-or-lose rule** on a Rule 12(b)(6) or 23.1 motion (Rule 15(a)(5)).
- **Demand particularity and the derivative plaintiff's affidavit** (Rule 23.1).
- **The word limit and word count in the signature block** (Rule 10(d)).
- **The motion with a form of order**, the 500-word motion that accompanies a brief, and the 1,000-word letter to the court.
- **Security for costs from a non-resident plaintiff** (Rule 3(h)).

## Background: where the standards come from

Background only. Cite these in a draft only if the user supplies or verifies them.

- *Central Mortgage Co. v. Morgan Stanley Mortgage Capital Holdings LLC*, 27 A.3d 531 (Del. 2011): the Delaware Supreme Court's statement that the pleading standard on a motion to dismiss in Delaware's courts is reasonable conceivability, not federal plausibility. The citation was confirmed through a search index; the opinion text and the formulation were not read from a primary source.
