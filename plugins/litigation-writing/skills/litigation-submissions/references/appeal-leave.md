# Application for leave to appeal, including out of time

## What it is for

The submissions on an application for leave to appeal, for an extension of time to appeal, or both; and the opposing side's submissions resisting leave or seeking to set a grant of leave aside. The court is not deciding the appeal. It is deciding whether the appeal deserves to be heard, and whether a late applicant should be let in. So the paper must show, quickly, that each proposed ground clears the threshold, that the delay is explained or outweighed, and, where it helps, that the question matters beyond the parties.

**Do not choose the threshold.** The leave test, the factors for an extension of time and any test for fresh evidence come from the provision and authorities the user supplies (`[statutory provision]`, `[authority needed: …]`). Common factors for an extension are the length of the delay, the reasons for it, the prospects of the appeal and prejudice to the other side; confirm them against the user's authority before using them as headings.

## Structure: Hong Kong

**Caption.** The appeal or application number, the title with an ordinal where papers come in sequence (`[number] SKELETON SUBMISSIONS OF THE [Applicant]`), a purpose line (`(In support of the intended appeal from [case number 2])`), and the legend, which declares the decision label: `[Decision/§n] = paragraph n of the judgment of [judge] dated [date]`.

**Sections.**

1. **A. OVERVIEW.** One sentence: the decision, its date, the judge, and the holding challenged, with its tag. Then `In gist, the [Applicant]'s stance is that:` with one line per proposed ground. If there is a procedural anomaly (no jurisdiction, a step taken without notice), raise it here, before the merits.
2. **B. BACKGROUND.** Short: what the decision decided and how, by `[Decision/§n]`. A longer history goes in a chronology.
3. **C. THE THRESHOLD.** The leave test, from the user's provision and authority, stated once. Name it ("the Threshold Test") and use that name at the end of each ground.
4. **D. GROUND 1: [the proposition as a sentence]** (one section per ground, strongest first). Sub-grounds are taken inside their ground ("For Ground 1.1, …"). Each ground: Legal Principles, then Analysis ("It is submitted that the finding at [Decision/§n] is erroneous because it overlooks …"), then the close: "Ground 1 meets the Threshold Test."
5. **E. THE DELAY** (for an application out of time). Put this first instead if the delay is the larger obstacle. Count it in days from the date time ran to the date of the application. Give the reasons with evidence references (the affirmation explaining the delay is drafted with litigation-evidence). Then the cascade: the delay is explained; if it is not fully explained, the merits are strong enough to outweigh it; in any event there is no prejudice the other side can point to.
6. **F. IMPORTANCE** (where it applies): the question the appeal raises, framed in one sentence, and why it matters beyond this case.
7. **G. FRESH EVIDENCE** (where it applies): the cascade again. The fresh-evidence test does not apply (for example, because of the nature of the proceedings); alternatively it is met; in any event the court should admit the evidence for the reason the user's authority gives.
8. **H. CONCLUSION.** One characterising sentence. Then the orders: the extension of time to a date; leave on the grounds named; directions for the appeal (lodging the notice, bundles, skeletons). Costs where the user wants them (often costs in the appeal).

**Resisting leave or setting leave aside.** Mirror the structure: state the threshold the user's authority sets for refusing or setting aside leave, name it, and close every section with it ("The appeal is not bound to fail; the Threshold Test for setting aside is not met.").

## Differences in the US profiles

The profiles cover trial courts, not appellate courts, so flag every formal requirement of the appellate court for the user to check. The routes differ sharply: a federal interlocutory appeal usually needs a certification under 28 U.S.C. § 1292(b) and a petition for permission under FRAP 5; an extension of time to file a notice of appeal is governed by FRAP 4(a)(5); New York appeals by permission run under CPLR 5602 and 5701(c); Washington uses discretionary review under RAP 2.3; Delaware interlocutory appeals run under Supreme Court Rule 42; California interlocutory review is usually by petition for an extraordinary writ. The user confirms which route applies. The same moves carry over: the question presented first, grounds strongest first, the standard stated once and repeated as each ground's close.

## Skeleton

```
[case number 1]

[number] SKELETON SUBMISSIONS OF THE [Applicant]
(In support of the intended appeal from [case number 2])

Legend: [A12/§3] = Bundle A, page 12, paragraph 3; [Decision/§n] = paragraph n of the judgment of [judge] dated [date 1]

A. OVERVIEW

1. The [Applicant] applies for an extension of time and for leave to appeal from the decision of [judge] dated [date 1] ("the Decision"), which held that … [Decision/§24].

2. In gist, the [Applicant]'s stance is that:
   (a) the Judge erred in law in holding that … (Ground 1);
   (b) the Judge's finding at [Decision/§31] was made without evidence (Ground 2); and
   (c) the delay of [number] days is explained and has caused no prejudice.

C. THE THRESHOLD

3. Leave is granted where [statutory provision] is satisfied [authority needed: the leave threshold] (the "Threshold Test").

D. GROUND 1: THE JUDGE MISDIRECTED HIMSELF ON …

4. It is submitted that the finding at [Decision/§24] is erroneous because it overlooks … [Bundle ref].

5. Ground 1 meets the Threshold Test.

E. THE DELAY

6. Time to appeal expired on [date 2]. This application was issued on [date 3]: a delay of [number] days.

7. The reasons are set out in the [Applicant]'s affirmation dated [date 4] [Bundle ref]. Even if the delay is not fully explained, the merits of Ground 1 outweigh it. In any event, the [Respondent] has identified no prejudice [Bundle ref].

H. CONCLUSION

8. The Decision turns on a misreading of …. The [Applicant] respectfully invites the Court to order that:
   (1) The time for filing and serving the notice of appeal be extended to [date 5].
   (2) Leave to appeal be granted on Grounds 1 and 2.
   (3) Costs of this application be costs in the appeal.

[counsel-name]
Counsel for the [Applicant]
[date 6]
```

## Checklist

The checker tests: numbered paragraphs; grounds identified; a CONCLUSION heading; orders sought in the last third; prospects, delay and importance addressed (soft); no bullets.

Check by eye:

- Every ground is in the overview, with the same number as its section.
- The delay is counted from the right date, and each reason has evidence behind it.
- The threshold is stated from the user's authority, not from memory.
- The orders include the extended date and the grounds on which leave is sought.
