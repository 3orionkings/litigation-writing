# Opening submissions for trial

## What it is for

The map the trial judge reads before the first witness: what the case is about, what each side says, what the court must decide and in what order, and how the evidence will come. It persuades by framing the issues so that the client's answers look like the natural ones. This is the one submission that states the other side's case neutrally, side by side with the client's, because the judge needs both cases before hearing either side's witnesses. The argument comes after that statement, issue by issue.

## Structure: Hong Kong

**Caption.** A trial uses a fuller heading than an interlocutory skeleton: the trial line (`For trial before [judge] on [date] at [time]`), the action number, a short title (`[Plaintiff] v [Defendant]`), the document title, the legend line, and a role-qualified party line ("the lender [Plaintiff] ("P") and the borrower [Defendant] ("D")") where roles help the reader.

**Sections.**

1. **A. INTRODUCTION.** The trial in one sentence: the claim, the originating process, the provision relied on, how long the trial is fixed for and by which order. Then `In gist, …` with the client's answer to the case. Then the witnesses each side will call, and any expert.
2. **B. BACKGROUND.** The agreed facts first ("It is common ground that …"), each with its reference. Then the key documents, quoted where their words decide the case. A full chronology goes in an appendix (`chronology.md`).
3. **C. THE PARTIES' CASES.** C1 the client's case and C2 the other side's case, each stated fairly and briefly from its pleading, with pleading references. No characterisation here. This is the exception to the rule against neutral summaries, and it should read as neutral.
4. **D. ISSUES FOR DETERMINATION.** The issues, numbered, drawn from the pleadings ("From the pleadings, the issues for determination are:"). Then the decision tree: which issue comes first and what follows from each answer ("If the answer to Issue 1 is no, Issues 2 and 3 do not arise."). Then what is not in issue.
5. **E. ISSUE 1** (one section per issue), headed with the answer as a sentence: `E. ISSUE 1: THE GUARANTEE IS ENFORCEABLE`. Inside: Applicable Legal Principles (a named, numbered test from the user's authorities), then Application (one sub-heading per limb, each closed in one line), then `Issue 1: Conclusion` in one or two sentences.
6. **F. IN ANY EVENT** (optional): the point that wins even if an earlier issue is lost.
7. **G. RELIEF.** The relief claimed, with the pleading reference; consequential orders (repayment of money in court, discharge of a registration).
8. **H. CONCLUSION.** The findings sought, numbered. The relief. Costs, with any reasons for a special basis and the number of counsel.

Some trial openings, especially in personal injury, deliberately defer the argument to closing: they state the issues and the evidence and stop. Ask the user which is wanted. A quantum section sets out each head of loss with its arithmetic in the text and a summary table.

## Differences in the US profiles

The written equivalent is a trial brief or pretrial memorandum to the judge, usually required by the pretrial order or the judge's individual practices (FRCP 16(e) governs the final pretrial order in federal court). The opening statement itself is oral, and before a jury it must preview the evidence, not argue it. In a bench trial the judge may also order proposed findings of fact and conclusions of law. Use this structure for the written brief: statement of the case, the parties' contentions (often already set out in the joint pretrial order: cross-refer rather than restate), the issues, then argument issue by issue. Check the profile and the judge's standing order for length and timing.

## Skeleton

```
For trial before [judge] on [date 1] at [time]
[case number]
[Plaintiff] v [Defendant]

OPENING SUBMISSIONS OF THE [Defendant]

Legend: [A12/§3] = Bundle A, page 12, paragraph 3
Abbreviations: the [Plaintiff] ("P"); the [Defendant] ("D").

A. INTRODUCTION

1. This is the trial of the [Plaintiff]'s claim for … under [statutory provision], fixed for [number] days by order of [judge] dated [date 2] [Bundle ref].

2. In gist, the [Defendant]'s case is that ….

3. The [Plaintiff] will call [number] witnesses; the [Defendant] will call [witness]. There is no expert evidence.

B. BACKGROUND

4. It is common ground that:
   (a) … [Bundle ref];
   (b) … [Bundle ref].

C. THE PARTIES' CASES

5. C1. The [Plaintiff]'s case is that … [SOC§5-12].

6. C2. The [Defendant]'s case is that … [Defence§4-9].

D. ISSUES FOR DETERMINATION

7. From the pleadings, the issues for determination are:
   (1) Issue 1: whether …;
   (2) Issue 2: if so, whether …; and
   (3) Issue 3: what relief follows.

8. If the answer to Issue 1 is no, Issues 2 and 3 do not arise.

E. ISSUE 1: …

E1. Applicable Legal Principles

9. The test has three limbs [authority needed: the test for Issue 1]:
   (a) first, …;
   (b) second, …; and
   (c) third, ….

E2. Application

10. First limb. … [Bundle ref]. The first limb is not met.

11. Issue 1: Conclusion. The answer to Issue 1 is no.

H. CONCLUSION

12. The Court is respectfully invited to dismiss the claim, and to find that:
   (a) …;
   (b) ….

13. Costs should follow the event: the [Plaintiff] do pay the [Defendant]'s costs of the action, with a certificate for counsel, to be taxed if not agreed.

[counsel-name]
Counsel for the [Defendant]
[date 3]
```

## Checklist

The checker tests: numbered paragraphs; a statement of the parties' cases; the issues for determination; the decision tree (soft); a closing request with the findings sought (soft); no bullets; a legend line (soft).

Check by eye:

- The other side's case is stated fairly and from its pleading, without adjectives.
- Each issue section's heading states its answer.
- The decision tree matches the order of the issue sections.
- The witness list matches the witness statements filed.
- The findings sought in the conclusion map onto the issues, one by one.
