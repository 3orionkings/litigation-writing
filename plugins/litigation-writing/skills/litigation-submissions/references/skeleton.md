# Skeleton submissions (US: memorandum of law, brief in support or opposition)

## What it is for

The written argument for a contested interlocutory application: strike-out, summary judgment, amendment, security for costs, a stay, an extension of time, leave to adduce evidence, an injunction, or an unopposed originating summons that still needs reasons. It is also the form for a supplemental skeleton filed after the court asks questions. The judge reads it before the hearing, often only page one with care. So page one carries the application, the relief and the answer, and every later section can be found from the overview.

## Structure: Hong Kong

**Caption.** No full court heading or BETWEEN block. In order: the hearing line (`For hearing before [judge] on [date] at [time]`), the action number, the title, the legend line, the party line. A supplemental paper adds `(to be read with …)` under the title.

**Sections.** Letter them and keep the letters stable, because the reply will cite them.

1. **A. OVERVIEW.** The hearing sentence: whose summons, its date, its defined term, the relief as (1), (2), (3), and its bundle tag. Then `In gist, the [Plaintiff]'s stance is that:` with one lettered line per ground or reason, (a) to (d) at most. Then one sentence on costs if costs will be sought on a special basis. Page one ends with the answer, not with facts.
2. **B. BACKGROUND.** Only the facts the grounds need, each with its reference. Lock the gateway facts first with "It is not in dispute that:" and the other side's own documents. A longer history goes in a chronology annex (see `chronology.md`).
3. **C. PROCEDURAL HISTORY** (only when the history is the argument: repeated applications, defaults, prior orders). Number the other side's attempts; give each its date, its result and its tag.
4. **D. LEGAL PRINCIPLES** (optional general block): the rule quoted, then the propositions that every ground uses.
5. **E. [Defendant]'S SUMMONS** (one lettered section per application). Inside it, one sub-section per ground, headed with the ground's conclusion as a sentence: `Ground 1: the amendment is not explained and should be refused`. Each ground has two parts, E1.1 Legal Principles and E1.2 Analysis, and ends on a disposal sentence.
6. **F. [Plaintiff]'S SUMMONS**, in the same pattern, if there are cross-applications.
7. **G. ADVERSE AUTHORITY** (or a sub-section inside the ground it affects): what it decided, why it does not bind or does not apply.
8. **H. CONCLUSION.** One sentence that characterises the other side's application. Then the orders sought, drafted in full as numbered order paragraphs, with costs: basis, assessment, certificate for counsel, and the numbered conduct reasons if indemnity costs are sought.

Then the signature block and the distribution block, and the list of authorities (`authorities-list.md`) with the same caption.

**Paragraphs.** Numbered 1 to n continuously through all sections. Sub-points (a), (b), then (i), (ii). Two levels at most.

**Stating a test.** Quote the rule or the governing passage in an indented block, keeping the judgment's paragraph numbers, with "(emphasis added)" only where emphasis was added. Distil it into a named, numbered test ("Step 1 to Step 4", "the three limbs"). Apply it in the same order under the same names, and close each limb in one line ("Step 2 is not met."). Where the steps are sequential, open each later one on the footing that the earlier answer went against the client: `If the answer to Step 1 is yes (which is denied), …`.

## Differences in the US profiles

A US motion travels as a package: a notice of motion (or the motion itself), a memorandum of law or brief, the evidence by declaration or affirmation (drafted with litigation-evidence), and a proposed order. This variant drafts the memorandum. The separate notice of motion and the proposed order are procedural papers for litigation-writing; keep the relief in the memorandum word for word the same as theirs. Where the court combines the notice and the argument in one paper (N.D. Cal.; King County, where the motion is the brief), this variant drafts the combined paper. Read `references/jurisdictions/<code>.md` for the current limits and format; the points below are the shape.

- **Federal.** Notice of motion, memorandum, supporting declarations; the district's local rules set format, length and whether a word certificate is needed (S.D.N.Y. and E.D.N.Y. LCR 7.1; N.D. Cal. Civil L.R. 7-2 and 7-4, which put the notice, the relief and the points and authorities in one document with a statement of the issues; W.D. Wash. LCR 7(e); D. Del. LR 7.1.3, which fixes the order: tables, nature and stage of the proceedings, summary of argument in numbered paragraphs, facts with record references, argument, a short conclusion stating the precise relief).
- **New York.** Notice of motion, attorney affirmation or affidavit with exhibits, memorandum of law with `POINT` headings; word limits and a certificate on a separate last page (22 NYCRR 202.8-b; Commercial Division Rule 17). In the Commercial Division the notice and the memorandum's conclusion state the exact relief (Rule 16(a)), and no proposed order goes with a dispositive motion (Rule 16(b)). A Rule 19-a statement of material facts may be directed on summary judgment.
- **California.** Notice of motion and motion, memorandum of points and authorities, declarations, proposed order lodged separately (CRC 3.1110, 3.1112, 3.1113). Page limits, not word counts; tables of contents and authorities above the length set by CRC 3.1113(f), and an opening summary of argument above the longer threshold. On summary judgment, a **separate statement** of undisputed material facts in the two-column format of CRC 3.1350, each fact followed by its evidence by exhibit, page and line (CCP 437c(b)(1)). Draft it as its own document, numbered, with no argument in it. A demurrer or motion to strike needs the meet-and-confer declaration (CCP 430.41, 435.5).
- **Washington (King County).** One document, no separate memorandum, under the headings of KC LCR 7(b)(5)(B): relief requested, statement of facts, statement of issues, evidence relied upon, argument; a word certificate in the signature block; a proposed order (editable copy with working copies); a notice of court date. Summary judgment limits are in KC LCR 56.
- **Delaware Superior.** Super. Ct. Civ. R. 107: typeface, tables, statement of the case, questions involved, argument, a word-count certificate.
- **Delaware Chancery.** A motion not supported by a brief is in numbered paragraphs with a form of order (Ct. Ch. R. 7(b)); a brief has tables, introduction, facts with record references, argument and a conclusion stating the relief, and **must not have numbered paragraphs** (Ct. Ch. R. 7(c)(2)(C)). The signature block states the word limit and the count (Ct. Ch. R. 10(d)).

In every US form: write "Plaintiff" or the defined short name, not "P"; cite the record in the court's form (`ECF No. [number] at [number]`); use the profile's citation style; no costs paragraph unless a rule, statute or contract shifts fees. US memoranda are often unnumbered. These skills number paragraphs by default, which most courts accept and which lets a reply cite `[D§n]`; leave the numbering out only where a rule forbids it or the user asks, and tell the user why the checker's numbering test then reports.

## Skeleton (Hong Kong)

```
For hearing before [judge] on [date 1] at [time]
[case number]

SKELETON SUBMISSIONS OF THE [Plaintiff]

Legend: [A12/§3] = Bundle A, page 12, paragraph 3; [D§n] = paragraph n of the [Defendant]'s Skeleton Submissions dated [date 2]
Abbreviations: the [Plaintiff] ("P"); the [Defendant] ("D").

A. OVERVIEW

1. This is the hearing of the [Defendant]'s Summons dated [date 3] ("the Summons") seeking that: (1) …; (2) …; and (3) the costs of the Summons be paid by the [Plaintiff] [Bundle ref].

2. In gist, the [Plaintiff]'s stance is that:
   (a) … (Ground 1);
   (b) … (Ground 2); and
   (c) taken at its highest (which is denied), the [Defendant]'s case on … does not meet the test (Ground 3).

3. The Summons should be dismissed with costs on an indemnity basis, for the reasons in paragraph [number] below.

B. BACKGROUND

4. It is not in dispute that:
   (a) … [Bundle ref];
   (b) … [Bundle ref].

5. The [Defendant]'s own letter of [date 4] accepted that … [Bundle ref]. The [Defendant] did not reply to … [Bundle ref].

E. THE [Defendant]'S SUMMONS

Ground 1: … should be refused because …

E1.1 Legal Principles

6. [rule] provides: (quoted in an indented block) [authority needed: the governing statement of the test].

7. The principles distil into four steps (the "Steps"):
   (a) Step 1: …;
   (b) Step 2: …;
   (c) Step 3: …; and
   (d) Step 4: ….

E1.2 Analysis

8. Step 1. … [Bundle ref]. Step 1 is not met.

9. If the answer to Step 1 is yes (which is denied), Step 2 … [Bundle ref]. Step 2 is not met either.

10. [D§12-14]: the [Defendant] says that …. That is no answer, because … [Bundle ref].

11. The Summons fails on this ground alone.

H. CONCLUSION

12. The Summons is a further attempt to … after … [Bundle ref].

13. The [Plaintiff] respectfully seeks the following orders:
   (1) The Summons be dismissed.
   (2) The costs of and occasioned by the Summons be paid by the [Defendant] to the [Plaintiff] on an indemnity basis, with a certificate for counsel, to be taxed if not agreed.

14. Indemnity costs are sought to mark the Court's disapproval of the [Defendant]'s conduct of these proceedings, namely:
   (a) … [Bundle ref];
   (b) … [Bundle ref].

[counsel-name]
Counsel for the [Plaintiff]
[date 5]

To: Clerk to [judge], [court]
    [solicitors-firm], Solicitors for the [Defendant]
```

## Skeleton (US memorandum, New York shape)

```
[court]
[Plaintiff], Plaintiff, against [Defendant], Defendant.        Index No. [case number]

MEMORANDUM OF LAW IN SUPPORT OF [Defendant]'S MOTION TO DISMISS THE COMPLAINT

PRELIMINARY STATEMENT

1. [Defendant] moves under [rule] for an order dismissing the complaint. The motion should be granted for [number] reasons: (a) … (Point I); and (b) … (Point II).

STATEMENT OF FACTS

2. … ([witness] Aff. ¶ [number]; NYSCEF Doc. No. [number].)

ARGUMENT

POINT I: THE COMPLAINT FAILS TO STATE A CLAIM FOR …

3. The standard on a motion under [rule] is … [authority needed: the pleading standard on this motion].

4. The first element is …. The complaint pleads no fact that supports it (Compl. ¶ [number]). The first element fails.

POINT II: …

CONCLUSION

5. For the foregoing reasons, [Defendant] respectfully requests that the Court grant the motion and dismiss the complaint, together with such other relief as the Court deems just and proper.

Dated: [date]
Respectfully submitted,
[counsel-name] / [solicitors-firm] / [address] / Attorneys for [Defendant]

CERTIFICATION OF WORD COUNT (on a separate last page)
```

## Checklist

The checker tests: numbered paragraphs (switched off for a Delaware Chancery brief by the profile; if it is not, tell the user the rule prevails); a CONCLUSION heading; orders sought in the last third; a legend line near the start (soft); the hearing sentence or preliminary statement near the start (soft); a costs order in a Hong Kong paper (soft); a word certificate and tables in the US profiles that need them (soft); no bullet points; no neutral "summary of the other side's submissions" heading (soft).

Check by eye:

- Page one carries the application, the relief, the stance and every ground.
- Every fact sentence ends with a reference; no fact without one.
- The overview's grounds and the analysis's grounds have the same numbers, in the same order.
- Every authority is in the user's list; every gap is `[authority needed: …]`.
- The orders are drafted in full and match the summons or notice of motion.
- In California summary judgment, the separate statement exists and its numbers match the memorandum's citations.
