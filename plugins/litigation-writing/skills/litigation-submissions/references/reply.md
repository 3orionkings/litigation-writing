# Reply skeleton or reply note (US: reply brief, reply memorandum)

## What it is for

The answer to the other side's skeleton or opposition, filed after it or handed up at the hearing. A reply has one job: to meet what the other side has said that the main paper did not already meet. It does not restate the case. Courts read replies quickly and limit them (New York replies may not raise arguments that do not respond to the papers in chief; California replies on summary judgment may not add evidence), so every paragraph must earn its place by answering something.

## Structure: Hong Kong

**Caption.** Hearing line, action number, and a title with an ordinal and a purpose line: `[number] SKELETON SUBMISSIONS OF THE [Plaintiff] (IN REPLY TO THE [Defendant]'S SKELETON SUBMISSIONS DATED [date])`. A reply note handed up at the same hearing can use a short title.

**Legend.** Declare every paper you will cite by label, once: `[D§n] = paragraph n of the [Defendant]'s Skeleton Submissions dated [date]`; `[P1§n] = paragraph n of the [Plaintiff]'s Skeleton Submissions dated [date]`. Then say that the main skeleton's abbreviations are adopted. Paragraph references without a label then mean the other side's paper, and the legend says so.

**Sections.**

1. **Opening.** One sentence identifying the paper answered. Then the common ground the other side's paper has conceded, harvested as numbered points with their `[D§n]` labels. Conceded ground narrows the hearing; put it first.
2. **The points in reply.** One heading per point, carrying the other side's paragraph range: `A. The "no prejudice" point [D§14-19]`. Order them so that the other side's weakest point comes first, not in the other side's order. Inside each, paragraph lead-ins by label: `[D§15]:`, `At [D§16], the [Defendant] contends that …`.
3. **What is not answered.** Where the other side was silent on a point that matters, say so in one sentence with the reference to where it was raised: "[D§§1-30] do not address [P1§12]."
4. **Cross-references, not re-argument.** Where the main skeleton already meets a point, cross-refer: `For [D§20-25], the [Plaintiff] relies on [P1§30-34] and does not repeat that argument here.` Do not restate law already set out; cite the earlier paragraph.
5. **New authority.** Only if the other side's paper makes it necessary. Continue the numbering of the main list of authorities in a supplemental list.
6. **Close.** One sentence that characterises the other side's position, then a short request in the terms of the main skeleton's orders (or a cross-reference to them). A reply note at the hearing may end without one.

## Differences in the US profiles

- A reply brief has its own, lower limit and its own certificate (S.D.N.Y. and E.D.N.Y. LCR 7.1(c); 22 NYCRR 202.8-b(a)(ii); N.D. Cal. Civil L.R. 7-4(b); CRC 3.1113(d); KC LCR 7(b)(5)(B)(vi); Super. Ct. Civ. R. 107(h)(1); Ct. Ch. R. 7(c)(3)). The reply may omit the facts and issues headings where the local form allows (King County).
- New arguments are out. New York confines a reply to the arguments in chief; the District of Delaware forbids holding back for the reply what belonged in the opening brief (LR 7.1.3(b)(2)). A sur-reply needs permission in advance (22 NYCRR 202.8-c).
- In California summary judgment, the reply may not add evidence (CCP 437c(b)(4)); answer the opposing separate statement by its numbers.
- Refer to the opposition by its page and line or ECF page (`Opp. at [number]`) when the court's practice is not to number paragraphs; keep the `[D§n]` labels where the paper is numbered.

```
Legend: [D§n] = paragraph n of the [Defendant]'s Skeleton Submissions dated [date 2]; [P1§n] = paragraph n of the [Plaintiff]'s Skeleton Submissions dated [date 3]. Abbreviations are as in the [Plaintiff]'s Skeleton Submissions.

   (a) the [Defendant] accepts that … [D§8]; and
   (b) the [Defendant] does not challenge … [D§11].

A. THE "NO PREJUDICE" POINT [D§14-19]

3. [D§15]: the [Defendant] says that …. That is misconceived. The [Defendant]'s own evidence is that … [Bundle ref].

B. THE AUTHORITIES [D§20-25]

5. For [D§20-25], the [Plaintiff] relies on [P1§30-34] and does not repeat that argument here.

6. The one new authority the [Defendant] cites [D#4] concerned …. It does not assist the [Defendant], because ….

C. CONCLUSION

7. The [Defendant]'s skeleton concedes the facts that decide the Summons. The [Plaintiff] maintains the orders sought at [P1§40].

[counsel-name]
Counsel for the [Plaintiff]
[date 4]
```

## Checklist

The checker tests: numbered paragraphs; the paper answered is identified near the start; `[D§n]` labels are used (soft); a legend line (soft); a word certificate in the US profiles that need one (soft); no bullets.

Check by eye:

- Every heading carries the other side's paragraph range.
- Nothing is argued that the main skeleton already argued; it is cross-referred.
- No new ground and no new evidence, unless the other side opened the door and the reply says so.
- Each label is declared in the legend and points to the right paper and paragraph.
- Supplemental authorities continue the main list's numbering.
