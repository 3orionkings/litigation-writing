# Hearing note

## What it is for

A short, factual paper for a hearing where the court manages the case rather than decides a contested point of law: a call-over, a case management hearing, a first appointment, a checklist review, or an application for more time. The judge needs to know in two minutes where the case stands, what is wrong, and what order to make. So the note is mostly facts and an agenda, and its centre of gravity is the directions sought, drafted as the order.

## Structure: Hong Kong

**Caption.** Hearing line, action number, title (`[Plaintiff]'S HEARING NOTE`), a bracketed hearing type where useful (`(Case Management Hearing)`), and the party line.

**Register.** One fact per sentence, each with its reference. No adjectives about the other side; the dates and counts do the work. No authorities in a call-over or time-extension note unless a contested point needs one; if it does, it probably needs a skeleton.

**Sections.**

1. **A. OVERVIEW.** What this hearing is (`This is the [number] call-over hearing of …`, or the hearing sentence for a summons), what the client asks for, and, when opposing an application, the fallback in one sentence.
2. **B. BACKGROUND.** The case in three to six sentences, with pleading references (`[SOC§9-13]`). In a family case: the marriage and separation dates, each child as `[the Child 1]` with age and residence, the current arrangements. In a sequence of notes for one case, carry this block forward and update it (ages, addresses, steps since the last hearing).
3. **C. PROCEDURAL HISTORY** (or **THE DELAY** when opposing more time). The steps since the last hearing, dated. When the other side seeks a further extension, count: how many extensions, how many days in total, what each order required, and what was done. Computed spans are stated ("[number] months since the writ was served").
4. **D. ISSUES FOR THIS HEARING.** Each issue numbered, with the position of each party in one line and the reference to where each raised it.
5. **E. THE WAY FORWARD.** The directions sought, drafted as numbered order paragraphs with dates, not as a wish list. Where the other side seeks time, two drafted orders:
   (a) the primary order (dismiss the application, with costs, summarily assessed and paid by a date); and
   (b) the fallback, only if the Court is minded to extend: a short extension on unless terms, the deadline worked out to a date and "4:00 pm", the consequence (judgment for a stated sum with interest at a stated rate from a stated date, or the pleading struck out), and costs.
6. **F. CONCLUSION.** One sentence asking for the directions as drafted, then the closing courtesy if the user wants it.

**Computing deadlines.** If the hearing date and the period are known, compute the date and show the arithmetic to the user above the draft; tell them to check it against the rules on computing time and court holidays. If either is unknown, write `[date]`.

## Differences in the US profiles

There is no exact US equivalent. The nearest forms are a joint status report or case management statement (for example an FRCP 26(f) report, or a California case management statement under CRC 3.725 on the mandatory Judicial Council form), a preliminary conference submission in New York (22 NYCRR 202.12), a letter to the court under the judge's individual practices, or an opposition to a motion or stipulation for more time (FRCP 6(b) in federal court). Where a mandatory form exists, use it and put this note's content into its boxes. Otherwise use the same sections in a letter or short submission, with the proposed order or stipulation attached, and check the judge's page limit for letters (Delaware Chancery caps letters by Ct. Ch. R. 7(d)(3)).

## Skeleton

```
For hearing before [judge] on [date 1] at [time]
[case number]

[Plaintiff]'S HEARING NOTE
(Opposing the [Defendant]'s Summons for a further extension of time)

Legend: [A12/§3] = Bundle A, page 12, paragraph 3
Abbreviations: the [Plaintiff] ("P"); the [Defendant] ("D").

A. OVERVIEW

1. This is the hearing of the [Defendant]'s Summons dated [date 2] ("the [number] Time Summons") seeking a further [period] to file and serve its Defence [Bundle ref].

2. The [Plaintiff] opposes the Summons. If the Court is minded to grant any extension, the [Plaintiff] asks that it be short and on the unless terms at paragraph 8 below.

B. BACKGROUND

3. This is a claim for [sum] due under a written agreement dated [date 3] [SOC§4-9].

C. THE DELAY

4. The writ was served on [date 4] [Bundle ref]. The Defence was due on [date 5].

5. The [Defendant] has already obtained [number] extensions, totalling [period]:
   (a) by consent on [date 6], to [date 7] [Bundle ref];
   (b) by order of [judge] dated [date 8], to [date 9] [Bundle ref].

6. The [Defendant] filed no evidence explaining the delay. It has none.

E. THE WAY FORWARD

7. Primary position. The [Plaintiff] invites the Court to order that:
   (1) The Summons be dismissed.
   (2) The costs of the Summons be paid by the [Defendant] to the [Plaintiff], summarily assessed in the sum of [sum] and paid within [period] days.

8. Alternatively, if the Court is minded to extend time, the [Plaintiff] invites the Court to order that:
   (1) Unless the [Defendant] files and serves its Defence by 4:00 pm on [date 10], judgment be entered for the [Plaintiff] against the [Defendant] in the sum of [sum] together with interest on [sum] at [rate] per annum from [date 11] to the date of judgment and thereafter at judgment rate until payment.
   (2) On any judgment so entered, the [Defendant] do pay the [Plaintiff]'s costs of the action, to be taxed if not agreed.
   (3) The [Defendant] do pay the costs of the Summons in any event, summarily assessed in the sum of [sum] and paid within [period] days.

F. CONCLUSION

9. The [Plaintiff] invites the Court to make the orders at paragraph 7, or alternatively paragraph 8. The [Plaintiff] is grateful for the Court's assistance.

[counsel-name]
Counsel for the [Plaintiff]
[date 12]
```

## Checklist

The checker tests: numbered paragraphs; a CONCLUSION or WAY FORWARD heading; orders or directions sought in the last third; no bullets; length (a hearing note is short).

Check by eye:

- Every fact has a date and a reference; no adjective does work a date could do.
- The directions are order paragraphs with dates, not descriptions.
- When opposing more time, the fallback unless order is fully drafted: deadline date and hour, consequence, sum, interest, costs.
- The background block is current (ages, addresses, steps since the last note).
- No authority unless the point is genuinely contested.
