# Variant `merits`: advice on the merits of a claim or defence

## What it is for

Counsel's view on whether a claim or defence is likely to succeed, issue by issue, on the documents provided. Use it once proceedings are on foot or about to be: on receipt of a defence, before an application, before trial, or when the client must decide whether to press on, settle or amend. It may also cover a side question the solicitors raise (whether expert evidence is needed, whether the pleadings need amendment, comments on a draft witness statement), each as its own numbered issue.

## Structure (Hong Kong)

1. **Heading.** The case number and court on the first line if proceedings exist; then "PRIVILEGED AND CONFIDENTIAL"; then the title in capitals ("ADVICE ON THE MERITS OF …" or "PRELIMINARY ADVICE ON …"). No caption or parties block: an advice is not a court document.
2. **A. Overview.** Who instructs and for whom; the instructions (date, defined as "the Instructions"); the questions asked; the documents relied on (Annex 1). Then "The Issues": a one-paragraph statement of the central concern, and the issues as numbered questions, with any preliminary issues first and a sentence saying why.
3. **Executive Summary.** One answer per issue, in the same order, on the verbal scale, hedged. Then the hand-off and the hedge.
4. **B. Background.** Only the facts that bear on the issues. The parties in "is and was at all material times" form. Events as dated paragraphs, each with its source document. The pleadings summarised with pinpoints such as `[§7/SOC]` or a declared label (`[D§12]`). A long chronology goes in an annexed table with a column for what is unknown.
5. **C. Analysis.** One sub-section per issue, headed with the issue as a question: the principle, the distilled test, "Applying the principles here, the question to ask is as follows:", the application, the client's own arguments tested, and the interim conclusion.
6. **D. The other side's case.** The opponent's pleading or letter taken paragraph by paragraph, each point answered. Fold this into the issues instead if it is short.
7. **E. Information required.** The fixed numbered list of what is unknown, and the formal request for it.
8. **F. Conclusion.** One overall evaluative sentence, the hedge, an optional hazards-of-litigation line and an optional settlement or mediation line, then the formal two-sentence close. No recap.
9. **Signature.** "Dated this [date]." then `[counsel-name]` and "Barrister-at-Law" (chambers optional).
10. **Annexes.** Annex 1: documents provided, each with its date. Then "List of Authorities" and "Other Authorities Considered".

## Differences for the US profiles

- **Form.** A privileged memorandum or opinion letter is usual: "TO / FROM / DATE / RE" lines in place of the title block, headed "PRIVILEGED AND CONFIDENTIAL / ATTORNEY-CLIENT COMMUNICATION / ATTORNEY WORK PRODUCT". The order of sections is unchanged. Sign as `[counsel-name]` for `[solicitors-firm]`.
- **Addressee.** The client's in-house counsel, lead counsel or the supervising attorney, by role. Still no "you should": write "I recommend that [Party] consider …".
- **Standard of review.** Where the merits question is how a motion will go (motion to dismiss, summary judgment, demurrer), state the standard the court applies from the profile (`references/jurisdictions/<code>.md`) and analyse the issue against it: plausibility in federal court, reasonable conceivability in Delaware, the state tests in New York, California and Washington. Do not state the standard from memory if the profile does not give it; mark `[authority needed: …]`.
- **Burden and standard of proof.** "Preponderance of the evidence" in place of "balance of probabilities", and "clear and convincing evidence" where the profile or a supplied authority says it applies.
- **Citation style.** The profile's style (Bluebook, New York Official Reports, California Style Manual, Washington Style Sheet), one style throughout.

## Skeleton

The ellipses mark counsel's own prose. Bracketed labels are fields from `references/fields.md`.

```
[case number] ([court])

PRIVILEGED AND CONFIDENTIAL

ADVICE ON THE MERITS OF THE CLAIM AGAINST [Defendant]

A. OVERVIEW

1. Instructing solicitors, [solicitors-firm], act for [Plaintiff] ("P") in the above action against [Defendant] ("D").

2. I am instructed by a letter dated [date 1] ("the Instructions") to advise on the merits of P's claim, on the documents provided to me and listed in Annex 1. In particular, I am asked to advise on:
   (a) …; and
   (b) … .

The Issues

3. The central concern is that … . The issues are:
   Issue 1: Whether …?
   Issue 2: If so, whether …?

Executive Summary

4. In a nutshell, on the information provided, I incline to take the view that:
   (a) Issue 1: it is more likely than not that … ;
   (b) Issue 2: it is at least arguable that … , but … .

5. Each issue is analysed in turn below. My views are subject to the information provided; the information still needed is listed in Section E.

B. BACKGROUND

6. As instructing solicitors are familiar with the background, this advice deals only with the facts that bear on the issues.

7. P is and was at all material times … . D is and was at all material times … .

8. On [date 2], P and D entered into [document title] ("the Agreement") [Bundle ref].

9. I am instructed that … .

C. ANALYSIS

C.1 Issue 1: Whether …?

10. The applicable principles are set out in [authority needed: the governing test for …] at [§ needed]:

    "…" (emphasis added)

11. The principles can be distilled as follows. First, … . Second, … . Finally, … .

12. Applying the principles here, the question to ask is as follows: whether … ?

13. In my view:
    (a) First, … [Bundle ref].
    (b) Second, … .

14. Is it an answer for P that …? This would not assist P, because … .

15. In interim conclusion, I incline to take the view that, on the information provided and subject to P coming up to proof, it is more likely than not that … .

C.2 Issue 2: If so, whether …?

16. …

D. D'S CASE

17. [D§n] = paragraph n of the Defence dated [date 3].

18. At [D§4], D pleads that … . First, … . Second, … .

E. INFORMATION REQUIRED

19. The views above depend on matters on which I have no information:
    (a) … (relevant to Issue 1);
    (b) … (relevant to Issue 2).

20. I would be grateful if instructing solicitors would provide that information, together with copies of … . Any of it may change the views expressed in this advice.

F. CONCLUSION

21. Taking the issues together, I incline to take the preliminary view that … .

22. My views are subject to the information provided, and may change as further information is obtained and the witnesses come up to proof. They make no allowance for the hazards of litigation. P may wish to consider mediation.

23. The foregoing is my preliminary advice on these matters. I would be happy to assist or advise further if so required.

Dated this [date 4].

[counsel-name]
Barrister-at-Law
```

## Checklist

What the checker tests:

- numbered paragraphs, first person present, and no "you should" or numeric odds;
- "I am asked" or "I am instructed" near the start;
- a hedge ("on the information provided", "subject to …") and a numbered issue;
- an executive summary or "In a nutshell" opening (soft);
- a request for further information;
- the formal close at the end, and no summary close;
- every placeholder listed in "FIELDS TO COMPLETE"; every citation in the matter file when run with `--matter`.

What the drafter checks by eye:

- the summary answers the issues in the same order, with the same scale word as each interim conclusion;
- each principle comes from a supplied authority, quoted faithfully with its paragraph numbers;
- each issue has an interim conclusion, and each conclusion that depends on an unknown points to the missing-information list;
- the client's weak points are stated, and the client's own arguments are tested;
- nothing in the background goes beyond the documents and the instructions.
