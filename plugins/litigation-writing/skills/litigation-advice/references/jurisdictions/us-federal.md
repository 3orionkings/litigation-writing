# United States federal courts

This profile covers civil actions in the United States district courts. The governing rules are the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure ("FRCP" or "Rule"), 28 U.S.C. § 1746 for unsworn declarations, and the local rules of the district. Four districts' local rules were read as examples: the Joint Local Rules of the Southern and Eastern Districts of New York (effective January 2, 2026), the Northern District of California Civil Local Rules (effective October 2, 2026), the Western District of Washington Local Civil Rules (revised effective January 13, 2026), and the District of Delaware Local Rules (effective September 1, 2026) with its Standing Order Regarding Briefing in All Cases (December 9, 2019). Citation follows the Bluebook. Researched October 2026. Every district and many judges add their own rules and standing orders: check them before filing, and never present a draft as compliant with a district this profile does not cover.

| Counterclaim and others | Counterclaim (Rule 13(a), (b)); crossclaim against a co-party (Rule 13(g)); third-party complaint (Rule 14) |

## Pleading standard

Rule 8(a): a pleading that states a claim for relief must contain "(1) a short and plain statement of the grounds for the court's jurisdiction, unless the court already has jurisdiction and the claim needs no new jurisdictional support; (2) a short and plain statement of the claim showing that the pleader is entitled to relief; and (3) a demand for the relief sought, which may include relief in the alternative or different types of relief."

- **Plausibility.** The complaint must plead factual content from which the court can reasonably infer liability. Labels, conclusions and a recital of the elements are disregarded on a motion to dismiss under Rule 12(b)(6) (see "Background"). A federal complaint is therefore usually more narrative than a Hong Kong statement of claim, while still pleading facts and not evidence.
- **Jurisdiction.** Plead the basis of subject-matter jurisdiction in its own paragraph (Rule 8(a)(1)). N.D. Cal. Civil L.R. 3-5(a) requires a paragraph entitled "Jurisdiction"; W.D. Wash. LCR 8(a) requires a diversity complaint to plead each party's citizenship, and for an LLC, LLP or partnership the citizenship of its members.
- **Form.** "Each allegation must be simple, concise, and direct. No technical form is required." (Rule 8(d)(1)). Numbered paragraphs, each limited as far as practicable to a single set of circumstances; separate counts for claims founded on separate transactions where that promotes clarity (Rule 10(b)).
- **Exhibits.** A written instrument attached as an exhibit is part of the pleading for all purposes (Rule 10(c)).
- **Alternatives.** Two or more statements of a claim may be made alternatively or hypothetically, and as many claims as the party has regardless of consistency (Rule 8(d)(2)-(3)).
- **Conditions precedent.** May be alleged generally (Rule 9(c)).
- **Belief.** A factual allegation that needs discovery to support it is identified as such ("on information and belief") to come within Rule 11(b)(3).
- **Relief.** A "Prayer for Relief" or "WHEREFORE" clause closes the complaint. A default judgment cannot exceed or differ in kind from what is demanded; other judgments may grant relief not demanded (Rule 54(c)). Special damage is specifically stated (Rule 9(g)).

## Answering a pleading

Rule 8(b) asks for three answers to each allegation, and no reasons.

- **Admit, deny, or lack knowledge.** A party must admit or deny the allegations (Rule 8(b)(1)(B)); a denial "must fairly respond to the substance of the allegation" (Rule 8(b)(2)); a party denying only part must admit the rest (Rule 8(b)(4)); a party that lacks knowledge or information sufficient to form a belief about the truth of an allegation must say so, and that operates as a denial (Rule 8(b)(5)).
- **Effect of silence.** An allegation other than one about the amount of damages is admitted if not denied (Rule 8(b)(6)).
- **No reasons and no own version.** Unlike Hong Kong O.18 r.13(5), the rule does not ask for reasons or the defendant's account inside the denial. The positive case goes in the affirmative defenses or a counterclaim.
- **No Hong Kong general traverse.** Rule 8(b)(3) permits a general denial only where the party in good faith denies everything, or a general denial of all except what is specifically admitted. The safe course is to answer every numbered paragraph in turn and not to rely on a "save as expressly admitted" catch-all.
- **Affirmative defenses.** Rule 8(c)(1): "a party must affirmatively state any avoidance or affirmative defense", including accord and satisfaction, arbitration and award, assumption of risk, contributory negligence, duress, estoppel, failure of consideration, fraud, illegality, injury by fellow servant, laches, license, payment, release, res judicata, statute of frauds, statute of limitations and waiver. The list is not closed. Set them out under a heading after the paragraph-by-paragraph responses, each as a separate defense (Rule 10(b)).
- **Conditions precedent.** A denial that a condition precedent occurred is made with particularity (Rule 9(c)).
- **Counterclaims.** A claim arising out of the same transaction or occurrence is compulsory and is lost if omitted (Rule 13(a)). Set-off is pleaded as an affirmative defense or a counterclaim. A third-party complaint served more than 14 days after the answer needs leave (Rule 14(a)(1)).
- **Pleadings close** with the answer, or the answer to a counterclaim; affirmative defenses are taken as denied without a reply.

Answer formulae:
- "[Defendant] admits the allegations in paragraph [number]."
- "[Defendant] denies the allegations in paragraph [number]."
- "[Defendant] lacks knowledge or information sufficient to form a belief about the truth of the allegations in paragraph [number] and therefore denies them."

## Particulars

- **Rule 9(b):** "In alleging fraud or mistake, a party must state with particularity the circumstances constituting fraud or mistake. Malice, intent, knowledge, and other conditions of a person's mind may be alleged generally." Courts read this as requiring the who, what, when, where and how of the misconduct in the complaint itself (see "Background").
- **Special damage:** "If an item of special damage is claimed, it must be specifically stated." (Rule 9(g)).
- **More definite statement (Rule 12(e)):** a motion, made before the responsive pleading, where a pleading "is so vague or ambiguous that the party cannot reasonably prepare a response"; it must point out the defects and the details desired. If an order is not obeyed within 14 days, the court may strike the pleading.
- **No particulars procedure.** Federal practice has no counterpart of the Hong Kong request; anything more is obtained in discovery. State of mind may be pleaded generally, so the Hong Kong rule against "knew or ought to have known" has no direct counterpart, but the factual circumstances of any fraud must be in the complaint.

## Verification and evidence

- **No statement of truth.** "Unless a rule or statute specifically states otherwise, a pleading need not be verified or accompanied by an affidavit." (Rule 11(a)). Every pleading, motion and paper is signed by at least one attorney of record, with the signer's address, e-mail address and telephone number.
- **The signature certifies (Rule 11(b)).** That the paper is not presented for an improper purpose; that the legal contentions are warranted; that factual contentions have, or after discovery will likely have, evidentiary support; and that denials are warranted. Sanctions under Rule 11(c) are sought by a separate motion that is served but not filed until 21 days have passed without correction (the safe harbor).
- **Declaration under 28 U.S.C. § 1746**, usable with like force and effect wherever a sworn affidavit is required (with stated exceptions). The statutory words:
  - Executed outside the United States: "I declare (or certify, verify, or state) under penalty of perjury under the laws of the United States of America that the foregoing is true and correct. Executed on (date). (Signature)"
  - Executed within the United States, its territories, possessions, or commonwealths: "I declare (or certify, verify, or state) under penalty of perjury that the foregoing is true and correct. Executed on (date). (Signature)"
  - A declarant in Hong Kong uses the first form.
- **No notary or commissioner** is needed for a § 1746 declaration. Who may take an affidavit, if one is used instead, was not researched.
- **Content (Rule 56(c)(4)).** An affidavit or declaration used on a motion "must be made on personal knowledge, set out facts that would be admissible in evidence, and show that the affiant or declarant is competent to testify on the matters stated." N.D. Cal. Civil L.R. 7-5(b): facts only, no conclusions or argument, and the basis of any statement on information or belief.
- **Interpreters and translations.** No federal rule was found; check the local rules and the judge's practices.

## Amendment

- **As a matter of course (Rule 15(a)(1)):** once, no later than 21 days after serving the pleading or, where a responsive pleading is required, 21 days after service of the responsive pleading or of a motion under Rule 12(b), (e) or (f), whichever is earlier.
- **Otherwise (Rule 15(a)(2)):** with the opposing party's written consent or the court's leave; "The court should freely give leave when justice so requires." Response to an amended pleading within the time left or 14 days, whichever is later (Rule 15(a)(3)).
- **Relation back (Rule 15(c))** where the claim arises out of the conduct, transaction or occurrence originally pleaded, or on the terms of Rule 15(c)(1)(C) for a change of party. **Supplemental pleadings** for events after the pleading, on motion (Rule 15(d)).
- **How changes are shown.** The amended pleading is a complete fresh document; it does not incorporate the earlier pleading by reference, and it has no colour or "re-amended" conventions. The local rules add a comparison:
  - S.D.N.Y./E.D.N.Y. Local Civil Rule 15.1(a): a motion under Rule 15(a)(2) or (d) attaches a clean copy of the proposed pleading and a version showing all differences "through redlining, underlining, strikeouts, or other similar typographic method"; file the new pleading within seven days of the order (15.1(b)).
  - N.D. Cal. Civil L.R. 15-1: two exhibits, the entire proposed pleading and a redlined or highlighted comparison with the operative pleading; a redline is filed with an amendment as of right too (15-1(c)).
  - W.D. Wash. LCR 15(a): the proposed amended pleading attached as an exhibit, with deletions bracketed or struck through and additions underlined or highlighted; LCR 15(b) requires the same marking for an amendment by consent.
  - D. Del. LR 15.1: the signed proposed pleading with exhibits, and a form showing deletions by bracketing or striking through and additions by underlining; the same form is filed for an amendment by consent (15.1(c)).

## Formal rules for papers

- **Caption (Rule 10(a)).** The court's name, a title, a file number and the Rule 7(a) designation; the complaint names all parties, later pleadings may name the first party on each side.
- **S.D.N.Y./E.D.N.Y. Local Civil Rule 7.1(b)-(c).** 12-point type or larger (footnotes 10-point); one-inch margins; double spacing except headings, footnotes and block quotations. Briefs in support of and in opposition to a motion are limited to 8,750 words and reply briefs to 3,500 words, excluding the caption, indexes, tables, signature blocks and required certificates but including footnotes. A brief prepared by computer carries counsel's certificate of compliance with the word count. Local Civil Rule 11.1 is withdrawn.
- **N.D. Cal. Civil L.R. 3-4.** First page: counsel's name, address, telephone, e-mail and state bar number at the upper left; from the eighth line, the court, the title of the action, the case number with the assigned judge's initials, and a title describing the paper. Text double-spaced, at most 28 lines a page, in a standard proportionally spaced font of 12 points or larger, no more than 10 characters per inch; a footer on every page with the title of the paper. Page limits: a motion 25 pages (Civil L.R. 7-2(b)), opposition 25 and reply 15 (7-4(b)); tables of contents and authorities above 10 pages (7-4(a)(2)).
- **W.D. Wash. LCR 10(e) and 7(e).** At least three inches clear at the top of the first page and one-inch margins elsewhere; 12-point text, double-spaced or exactly 24 points (footnotes 10-point, single-spaced allowed); a proportionally spaced font; the court's name on the first page and a title showing the paper's purpose and the party presenting it; line numbers in the left margin. Word limits by noting category: 2,100 words (LCR 7(d)(1) motions); 4,200 with replies of 2,100 (7(d)(2)); 4,200 (7(d)(3)); 8,400 with replies of 4,200 (7(d)(4)); 6,300 for motions in limine. Where a word limit applies, the signature block certifies, substantially: "I certify that this memorandum contains [number] words, in compliance with the Local Civil Rules."
- **D. Del. LR 5.1.1 and 7.1.3.** 8½ by 11 inch paper, double-spaced by ordinary word-processing spacing ("exactly 24 point" is not accepted), 12-point type, one-inch margins, pages numbered; the date of filing and a descriptive title; Delaware counsel's name, bar number, address, telephone and e-mail under the signature. Brief covers carry the court, caption, action number, nature of the paper, filing date, party and counsel. Opening and answering briefs 20 pages, reply briefs 10, excluding tables (LR 7.1.3(a)(4)). Under the 2019 Standing Order a party may instead use 250 words a page (5,000 words for a 20-page limit) with a word-count certification.
- **Colour.** No federal ban is recorded; W.D. Wash. LCR 15 expressly allows highlighting in a comparison.

## Applications and motions

- **S.D.N.Y./E.D.N.Y. Local Civil Rule 7.1(a):** a notice of motion (or an order to show cause), a memorandum of law, and supporting affidavits and exhibits.
- **N.D. Cal. Civil L.R. 7-2 and 7-4:** one document of no more than 25 pages, with the noticed hearing date and time on the first page, notice of the motion in the first paragraph, the relief sought in the second, then the points and authorities; noticed for hearing not less than 35 days after filing. The memorandum contains a statement of the issues to be decided, a succinct statement of the facts, and argument citing authority.
- **D. Del. LR 7.1.3(b):** an opening or answering brief has, in order, a table of contents, a table of citations, a statement of the nature and stage of the proceedings, a summary of argument in separately numbered paragraphs, a concise statement of facts with record references, the argument under headings, and a short conclusion stating the precise relief sought. Material that belongs in the opening brief may not be held back for the reply (7.1.3(b)(2)).
- **Motions to dismiss.** The Rule 12(b) defenses (including (6), failure to state a claim) may be raised by motion before the answer; otherwise in the answer and by motion for judgment on the pleadings (Rule 12(c)). A motion to strike "an insufficient defense or any redundant, immaterial, impertinent, or scandalous matter" is made before responding or within 21 days (Rule 12(f)).
- **Summary judgment (Rule 56(a)):** "The court shall grant summary judgment if the movant shows that there is no genuine dispute as to any material fact and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law." The motion identifies each claim or defense, or part, on which judgment is sought. S.D.N.Y./E.D.N.Y. Local Civil Rule 56.1 requires a separate, short and concise statement of material facts in numbered paragraphs, each followed by a citation to admissible evidence, and a correspondingly numbered response; paragraphs not controverted are deemed admitted.
- **Meet and confer.** No general federal requirement for these motions was found; check the district's rules and the judge's individual practices.
- **Security for costs.** No general rule; S.D.N.Y./E.D.N.Y. Local Civil Rule 54.2 allows the court to order a bond for costs.

## Citation style

The Bluebook. The bracketed items show the shape of a citation only; in a draft every authority comes from the user (see `authorities.md`).

- Courts of appeals: `[Case name], [volume] F.3d [page], [pin] ([court] [year])`, for example with "(9th Cir. [year])".
- District courts: `[Case name], [volume] F. Supp. 3d [page], [pin] ([court] [year])`, with the district always shown.
- Supreme Court: `[Case name], [volume] U.S. [page], [pin] ([year])`; S. Ct. where the U.S. Reports cite is not yet available.
- Database: `[Case name], No. [case number], [year] WL [number], at *[pin] ([court] [date])`.
- Short forms: `[Case name], [volume] F.3d at [pin].` and `Id. at [pin].`; do not use the name of a government or common litigant as the short form.
- Statutes: "28 U.S.C. § 1746". Rules: "Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(b)(6)". Local rules as they cite themselves: "S.D.N.Y. Local Civ. R. 7.1", "Civil L.R. 3-4", "LCR 7(e)", "D. Del. LR 7.1.3".
- Local overrides: N.D. Cal. Civil L.R. 3-4(d) and D. Del. LR 7.1.3(a)(5) require National Reporter System citations for lower federal courts and the official U.S. cite for the Supreme Court. Record citations: "D.I. [number]" (D. Del. LR 7.1.3(a)(6)); "Dkt. # [number] at p. [number]" (W.D. Wash. LCR 10(e)(6)).
- Before citing an unpublished or nonprecedential decision, check the issuing court's rule on citing it.

## Forms that exist only here

- **The jurisdiction paragraph** in a complaint.
- **The affirmative defenses section** of an answer, and the compulsory counterclaim.
- **The Rule 11 safe-harbor motion**, served 21 days before filing.
- **The Rule 12(e) motion** for a more definite statement.
- **The clean amended pleading with a redline exhibit**, under the district's rule.
- **The statement of material facts** on summary judgment (S.D.N.Y./E.D.N.Y. Local Civil Rule 56.1).
- **Word-count certificates** in or after the signature block.

## Background: where the standards come from

Background only. Cite these in a draft only if the user supplies or verifies them.

- *Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly*, 550 U.S. 544 (2007), and *Ashcroft v. Iqbal*, 556 U.S. 662 (2009): the plausibility standard for a complaint under Rule 8(a)(2) and Rule 12(b)(6). Twombly's passages at 555 and 570 were verified through Iqbal's own citations.
- *Vess v. Ciba-Geigy Corp. USA*, 317 F.3d 1097, 1106 (9th Cir. 2003): the Ninth Circuit's "who, what, when, where, and how" reading of Rule 9(b).
- *Foman v. Davis*, 371 U.S. 178 (1962): leave to amend is to be freely given absent undue delay, bad faith, repeated failure to cure, undue prejudice or futility.
- *Celotex Corp. v. Catrett*, 477 U.S. 317 (1986), and *Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.*, 477 U.S. 242 (1986): the summary judgment burden and what makes a dispute genuine.

## Not verified

- The conventional pinpoints for Iqbal (556 U.S. at 678), Foman (371 U.S. at 182), Celotex (477 U.S. at 322) and Anderson (477 U.S. at 248); the texts read were unpaginated.
- The Vess opinion itself was not fetched; its citation and pinpoint were confirmed through a Ninth Circuit memorandum that quotes it and through a search index.
- Rule 12(d) (conversion of a motion to dismiss into one for summary judgment) was not quoted from the federal text.
- Rule 15(c)(1)(C)(i)-(ii) is paraphrased, not quoted.
- D. Del. LR 9.4 (restricting a stated sum for unliquidated damages) was seen in the table of contents only.
- Individual District of Delaware judges' form scheduling orders vary the briefing limits; check the assigned judge's standing order.
- Not researched: who may administer oaths for a federal affidavit; translation of foreign-language declarations; meet-and-confer duties; proposed-order practice. Check the district's rules and the judge's practices.
