# Variant `pleadings`: advice on causes of action and how to plead them

## What it is for

An advice whose job is to feed the drafting of a pleading: which causes of action the client has, which to plead and which to leave out, what the other side's answer is likely to be, and what relief to claim. Use it before a statement of claim, counterclaim, points of claim or amendment is drafted, or when the solicitors ask whether the existing pleading needs changing. Every cause of action the advice recommends ends with the particulars to plead, so the pleader can work straight from it.

## Structure (Hong Kong)

1. **Heading.** Case number and court (or the arbitration reference) if proceedings exist; "PRIVILEGED AND CONFIDENTIAL"; the title ("ADVICE ON PLEADING: CAUSES OF ACTION AND REMEDIES").
2. **A. Overview.** Who instructs and for whom; the forum and any related proceedings; what counsel is asked to do, as a lettered list; the purpose in one line ("to assist in drafting the [document title]"); the documents relied on (Annex 1). For a long advice, a short table of contents. Then the issues as numbered questions, for example the nature of the agreement, each cause of action, the other side's likely answer, and the relief.
3. **Executive Summary.** Per issue: the view on the scale, hedged. Say plainly which causes should be pleaded and which should not yet be pleaded, and why. List the relief to be claimed.
4. **B. Background.** The parties; the essential events as dated paragraphs with sources; a chronology table in an annex if long (Date | Event | Source | Note, with the Note column recording what is unknown).
5. **C. The Agreements.** The operative terms of each contract or instrument reproduced verbatim, section by section, under "[document title] contains the following express terms:", with "(emphasis added)" and "(sic)" as needed. Analysis comes after, not here. A pleading contains a statement in a summary form of the material facts (O.18 r.7), but where the dispute turns on wording the pleader needs the exact words; say so in a footnote if the advice recommends pleading words verbatim.
6. **D. Analysis.** One sub-section per cause of action:
   - the elements, from the supplied authorities, quoted and then distilled ("the following elements must be present: (1) … (4) …");
   - the application, element by element, with the documents that prove each;
   - the interim conclusion on the scale, hedged: "… can, and should, be pleaded", or "there is presently insufficient material to plead …";
   - **Particulars to plead**: a lettered list (a), (b), (c) of the material facts and particulars the pleading must contain for that cause, in the order the elements run.
   Where a cause needs particulars by rule (misrepresentation, fraud, breach of trust, wilful default, undue influence, a condition of mind, knowledge or notice: O.18 r.12), the list must give them. A plea of fraud needs a proper evidential basis before it is advised: if the material is not there, say so and advise against pleading it yet.
7. **E. The other side's case.** Its answer, defence, letter or argument in related proceedings, taken paragraph by paragraph with declared `[R§n]` or `[D§n]` labels, and pre-rebutted ("First, … Second, …").
8. **F. Damages and remedies.** Each head of relief: whether it is claimed in a sum or "to be assessed"; a minimum sum only from the user's figures, with the arithmetic and assumptions shown in the text; interest under `[statutory provision]` or the contract rate the user gives. End with "Relief to be claimed", in the order it will appear in the prayer.
9. **G. Information required.** The fixed list and the formal request.
10. **H. Conclusion.** The hedge and the formal two-sentence close. No recap.
11. **Signature and annexes.** As in `merits.md`, plus, if useful, a dramatis personae table (Name | Role | What is unknown).

## Differences for the US profiles

- **Counts, not causes.** Each recommended cause of action becomes a count (a claim for relief). Head the list "Particulars to plead (facts to allege)": the elements and the facts that must be alleged for each.
- **The pleading standard decides how much fact goes in.** Read the profile: plausibility in federal court (FRCP 8(a)), with fraud and mistake pleaded with particularity (FRCP 9(b): who, what, when, where, how); ultimate facts in California, with fraud particularised in full; the material elements in New York, with CPLR 3016(b) matters "stated in detail"; notice pleading in Washington, with CR 9(b) particularity; reasonable conceivability in Delaware, where the Superior Court also requires negligence to be particularised and the Court of Chancery requires particularity for demand futility in a derivative claim. Advise to the standard of the court the claim will be filed in.
- **Contract terms.** Recommend quoting the operative terms or attaching the instrument as an exhibit (FRCP 10(c) and the state equivalents treat an attached written instrument as part of the pleading).
- **Damages figures.** Some courts restrict pleading a figure for unliquidated or personal-injury damages (for example the Delaware Superior Court, and California in personal-injury actions). Check the profile before advising a sum in the prayer.
- **Prayer.** "WHEREFORE, [Plaintiff] respectfully requests that the Court enter judgment in its favor and against [Defendant] as follows:" and the tail "such other and further relief as the Court deems just and proper."
- **Pre-filing certification.** The signing attorney certifies the factual contentions have or will likely have evidentiary support (FRCP 11(b) and the state equivalents). An advice that recommends a count should say what evidence supports each element, or what inquiry is still needed.

## Skeleton

The ellipses mark counsel's own prose. Bracketed labels are fields from `references/fields.md`.

```
[case number] ([court])

PRIVILEGED AND CONFIDENTIAL

ADVICE ON PLEADING: CAUSES OF ACTION AND REMEDIES

A. OVERVIEW

1. Instructing solicitors, [solicitors-firm], act for [Plaintiff] ("P") in its claim against [Defendant] ("D").

2. I am asked to advise on P's potential causes of action against D and how they should be pleaded. In particular, I am asked to:
   (a) identify the causes of action available to P;
   (b) advise on D's likely answer; and
   (c) advise on the relief P should claim.

3. The purpose of this advice is to assist in drafting the [document title]. It is based on the documents provided to me and listed in Annex 1.

4. The issues are:
   Issue 1: What is the nature of the Agreement?
   Issue 2: Which causes of action are available to P, and which should be pleaded?
   Issue 3: What relief should P claim?

Executive Summary

5. In a nutshell, on the information provided, I am of the view that:
   (a) Issue 1: the Agreement is more likely than not to be construed as … ;
   (b) Issue 2: breach of contract and … can, and should, be pleaded. There is presently insufficient material to plead … ;
   (c) Issue 3: P should claim the relief listed in paragraph [number].

6. Each issue is analysed in turn below. My views are subject to the information provided.

B. BACKGROUND

7. P is and was at all material times … .

8. On [date 1], … [Bundle ref].

C. THE AGREEMENT

9. [document title] dated [date 1] ("the Agreement") contains the following express terms:

   "…" (emphasis added)

D. ANALYSIS

D.1 Breach of contract

10. The elements P must establish are: (1) … ; (2) … ; (3) … [authority needed: elements of the cause of action].

11. Applying the principles here, the question to ask is as follows: whether … ?

12. … .

13. In interim conclusion, breach of contract can, and should, be pleaded.

14. Particulars to plead. The pleading should include the following:
    (a) the Agreement, its date and parties;
    (b) the terms relied on, by clause number;
    (c) P's performance;
    (d) each breach, with its date;
    (e) the loss caused, by head.

D.2 …

E. D'S CASE

15. [D§n] = paragraph n of D's [document title] dated [date 2].

17. On the figures provided, P's loss under this head is at least [sum 1], calculated as follows: … .

    (1) Damages for breach of contract;
    (2) … ;
    (3) Interest pursuant to [statutory provision];
    (4) Further and/or other relief as the Court deems fit;
    (5) Costs.

19. The following matters are unknown and bear on the views above:
    (a) … ;
    (b) … .

20. I would be grateful if instructing solicitors would provide that information before the pleading is settled.

H. CONCLUSION

21. My views are subject to the information provided, and may change as further information is obtained.

22. The foregoing is my preliminary advice on these matters. I would be happy to assist or advise further if so required.
```

## Checklist

What the checker tests:

- everything in the `merits` checklist;
- "Particulars to plead", "should be pleaded" or "facts to allege" present;
- a relief list (soft).

What the drafter checks by eye:

- every recommended cause or count has its particulars, in the order of its elements;
- every cause advised against has a reason, and the advice says what would change it;
- any plea needing particulars by rule (fraud, misrepresentation, knowledge, condition of mind) has them, or the advice says it should not yet be pleaded;
- contract terms are quoted exactly, with clause numbers;
- every sum comes from the user's figures with the arithmetic shown, and no interest provision has been chosen for the user;
- the relief list matches the causes recommended.
