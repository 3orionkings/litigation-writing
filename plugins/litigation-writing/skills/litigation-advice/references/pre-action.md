# Variant `pre-action`: advice on potential claims or defences before proceedings

## What it is for

Counsel's advice before any proceedings exist: on a claim the client is thinking of bringing, or on the defences open to a client who has received a demand or expects to be sued. It answers whether there is a claim or defence worth running, what the client would have to prove, what must be done now (limitation, preservation, the letter before action, notice to insurers), and how the dispute might be resolved without proceedings.

It comes in two forms:

- **Written advice**, structured like a merits advice.
- **Point form for oral advice**: notes counsel speaks to in a conference with the solicitors and the client. Shorter, numbered, bottom line first, with the same discipline on likelihood and hedging. Use it when the user asks for conference notes, speaking points or "point form".

## Structure (Hong Kong), written advice

1. **Heading.** "PRIVILEGED AND CONFIDENTIAL"; the title ("ADVICE ON POTENTIAL DEFENCES TO …" or "ADVICE ON A POTENTIAL CLAIM AGAINST …"). No case number. Parties are described as intended parties ("[Plaintiff], the intended plaintiff ("P")").
2. **A. Overview.** Who instructs and for whom; what has happened that prompts the advice (a demand received, a dispute arising); what counsel is asked; the documents (Annex 1). Then the issues as numbered questions.
3. **Executive Summary.** The bottom line first, in one sentence. A plain-words gloss ("Put simply, …"). Then each potential claim or defence with its elements as a checklist and the burden stated first: "The burden lies on [Party] to prove, on the balance of probabilities, that: (a) … ; (b) … ." The hand-off and the hedge.
4. **B. Background.** The essential facts as dated paragraphs, each with its source.
5. **C. Analysis.** One sub-section per potential claim or defence: principle, test, "Applying the principles here, the question to ask is as follows:", application, interim conclusion. Where one issue only arises if another is made out, say so ("If Issue 1 is made out, the next question is …").
6. **D. Steps before proceedings.** Each a numbered paragraph, so the solicitors can act on it:
   - **limitation**: the period under `[statutory provision]`, the date time is likely to run from on the user's facts, and whether anything is urgent. Never compute or choose the period yourself;
   - **forum**: any arbitration, jurisdiction or dispute-resolution clause, and what it requires before proceedings;
   - **pre-action steps**: Hong Kong has no general pre-action protocol, but some practice directions require steps for particular kinds of claim (`[rule]`); the letter before action, or the response to the demand received;
   - **preservation**: documents and electronic data to be kept, and by whom;
   - **insurance**: whether any policy may respond and whether notice should be given;
   - **interim relief**: whether there is any risk to assets or evidence that would justify urgent relief;
   - **settlement and costs**: the routes open (without prejudice negotiation, a without prejudice save as to costs proposal, a sanctioned offer under Order 22 where the rule allows it, mediation), and the costs exposure in outline.
7. **E. Information required.** The fixed list and the formal request.
8. **F. Conclusion.** The overall view in one sentence, the hedge, an optional hazards-of-litigation line, the formal close.
9. **Signature, Annex 1 and the two authority lists.**

## Point form for oral advice

- The date on the first line. Then "Re: …" with a descriptive subject and "(Point Form for Oral Advice)". No signature block is needed.
- "Executive Summary" comes first and carries the bottom line, the gloss, and the elements with the burden.
- A one-line list of the sections that follow.
- Sections in Roman numerals (I. Facts, II. Potential Defences, III. …, V. Conclusion), sub-sections (A), (B), and every point numbered 1, 2, 3 continuously, with sub-points (a), (b). Each point is one or two sentences.
- Each claim or defence still ends on an "Interim Conclusion" heading with a hedged view on the scale.
- The conclusion may be substantive here ("Taking all of the above into account, I consider that …"), because there is no separate summary at the end of a conference; keep it to the view and any point not made earlier, never a recap.
- The authority lists follow, as in the written form.

## Differences for the US profiles

- **Pre-suit duties.** Once litigation is reasonably anticipated, the client must preserve relevant evidence; recommend a litigation hold and say who should receive it. The signing attorney's pre-filing inquiry (FRCP 11(b), 22 NYCRR 130-1.1a, Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 128.7, Wash. CR 11, Del. Super. Ct. Civ. R. 11, Del. Ch. Ct. R. 11) means the advice should say what further inquiry is needed before a complaint can be signed.
- **Limitation.** "Statute of limitations" or "limitations period", always `[statutory provision]`; also any contractual limitation clause, any tolling agreement in place or worth proposing, and any pre-suit notice or claim requirement (claims against public bodies, some insurance and consumer statutes, a pre-suit demand in a Delaware derivative claim), each as `[statutory provision]` or `[rule]`.
- **Forum.** Forum-selection, arbitration and jury-waiver clauses; whether the claim belongs in federal or state court is noted only as a question for the user if the profile does not settle it.
- **Settlement.** Early negotiations are protected only as far as FRE 408 or the state equivalent goes; there is no "without prejudice save as to costs" device. Costs-shifting offers come later, through an offer of judgment where the rule provides one.
- **Form.** A privileged memorandum ("TO / FROM / DATE / RE"), headed as in `merits.md`. Point-form notes keep the same shape as the Hong Kong form.

## Skeleton: point form for oral advice

The ellipses mark counsel's own prose. Bracketed labels are fields from `references/fields.md`.

```
[date 1]

PRIVILEGED AND CONFIDENTIAL

Re: Potential defences to a claim by [Plaintiff] against [Defendant] under [document title] (Point Form for Oral Advice)

Executive Summary

1. I am asked to advise on the defences open to [Defendant] ("D") if [Plaintiff] ("P") sues on [document title] ("the Agreement"), on the documents listed in Annex 1.

2. On the information provided, there is presently insufficient material to support any defence that is more likely than not to succeed.

3. Put simply, the defences open to D are difficult to prove on the documents seen so far.

4. Two potential defences arise: (a) … ("the 1st Potential Defence"); and (b) … ("the 2nd Potential Defence"). The burden lies on D to prove each on the balance of probabilities.

5. For the 1st Potential Defence, D would have to establish that: (a) … ; (b) … ; and (c) … .

6. The sections below are: I. Facts; II. The 1st Potential Defence; III. The 2nd Potential Defence; IV. Steps Before Proceedings; V. Information Required; VI. Conclusion.

I. Facts

7. On [date 2], D signed the Agreement [Bundle ref].

8. I am instructed that … .

II. The 1st Potential Defence

(A) Legal principles

9. The relevant principles are at [authority needed: elements of the defence] at [§ needed].

(B) Issue 1: Whether …?

10. Applying the principles here, the question to ask is as follows: whether … ?

11. … .

(C) Interim Conclusion

12. On the information provided, I am of the view that the 1st Potential Defence is unlikely to succeed unless … .

III. The 2nd Potential Defence

13. … .

IV. Steps Before Proceedings

14. Limitation: the period under [statutory provision] appears to run from [date 3]. This should be confirmed.

15. Documents and electronic data relating to … should be preserved now, including … .

V. Information Required

16. I have no information on: (a) … ; (b) … .

17. I would be grateful if instructing solicitors would obtain that information before D responds to P's letter dated [date 4].

VI. Conclusion

18. Taking all of the above into account, and on the information provided, I consider that … .

19. The foregoing is my preliminary advice on these matters. I would be happy to assist or advise further if so required.

LIST OF AUTHORITIES

1. [authority needed: elements of the defence]

OTHER AUTHORITIES CONSIDERED

1. …
```

For a written pre-action advice, use the `merits.md` skeleton with the heading and parties described as intended, and add Section D, Steps before proceedings, before the information required.

## Checklist

What the checker tests:

- everything in the `merits` checklist (point form included: every point numbered);
- a reference to limitation (soft), so the time point is never forgotten.

What the drafter checks by eye:

- the bottom line comes first and matches the conclusion;
- each potential claim or defence lists its elements with the burden stated;
- the steps before proceedings are concrete and none of them chooses the law (limitation period, notice requirement) for the user;
- in point form, each point is short enough to speak, and nothing is lost from the hedging;
- the client is described as the intended party, never as "you".
