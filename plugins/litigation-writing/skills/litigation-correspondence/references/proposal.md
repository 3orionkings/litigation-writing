# Variant `proposal`: open, without prejudice, or without prejudice save as to costs

## What it is for

A letter putting forward terms to settle all or part of a dispute, or a step towards settlement (mediation, a without prejudice meeting). Which of three forms it takes decides who will ever read it.

This variant is built from general practice and the rules, not from a precedent. Keep it conservative: the terms are exactly those the client has authorised, nothing in the letter admits liability, and the status is marked at the top.

## Choosing the status (Hong Kong)

| Marking | Who may see it | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| No marking, or "OPEN" | The court, at any time | A position the client is content for the judge to see: an offer to mediate, a proposal for directions, an open offer of terms meant to show reasonableness |
| "WITHOUT PREJUDICE" | The parties only; the court does not see it, save in the narrow cases the law allows (`[authority needed: the exceptions to without prejudice privilege]`) | Exploratory negotiation and concessions the client does not want the court to see, ever |
| "WITHOUT PREJUDICE SAVE AS TO COSTS" | The court, but only once liability and quantum are decided, on the question of costs | An offer the client wants to count on costs if it is refused and the client does as well or better |

Points that decide the choice:

- The label does not create the protection. Without prejudice protection attaches to a genuine attempt to settle; a letter that only complains or threatens is open whatever it says at the top. Mark accurately all the same, because an unmarked offer invites an argument.
- A without prejudice save as to costs offer is not a sanctioned offer. The court may take it into account in its discretion on costs, but the defined consequences of O.22 (costs and enhanced interest) attach only to an offer that complies with that Order. If the client wants those, use the `offer` variant, or send both.
- An offer to mediate, and the response to one, usually belong in an open letter, so that the court can see them when it considers costs. Check Practice Direction 31 for the forms to use.
- Never mix statuses. A letter that both complains (open) and offers terms (without prejudice) becomes two letters.

## Structure (Hong Kong)

1. **Status heading**, alone on the first line, in capitals: "WITHOUT PREJUDICE SAVE AS TO COSTS", "WITHOUT PREJUDICE" or "OPEN LETTER".
2. **Header and "Re:" line**, as for any letter.
3. **Opening.** "We refer to …" (the action, or the last letter).
4. **Purpose.** That the letter sets out the client's proposal to settle the action (or the claim, or an issue), on the basis stated in the heading.
5. **The terms**, numbered, each a single obligation: what is paid or done, by whom, by when and how; what claims are settled (the action, any counterclaim, all claims arising from the matters in the action); how the proceedings end (a consent order, a stay on terms, a discontinuance); costs ("each party to bear its own costs", a fixed sum, or "to be taxed if not agreed"); confidentiality if wanted; that the proposal is made without any admission of liability; and, if a formal agreement is to follow, that the terms are subject to contract.
6. **Acceptance.** How to accept (in writing to the firm), and until when: "open for acceptance until [time] on [date]". After that date the proposal lapses.
7. **For a without prejudice save as to costs letter**, the reservation: the client will refer to the letter on the question of costs.
8. **Sign-off.**

## Differences for the US profiles

- **Marking.** "CONFIDENTIAL SETTLEMENT COMMUNICATION" and the evidence rule that limits its use: FRE 408 in federal court, and the state equivalent (Cal. Evid. Code §§ 1152 and 1154; CPLR 4547; Wash. ER 408; Del. R. Evid. 408), each to be checked against the profile. These rules stop a compromise offer being used to prove or disprove the claim; they do not make facts or documents inadmissible merely because they appear in a settlement letter, and they allow some other uses. So put nothing in the letter that the client could not afford to have proved some other way.
- **No "save as to costs".** US practice has no direct equivalent. A costs-shifting offer is made under a rule (FRCP 68, Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 998, CPLR 3221 and others): use the `offer` variant.
- **Mediation.** Court-annexed mediation and ADR programmes are set by local rule or order (`[rule]`).
- **Sign-off.** "Very truly yours," or "Sincerely," then `[counsel-name]` and the firm.

## Skeleton (Hong Kong, without prejudice save as to costs)

The ellipses mark the drafter's own words. Bracketed labels are fields from `references/fields.md`.

```
WITHOUT PREJUDICE SAVE AS TO COSTS

Our Ref.: [number 1]
Your Ref.: [number 2]
Date: [date 1]

BY EMAIL ([address 1])

[solicitors-firm 1]
[address 2]

Attn: [name 1]

Dear Sirs,

Re: [case number] ("the Action")
[Plaintiff]: [name 2] ("our client")
[Defendant]: [name 3]
Proposal for settlement

We refer to the Action.

We are instructed to put forward the following proposal to settle the Action. It is made without prejudice save as to costs.

1. Your client pays our client the sum of [sum 1] on or before [date 2], by transfer to [account number].

2. The payment is in full and final settlement of all claims in the Action, including the counterclaim.

3. Within [period] of receipt of the payment, the parties file a consent order staying the Action on those terms.

4. Each party bears its own costs of the Action.

5. The terms of the settlement are confidential to the parties.

6. This proposal is made without any admission of liability.

This proposal is open for acceptance in writing until [time] on [date 3], after which it lapses without further notice.

Our client reserves the right to bring this letter to the attention of the Court on the question of costs.

Yours faithfully,

[solicitors-firm 2]
```

For a without prejudice letter, change the heading and leave out the costs reservation. For an open letter, head it "OPEN LETTER" and write it as if the judge will read it, because the judge may.

## Checklist

What the checker tests:

- a status heading at the top ("WITHOUT PREJUDICE", "WITHOUT PREJUDICE SAVE AS TO COSTS", "OPEN LETTER", or the US settlement-communication marking);
- a deadline for acceptance;
- a no-admission line (soft);
- a sign-off at the end and a "Re:" line near the start (soft).

What the drafter checks by eye:

- the status is right for what the client wants the court to see, and when;
- every term, sum, date and account is from the client's instructions;
- the terms say what is settled, how the proceedings end, and who pays the costs;
- nothing in an open letter concedes anything the client would not concede in court;
- if the client wants the O.22 or Rule 68 consequences, a rule-based offer has been prepared as well.
