# California

This profile covers civil actions in the California Superior Court, with San Francisco as the example of local practice. The governing rules are the Code of Civil Procedure ("CCP"), the California Rules of Court 2026 ("CRC"), and the San Francisco Superior Court Uniform Local Rules of Court ("LRSF", revised July 1, 2026; changes proposed to take effect January 1, 2027 were out for comment). CRC 2.100 reserves the form and format of papers to the Judicial Council, so local rules do not vary it. Citation follows the California Style Manual (4th ed. 2000) or the Bluebook, at the filer's option. CCP 473 exists in two versions on the official site, one effective January 1, 2026 and one operative January 1, 2027. Researched October 2026. Check the department's rules and any tentative-ruling practice before filing.

## Document names

| Document type | California name |
|---|---|
| Parties | Plaintiff and Defendant; Cross-Complainant and Cross-Defendant; Does 1 through [number] for unknown defendants |
| Claim pleading | Complaint |
| Defence | Answer |
| Reply | None. New matter in the answer is deemed controverted (CCP 431.20(b)) |
| Counterclaim | Cross-Complaint, a separate document (CCP 428.10); it also carries third-party, contribution and indemnity claims, and is answered like a complaint. California has no "counterclaim" |
| Amendment | First Amended Complaint (serially numbered: CRC 3.1324(a)(1)), with the deletions and additions listed by page, paragraph and line |
| Evidence instrument | Declaration under CCP 2015.5; affidavits are also used |
| Witness statement | No separate form; evidence on a motion is a declaration |
| Skeleton | Memorandum of points and authorities (CRC 3.1113) |
| Hearing note | No equivalent. In San Francisco the nearest short paper is the notice contesting a tentative ruling, which names the parts contested without argument (LRSF 8.3) |
| Application papers | Notice of hearing, the motion, a memorandum, supporting declarations and exhibits (CRC 3.1112(a)); any proposed order lodged separately (CRC 3.1113(m)) |

| Request for particulars | None. A special demurrer for uncertainty (CCP 430.10(f)) is the nearest device; detail comes through discovery |
| Attacks on a pleading | Demurrer (general or special); motion to strike |

## Pleading standard

CCP 425.10(a): a complaint or cross-complaint contains "(1) A statement of the facts constituting the cause of action, in ordinary and concise language. (2) A demand for judgment for the relief to which the pleader claims to be entitled. If the recovery of money or damages is demanded, the amount demanded shall be stated."

- **Fact pleading.** California is a fact-pleading jurisdiction, the closest of the US profiles to Hong Kong's material facts. Plead the ultimate facts for each element of each cause of action; contentions and conclusions are not admitted on demurrer (see "Background").
- **Material and immaterial.** A material allegation is one essential to the claim or defense; an immaterial allegation is one not essential, not pertinent, or a demand for relief the allegations do not support, and may be struck as irrelevant matter (CCP 431.10, 436). Allegations are liberally construed (CCP 452).
- **Headings (CRC 2.112).** Each cause of action, count or defense states its number, its nature, the party asserting it where more than one is represented, and the party against whom it is directed: "FIRST CAUSE OF ACTION (Breach of Contract by [Plaintiff] against [Defendant])".
- **Contract actions.** It must be possible to tell from the pleading whether the contract is written, oral or implied by conduct, or the complaint is open to demurrer (CCP 430.10(g)).
- **Unknown defendants.** State the plaintiff's ignorance of the true name and sue the defendant under a fictitious name; amend when the name is discovered (CCP 474).
- **Relief.** State the amount demanded for money or damages. In personal injury and wrongful death actions state no amount; the defendant may request a statement of the nature and amount of damages, served within 15 days, and the statement must be served before a default is taken (CCP 425.10(b), 425.11).
- **Judicial Council forms.** Official form complaints, cross-complaints and answers exist for personal injury, property damage, wrongful death, unlawful detainer, breach of contract and fraud (CCP 425.12).

## Answering a pleading

CCP 431.30(b): the answer contains "(1) The general or specific denial of the material allegations of the complaint controverted by the defendant. (2) A statement of any new matter constituting a defense."

- **The decisive question is verification.** Against an unverified complaint a general denial is sufficient and puts the material allegations in issue (CCP 431.30(d)): "Defendant generally denies each and every allegation of the complaint." Against a verified complaint the denials are made positively or on information and belief, paragraph by paragraph, and the answer must itself be verified (CCP 431.30(d), 446). The general denial rule does not reach the economic litigation procedures for limited civil cases (CCP 90 and following).
- **No knowledge.** A defendant with no information or belief sufficient to answer an allegation may say so and deny on that ground (CCP 431.30(e)).
- **Ways to deny (CCP 431.30(f)).** By reference to specific paragraphs; by admitting some allegations with a general denial of the rest; or by denying some on information and belief, or for lack of information or belief, with a general denial of the rest.
- **Effect of silence.** Every material allegation not controverted is taken as true (CCP 431.20(a)).
- **Affirmative defenses.** New matter is pleaded in separately stated defenses, each referring to the causes of action it answers (CCP 431.30(g)) and headed under CRC 2.112: "FIRST AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSE (Statute of Limitations, to all causes of action)". The CCP sections read contain no list of affirmative defenses.
- **No relief in the answer.** "Affirmative relief may not be claimed in the answer." (CCP 431.30(c)). Claims go in a cross-complaint, and a related cause of action against the plaintiff not pleaded in a cross-complaint is barred (CCP 426.30(a)). Set-off may still be raised as new matter.
- **Demurrer and answer together.** A party may demur and answer at the same time (CCP 430.30(c)).

## Particulars

- **No particulars procedure.** There is no request for further and better particulars and no bill of particulars. Fraud is therefore fully particularised in the complaint: the facts showing how, when, where, to whom and by what means the representations were made, and against a company the names of the persons who made them, their authority to speak, to whom they spoke, what they said or wrote, and when (see "Background"). This is stricter than Hong Kong practice.
- **Uncertainty.** A pleading that is uncertain ("ambiguous and unintelligible") is open to special demurrer (CCP 430.10(f)).
- **Damages.** The personal injury statement of damages (CCP 425.11) is the one statutory request for detail.

## Verification and evidence

- **Signature (CCP 128.7(a)).** Every pleading, petition, written notice of motion or similar paper is signed by at least one attorney of record, with the signer's address and telephone number. "Except when otherwise provided by law, pleadings need not be verified or accompanied by affidavit."
- **Certification (CCP 128.7(b)).** Signing certifies proper purpose, warranted legal contentions, evidentiary support for factual contentions (or, if so identified, likely support after discovery), and warranted denials. A sanctions motion is served and may be filed only if the paper is not withdrawn or corrected within 21 days (CCP 128.7(c)(1)).
- **Verification (CCP 446).** Optional for the plaintiff; once the complaint is verified, the answer must be. The party's affidavit states that the pleading is true of the party's own knowledge, except matters stated on information or belief, which the party believes to be true. An attorney or officer may verify in the cases the section lists; the affidavit then states that the verifier has read the pleading and is informed and believes the matters in it to be true, and the pleading does not count as evidence of the facts. A verifier may assert truth "under penalty of perjury" instead of swearing.
- **Declaration under CCP 2015.5.** A declaration certified or declared true under penalty of perjury, subscribed, and stating the date and place of execution (within California) or the date and that it is made under the laws of the State of California (anywhere), has the force of a sworn statement. The statutory forms:
  - Executed within this state: "I certify (or declare) under penalty of perjury that the foregoing is true and correct": followed by (Date and Place) and (Signature).
  - Executed at any place, within or without this state: "I certify (or declare) under penalty of perjury under the laws of the State of California that the foregoing is true and correct": followed by (Date) and (Signature).
  - A declarant in Hong Kong uses the second form.
- **No notary or commissioner** is needed for a declaration.
- **Caption of a declaration (CRC 3.1115).** It states the declarant's name and identifies the motion or proceeding it supports or opposes.
- **Foreign-language exhibits** are accompanied by an English translation certified under oath by a qualified interpreter (CRC 3.1110(g)).
- **Summary judgment evidence.** Supporting and opposing declarations are made on personal knowledge (CCP 437c(d)).

## Amendment

- **Without leave (CCP 472(a)).** Once, before the answer, demurrer or motion to strike is filed; or after a demurrer or motion to strike is filed but before it is heard, if the amended pleading is filed and served by the date for filing an opposition. Later, by stipulation.
- **With leave (CCP 473(a)(1)).** The court may, in furtherance of justice and on proper terms, allow amendment to add or strike out a party's name, correct a mistake, or amend in other particulars after notice; it may require costs as a condition (473(a)(2)).
- **The motion (CRC 3.1324).** It must (1) include the proposed amendment or amended pleading, serially numbered to distinguish it from earlier ones; (2) state the allegations to be deleted and where they are, by page, paragraph and line; and (3) state the allegations to be added and where, by page, paragraph and line. A separate declaration states the effect of the amendment, why it is necessary and proper, when the facts giving rise to it were discovered, and why the request was not made earlier. No alterations on the face of a pleading except by permission, initialled by the court or clerk (3.1324(d)). In San Francisco the amendment, once granted, is filed as a separate document (LRSF 8.5).
- **After a demurrer.** Leave to answer or amend within 10 days is deemed granted unless the court orders otherwise (CRC 3.1320(g)). In response to demurrers, and before the case is at issue, a complaint may not be amended more than three times without an offer of curative facts (CCP 430.41(e)(1)).
- **How changes are shown.** A complete fresh amended pleading, serially numbered, plus the page, paragraph and line schedule; no colour conventions.

## Formal rules for papers

- **Font and margins.** At least 12-point (CRC 2.104); a font essentially equivalent to Courier, Times New Roman or Arial (2.105); left margin at least one inch, right margin at least half an inch (2.107).
- **Pleading paper (CRC 2.108).** Lines one and a half or double spaced and numbered consecutively; line numbers at the left margin, separated from the text by at least 1/5 inch of space or a vertical line; numbering starts at 1 on each page; at least three line numbers per vertical inch. Footnotes and quotations may be single-spaced.
- **Page numbers and footer.** Pages numbered consecutively at the bottom in Arabic numerals (2.109); a footer below the page number, divided by a printed line, with the title of the paper in at least 10-point (2.110).
- **First page (CRC 2.111).** From line 1, left of centre: the attorney's name, office address, telephone, fax, e-mail and State Bar number. Lines 1 to 7, right of centre: blank for the clerk. On line 8, at or below 3⅓ inches: the title of the court. Below it, left: the title of the case (each party on a separate line in an initial complaint). Opposite, right: the case number; below it the nature of the paper and, on complaints, the character of the action; then the assigned judge and department.
- **Memorandum length (CRC 3.1113(d)).** Opening or responding memorandum 15 pages; on summary judgment or summary adjudication 20 pages; reply 10 pages; excluding the caption page, notice of motion, exhibits, declarations, attachments, tables and proof of service. Above 10 pages, a table of contents and a table of authorities; above 15 pages, an opening summary of argument (3.1113(f)). Leave for a longer memorandum is sought ex parte on written notice at least 24 hours before it is due (3.1113(e)); an oversize memorandum is treated as a late-filed paper (3.1113(g)). California uses page limits, not word counts.
- **San Francisco.** LRSF 2.7(A) defers to the CRC and requires barcodes to be redacted from conventionally filed documents. Paper courtesy copies, tabbed and bound, are required for any document needing court review, action or signature; for papers e-filed 7 or more court days before the hearing they are lodged within 2 court days of e-filing (LRSF 2.7(B)).

## Applications and motions

- **The papers (CRC 3.1112(a)).** A notice of hearing, the motion, and a memorandum. The notice states in its opening paragraph the nature of the order sought and the grounds (3.1110(a)); the first page shows, below the case number, the hearing date, time and location, the judge, the date the action was filed, and the trial date if set (3.1110(b)). Exhibits have an index, and electronic exhibits bookmarks (3.1110(f)). Exhibit references give the exhibit, page and, where applicable, paragraph or line (3.1113(k)). A proposed order is lodged and served with the papers, not attached (3.1113(m)).
- **The memorandum (CRC 3.1113(b)).** A statement of facts, a concise statement of the law, the evidence and arguments relied on, and a discussion of the authorities.
- **Demurrer.** Before filing, meet and confer in person, by telephone or by video at least 5 days before the responsive pleading is due, identifying each cause of action challenged and the legal basis of each deficiency; if that cannot happen in time, an automatic 30-day extension follows. File with the demurrer a declaration stating how the parties met and conferred and that they did not agree, or that the other side did not respond (CCP 430.41(a)). Each ground goes in a separate paragraph and says whether it goes to the whole pleading or to specified causes of action (CRC 3.1320(a)); the first page names the demurring party and the party whose pleading is challenged (3.1320(e)); the hearing is set within 35 days of filing or the first available date (3.1320(d)).
- **Motion to strike.** The same meet-and-confer duty (CCP 435.5(a)). Strikes irrelevant, false or improper matter, or matter not drawn in conformity with law, a court rule or an order (CCP 436). The notice quotes in full the words to be struck, unless a whole paragraph, cause of action, count or defense is attacked, with numbered specifications (CRC 3.1322(a)); it is heard with any demurrer (3.1322(b)).
- **Summary judgment (CCP 437c).** May be made 60 days after the general appearance of each party against whom it is directed; notice and papers served at least 81 days before the hearing; heard at least 30 days before trial; one motion per adverse party (summary adjudication excepted). The motion is supported by declarations and other evidence and a separate statement of the material facts said to be undisputed, each followed by the supporting evidence. The opposition, filed at least 20 days before the hearing, has its own separate statement responding to each fact; the reply, at least 11 days before, adds no new evidence. The opponent may not rest on its pleadings (437c(p)).
- **The separate statement (CRC 3.1350).** Two columns: on the left, the moving party's undisputed material facts in numbered sequence, each with its evidence cited by exhibit, title, page and line; on the right, the opposing party's response ("Undisputed." or "Disputed." with evidence). Issues for summary adjudication are stated in the notice and repeated verbatim in the separate statement.
- **San Francisco tentative rulings (LRSF 8.3; CRC 3.1308).** Tentative rulings are generally available by 3:00 p.m. the day before the hearing. A party intending to appear notifies the other parties and the court by 4:00 p.m. that day, stating without argument the parts contested, by the e-mail route the rule gives. A party who does not appear submits to the tentative. The prevailing party prepares a proposed order repeating the substantive part of the tentative verbatim and brings it to the hearing.

## Citation style

CRC 1.200: the California Style Manual or the Bluebook, at the filer's option, used consistently. CRC 3.1113(c): a case citation includes the official report volume and page and the year, and the court may not require more. The bracketed items show the shape of a citation only; in a draft every authority comes from the user (see `authorities.md`).

- Supreme Court, Style Manual form: `([Case name] ([year]) [volume] Cal.5th [page], [pin])`; the year in parentheses after the case name; "Cal.5th" without a space; the case name italicised in full, "v." included.
- Court of Appeal: `([Case name] ([year]) [volume] Cal.App.5th [page], [pin])`.
- Parallel citations, if given, follow in square brackets; they are optional in a trial-court memorandum.
- Short forms: adopt a short name in parentheses after the first citation if needed, then `([Case name], supra, [volume] Cal.5th at p. [pin])`.
- Statutes in parentheses: "(Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (c).)"; in running text "Code of Civil Procedure section 437c". After a footnote saying that further references are to the Code of Civil Procedure unless stated, "(§ 437c, subd. (c).)"; only one code may be treated so in a document.
- Rules of court in this profile are abbreviated "CRC"; the Style Manual form for citing them was not researched.

## Forms that exist only here

- **Pleading paper** with numbered lines and a footer on every page.
- **The demurrer**, general or special, with its meet-and-confer declaration, and the **motion to strike** that quotes the words to be struck.
- **The cross-complaint** in place of a counterclaim, and the compulsory cross-complaint rule.
- **The general denial** against an unverified complaint.
- **The CRC 2.112 cause-of-action heading** naming number, nature, by whom and against whom.
- **The CRC 3.1324 amendment schedule** and declaration.
- **The separate statement** of undisputed material facts, in two columns.
- **Doe defendants** and the statement of damages in personal injury actions.
- **Judicial Council form pleadings.**
- **The tentative-ruling procedure** and the proposed order that mirrors it (San Francisco).

## Background: where the standards come from

Background only. Cite these in a draft only if the user supplies or verifies them.

- *Lazar v. Superior Court* (1996) 12 Cal.4th 631, 645: fraud must be pleaded specifically, with the facts showing how, when, where, to whom and by what means; against a corporate employer, the speaker's name and authority and what was said, to whom and when.
- *Blank v. Kirwan* (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318: a demurrer admits the material facts properly pleaded but not contentions or conclusions; where a demurrer is sustained without leave, the question is whether there is a reasonable possibility of curing the defect by amendment, and the plaintiff bears that burden.
- The summary judgment burdens are statutory (CCP 437c(p)).
