# Variant `demand`: letter before action or demand

## What it is for

An open letter, before proceedings, that tells the other side what the client claims, demands payment or performance by a date, and says what will follow if it is not met. It does three jobs: it may resolve the matter; it shows the court later that the other side had fair warning and a chance to pay (which bears on costs and on any argument that proceedings were premature); and it may be a condition of the claim, where a contract or statute requires notice or a demand before suit.

This variant is built from general practice and the rules, not from a precedent. Keep it conservative: state the claim in a few sentences, demand only what the client is owed on the user's figures, and threaten only the step the client has authorised.

It does not draft a statutory demand in insolvency, a notice under a contract's own notice-and-cure clause that prescribes its wording, or a pre-action notice whose form a statute prescribes. Those have prescribed content: ask for the form, or follow the clause word for word.

## Structure (Hong Kong)

1. **Header.** References, date, mode of delivery, addressee (the other side's solicitors if known; otherwise the party, at the address any contract notice clause specifies), "Attn:", salutation. The letter is open: no status heading.
2. **"Re:" line.** The parties ("[name] ("our client")" and "[name] ("your client")"), and the subject ("Claim for [sum] under [document title] dated [date]").
3. **Who we act for.** "We act for [name] ("our client")." If writing to the party direct, say so plainly and suggest they pass the letter to their lawyers.
4. **The claim, briefly.** In numbered paragraphs: the agreement or relationship, the obligation, what was not done and when, and the sum or performance due, each fact with the document it comes from. No case law and no argument.
5. **The demand.** What is demanded, the sum (from the user's figures), any contractual or statutory interest at `[rate]` under `[statutory provision]` or the clause the user gives, and the date: "on or before [date]".
6. **The consequence.** That proceedings will be commenced without further notice if the demand is not met, and that the client will seek its costs. Only if instructed.
7. **Practical requests.** Whether their solicitors have instructions to accept service; that documents relevant to the dispute be preserved; any pre-action step a practice direction requires for this kind of claim (Hong Kong has no general pre-action protocol: check for one that applies, `[rule]`); any mediation proposal (better made in an open letter, so it can be shown on costs).
8. **Reservation of rights.**
9. **Sign-off.** "Yours faithfully," then the firm (or "Yours sincerely," to a named addressee).

## Differences for the US profiles

- **Delivery.** By the method any contract notice clause requires; otherwise a traceable method ("VIA EMAIL ([address]) AND CERTIFIED MAIL, RETURN RECEIPT REQUESTED"), so receipt can be proved.
- **Opening.** "This firm represents [name] in connection with …". "We" throughout; a letter signed by one attorney may use "I" sparingly.
- **Pre-suit requirements.** Some claims need a notice or demand before suit, with content and timing set by statute or contract (claims against public bodies, some consumer and insurance claims, a pre-suit demand in a Delaware derivative claim). Mark each `[statutory provision]` and ask the user to confirm it before the letter goes.
- **Consumer debts.** A letter that collects a consumer debt may be governed by federal and state debt-collection statutes that prescribe content and forbid some statements. If the claim is a consumer debt, flag this to the user before drafting.
- **Preservation.** A demand that litigation is reasonably anticipated, and that relevant documents and electronically stored information be preserved, is common and useful.
- **Settlement terms.** Keep any compromise out of the demand. Put it in a separate letter under the `proposal` variant, marked as a settlement communication (FRE 408 or the state equivalent), so the demand itself stays usable.
- **Reservation.** "Nothing in this letter waives any of [name]'s rights or remedies, all of which are expressly reserved."
- **Sign-off.** "Very truly yours," or "Sincerely," then `[counsel-name]` and the firm.

## Skeleton (Hong Kong)

The ellipses mark the drafter's own words. Bracketed labels are fields from `references/fields.md`.

```
Our Ref.: [number 1]
Your Ref.: [number 2]
Date: [date 1]

BY EMAIL ([address 1]) AND BY POST

[solicitors-firm 1]
[address 2]

Attn: [name 1]

Dear Sirs,

Re: [name 2] ("our client") and [name 3] ("your client")
Claim for [sum 1] under [document title] dated [date 2] ("the Agreement")

1. We act for our client.

2. By the Agreement, your client agreed to … (clause [number 3]).

3. Our client … on [date 3]. Your client has not paid the sum of [sum 1], which fell due on [date 4] under clause [number 4] of the Agreement.

4. Our client demands that your client pay the sum of [sum 1], together with interest at [rate] from [date 4] under [statutory provision], on or before [date 5].

5. Failing which, our client will commence proceedings against your client without further notice, and will seek the costs of those proceedings.

6. Please confirm whether you have instructions to accept service of proceedings on behalf of your client.

7. Your client should preserve all documents, including electronic records, relating to the Agreement and the matters in this letter.

8. All our client's rights are hereby expressly reserved.

Yours faithfully,

[solicitors-firm 2]

c.c. Client
```

## Checklist

What the checker tests:

- a sign-off at the end and a "Re:" line near the start (soft);
- a deadline ("on or before", "within … days", "by [date]");
- the consequence of not complying, and a reservation of rights (soft);
- no without prejudice heading (soft: a letter before action is normally open);
- no "It is submitted", and no threat of a criminal complaint (soft).

What the drafter checks by eye:

- every fact and sum is from the user's documents and figures, and the interest provision is the one the user gave;
- the consequence is the step the client has authorised, and nothing more;
- any contract notice clause has been followed for address, method and content;
- the letter can be shown to the judge as it stands;
- any settlement offer has been moved to a separate letter.
