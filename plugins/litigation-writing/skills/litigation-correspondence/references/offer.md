# Variant `offer`: a rule-based offer

## What it is for

A formal offer made under a rule of court, so that the rule's consequences follow if it is refused and the offeror does as well or better at trial: in Hong Kong, a sanctioned offer under O.22 of the Rules of the High Court or the Rules of the District Court; in the US, an offer of judgment under FRCP 68 or the state equivalent. The consequences (costs, and in Hong Kong enhanced interest) attach only if the offer complies with the rule, so the form matters more here than in any other letter.

This variant is built from the rules and general practice, not from a precedent. It is conservative by design: it says only what the rule requires, it says nothing the rule does not, and every rule reference is for the user to check against the current text before the offer is served. An offer that fails the rule is, at best, an ordinary without prejudice save as to costs offer.

## Hong Kong: sanctioned offer under O.22

**Marking and title.** "WITHOUT PREJUDICE SAVE AS TO COSTS" at the top, then "SANCTIONED OFFER PURSUANT TO ORDER 22 OF THE RULES OF THE HIGH COURT (CAP. 4A)" (or "OF THE RULES OF THE DISTRICT COURT (CAP. 336H)").

**What the offer must state.** Check each against the current text of O.22 and list any the user has not confirmed in "FIELDS TO COMPLETE":

1. that it is a sanctioned offer made under Order 22;
2. whether it relates to the whole of the claim, to part of it, or to an issue in it, and if so which;
3. whether it takes into account any counterclaim;
4. the period for which it is open for acceptance, which must be at least the period the rule fixes (`[period]`);
5. whether the sum offered is inclusive of interest and, if not, the interest offered; check what the rule presumes;
6. the terms: the sum, or the non-money terms, exactly as the client has authorised.

**Points to check before it goes.**

- **Sanctioned payment.** Where a defendant offers to pay money, check whether the rule requires the offer to be made by a sanctioned payment into court rather than (or as well as) a sanctioned offer. An offer in the wrong form may not carry the rule's consequences.
- **Before proceedings.** If no action has begun, check whether and on what terms the rule allows a sanctioned offer to be made.
- **Costs on acceptance.** Check what the rule provides for costs if the offer is accepted, and say nothing in the letter that contradicts it.
- **Secrecy.** The rule restricts telling the trial judge about the offer until liability and quantum are decided. Never mention it in an open letter, an affirmation or submissions before then.
- **Acceptance.** Check how acceptance must be given (written notice of acceptance served on the offeror) and say so in the letter.

## US: offer of judgment

**FRCP 68 (federal court).** Read the current text and the profile. In outline: only a party defending against a claim may serve it; it must be served at least 14 days before the date set for trial; it offers to allow judgment on specified terms, with the costs then accrued; the other side has 14 days after service to accept in writing, after which either party may file the offer, the notice of acceptance and proof of service and the clerk enters judgment; an unaccepted offer is treated as withdrawn and is not admissible except in a proceeding to determine costs; and if the judgment finally obtained is not more favourable than the unaccepted offer, the offeree must pay the costs incurred after the offer was made.

**Form.** A captioned paper, served on counsel and not filed unless accepted: the caption; "[Defendant]'S OFFER OF JUDGMENT PURSUANT TO FED. R. CIV. P. 68"; the offer; the date; the signature block; a certificate of service. Serve it with a short covering letter.

**Drafting points.**

- State the sum and say expressly whether it includes costs accrued to date, and whether it includes attorney's fees. Where the statute under which the claim is brought treats fees as part of costs, the wording can cap or exclude them (`[statutory provision]`). Ask the user.
- Identify which claims the judgment covers, and against which defendant.
- Add that the offer is made for the purposes of the rule and is not an admission of liability.
- A plaintiff cannot use Rule 68. A plaintiff's settlement offer goes under the `proposal` variant.

**State equivalents.** Each is named here to be checked, not relied on; none is verified in this skill. Mark the rule `[rule]` in the draft until the user confirms it:

- New York: an offer to compromise under CPLR 3221, and a conditional offer to liquidate damages under CPLR 3220;
- California: an offer under Code of Civil Procedure § 998, which either party may make and which needs a provision for acceptance in writing;
- Washington: CR 68, and the offers of settlement statute for smaller damages claims (RCW 4.84.250 and following);
- Delaware Superior Court: Civil Rule 68, and any statutory interest rule tied to a written settlement demand;
- Delaware Court of Chancery: check whether the Court of Chancery Rules provide an offer of judgment; if not, use a settlement proposal.

## Skeleton (Hong Kong)

The ellipses mark the drafter's own words. Bracketed labels are fields from `references/fields.md`.

```
WITHOUT PREJUDICE SAVE AS TO COSTS

Our Ref.: [number 1]
Your Ref.: [number 2]
Date: [date 1]

BY EMAIL ([address 1]) AND BY HAND

[solicitors-firm 1]
[address 2]

Attn: [name 1]

Dear Sirs,

Re: [case number] ("the Action")
[Plaintiff]: [name 2]
[Defendant]: [name 3] ("our client")
SANCTIONED OFFER PURSUANT TO ORDER 22 OF THE RULES OF THE HIGH COURT (CAP. 4A)

1. We are instructed to make the following sanctioned offer on behalf of our client under Order 22 of the Rules of the High Court.

2. The offer relates to the whole of your client's claim in the Action, and takes into account our client's counterclaim.

3. Our client offers to pay your client the sum of [sum 1] in settlement of that claim, inclusive of interest.

4. The offer is open for acceptance for [period] from the date of service of this letter, that is until [date 2].

5. Acceptance must be given by written notice of acceptance served on this firm.

6. The offer is made without any admission of liability.

Yours faithfully,

[solicitors-firm 2]
```

Paragraphs 2 and 3 show one set of choices. Change them to match the client's instructions: an offer on part of the claim or an issue says which; an offer that ignores the counterclaim says so; a sum exclusive of interest states the interest offered. For a defendant's money offer, check first whether a sanctioned payment is needed.

## Skeleton (US, FRCP 68)

```
[court]

[Plaintiff],
        Plaintiff,
    v.                                  Case No. [case number]
[Defendant],
        Defendant.

[Defendant]'S OFFER OF JUDGMENT PURSUANT TO FED. R. CIV. P. 68

Pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 68, [Defendant] offers to allow judgment to be taken against it in this action in favor of [Plaintiff] in the amount of [sum 1], plus costs accrued to the date of this offer. …

This offer is made for the purposes of Rule 68 only and is not an admission of liability.

If [Plaintiff] does not accept this offer in writing within fourteen (14) days after service, it is deemed withdrawn.

Dated: [date 1]

[counsel-name]
[solicitors-firm]
[address]
Attorneys for [Defendant]

CERTIFICATE OF SERVICE
…
```

The sum is shown "plus costs accrued". If the client's offer is to be inclusive of costs (and, where the governing statute treats fees as costs, of fees), say so in those words instead.

## Checklist

What the checker tests:

- Hong Kong: a reference to Order 22, the words "sanctioned offer" or "sanctioned payment", and the "WITHOUT PREJUDICE SAVE AS TO COSTS" heading (soft);
- US: a reference to Rule 68, § 998, CPLR 3221, CR 68, or `[rule]`; "offer of judgment" or "offer to compromise" (soft);
- an acceptance period or deadline;
- Hong Kong: a sign-off at the end; US: a sign-off or the attorneys' signature block.

What the drafter checks by eye:

- every element the rule requires is present, in the rule's terms, and the user has confirmed the current text;
- the offeror is a party the rule allows to make it, and the timing is within the rule;
- the sum and terms are exactly as the client authorised, including interest, costs and fees;
- the choices in the skeleton (whole claim or part, counterclaim, interest, costs) have been set to the client's instructions;
- the offer is not mentioned in any open document or court paper.
