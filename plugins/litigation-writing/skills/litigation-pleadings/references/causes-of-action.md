# Causes of action: the module library

The claim engine in `claim.md` gives the frame (caption, parties, background, prayer, formal parts). Each module below supplies the body for one cause of action: the paragraphs in the order they are pleaded, and the particulars that must be there. A counterclaim uses the same modules.

How to use the modules:

- **Elements are the governing law's.** Each sequence follows the usual common-law shape. The user decides which law governs and what its elements are; where the user's law adds or removes an element, follow the user. Never cite a case for an element; a pleading carries none.
- **One module per cause, primary first.** Plead the cause the party chiefly relies on first. Plead each alternative expressly as one ("Further or alternatively,"), in its own paragraphs, and only where there are reasonable grounds for it (HK O.18 r.12A; US counts may be inconsistent, FRCP 8(d)(3) and the state equivalents). Where alternatives rest on the same facts, plead the facts once and refer back to them by paragraph number.
- **One set of facts, many assets.** Where the same cause is pleaded for several assets or payments, give each asset its own section with a factual heading ("The Purchase of the Property", "The Second Advance") and run the same sequence in each. Check each section's nouns, sums and dates after drafting: copy-paste nouns are the commonest slip in this kind of pleading.
- **Particulars in the claim, not later.** Where the rules require particulars (fraud, misrepresentation, breach of trust, wilful default, undue influence, any state of mind, special damage), plead them under a heading in capitals, as lettered or numbered sub-paragraphs. In Hong Kong, particulars of debt or damage longer than three folios go in a separate schedule that the pleading refers to (O.18 r.12(2)).
- **Do not anticipate the defence.** Do not plead against a defence the other side has not raised (change of position, limitation, illegality). Answer it in the reply. The exception is a fact that takes the claim out of a limitation period (an acknowledgment, a part payment), which may be pleaded in the claim.
- **US differences.** Each US count opens by incorporating the earlier paragraphs, carries its own elements, and ends with a short damages or relief paragraph. Plead facts, not element labels: the federal plausibility standard disregards bare conclusions, and the other profiles state their own standard. Use the profile for which facts must be pleaded with particularity.

Text in braces `{...}` is a drafting note.

## 1. Debt and breach of contract

**Fits** a claim for the price or another fixed sum due under a contract (debt), or for damages for breach of any term.

**Sequence (debt):**

1. The parties, and the claimant's business where it explains the contract.
2. The agreement: date, parties, subject matter (with a defined term), price.
3. The form of the agreement. Written: identify the document by title and date and say who signed it. Oral: what was said, by whom and to whom, and the date and place. Partly written and partly oral: say which terms are in which. By conduct: the conduct, generally described.
4. The terms relied on, clause by clause, each ending "(see Clause [number])": the obligation to pay, the due date or the event that makes payment due, any acceleration clause, any interest clause with its rate, any indemnity-costs clause.
5. Performance by the claimant: delivery, services rendered, or completion of whatever made the sum due, with dates. In Hong Kong, do not plead performance of conditions precedent; that is implied (O.18 r.7(4)). In US federal practice a general averment that all conditions precedent have occurred is usual (FRCP 9(c)).
6. The due date and non-payment: "the Defendant failed to pay the Price or any part of it".
7. Demand: the date and form of each demand, and the response or "No reply was received."
8. The sum due, stated once and used identically in the prayer.
9. Interest: the contractual clause, rate and period, or `[statutory provision]`, or both in the alternative.

**Sequence (breach and damages):** steps 1 to 4 as above, then:

5. Implied terms, with the facts they arise from (module 2).
6. "The Plaintiff will refer to the Agreement at the trial of this action for its full terms, true meaning and effect."
7. The claimant's performance, or readiness and willingness to perform. Where specific performance is claimed, itemise what the claimant has done and is ready to do.
8. Breach, stated as a conclusion naming the clauses ("In breach of Clauses [number 1] and [number 2] of the Agreement"), then **PARTICULARS OF BREACH**, one failure to a sub-paragraph, each with its clause and date.
9. Consequences: termination or acceptance of a repudiation (the letter and its date), the other side's refusal to perform, any acceleration.
10. Loss: "By reason of the matters aforesaid, the Plaintiff has suffered loss and damage." Then **PARTICULARS OF LOSS AND DAMAGE**: each head (for example a shortfall in value, lost profit, money spent in vain, the cost of a substitute) with its figure or "to be assessed", and the facts that let any calculation be made. Special damage is always particularised (HK O.18 r.12; FRCP 9(g)).
11. Interest.

**Particulars that must be there:** the date and form of the agreement; each term relied on with its clause; each breach with its clause and date; each head of special damage with its figure; the basis, rate, sum and period of interest.

**Prayer:** the sum (debt), or damages to be assessed; specific performance, with damages in lieu or in addition; any declaration (for example that the agreement was validly terminated); interest; costs.

**US:** a breach of contract count pleads the contract, the plaintiff's performance (or the general conditions-precedent averment), the breach and the damage. New York requires the corporate status of a corporate party (CPLR 3015(b)); a contract or other writing attached to a US pleading becomes part of it. California requires the amount demanded to be stated (CCP 425.10); Delaware Superior Court forbids a figure for unliquidated damages (Rule 9(g)).

**Flags:** a sum in the prayer that differs from the body; an interest period that starts on one date in the body and another in the prayer; an indemnity-costs prayer with no pleaded indemnity clause.

## 2. Implied terms

**Fits** wherever the claim depends on a term the written agreement does not state.

**Sequence:**

1. The term, stated in words precise enough to be breached: "It was an implied term of the Agreement that the Defendant would {obligation} within a reasonable time of [date]." {state the obligation in full}
2. The basis: implied in fact (because the agreement would not work without it, or because it is so obvious it goes without saying); implied by `[statutory provision]`; implied by custom or by a course of dealing between the parties.
3. The facts from which it arises, as lettered sub-paragraphs: what each party knew of the purpose; the nature of the transaction; the earlier dealings (dates and what happened); the custom (the market and the practice). This step is the one most often missing, and without it the term is pleaded by label only.
4. The breach of the implied term, particularised separately from any breach of the express terms.

**Particulars that must be there:** the term in words; its basis; the facts from which it is implied (HK O.18 r.12; the facts behind an implied contract are described generally).

**Flags:** "By reason of necessity and/or business efficacy" with nothing after it; an implied term that contradicts an express one; a statutory term with no provision named (use `[statutory provision]` and list it).

## 3. Misrepresentation: fraudulent and negligent

**Fits** a claim that the claimant was induced to contract, or to pay or act, by a false statement of fact.

**Sequence:**

1. The parties and background: who owned or sold what, and how they came into contact.
2. The approach or negotiations that led to the statements.
3. The representations, each numbered and defined ("the First Representation"), and each stating: the medium (which document, by title and date, or which conversation); who made it, and for a company, through whom and with what authority; to whom; when and where; and its content. Precise words only where they matter; otherwise the substance. An implied representation is pleaded as implied, with the conduct or words it is implied from.
4. Reliance: that each representation induced the claimant to contract, to pay or to act as it did.
5. Falsity, as a parallel numbered list: for each representation, the true position. Head it **PARTICULARS OF FALSITY**.
6. Fraud (module 4): that the maker knew each representation was false, or made it recklessly, not caring whether it was true or false, followed by the primary facts from which knowledge is inferred.
7. Alternatively, negligence: the facts giving rise to a duty of care in making the statement (special relationship, assumption of responsibility, knowledge that the claimant would rely), that the maker had no reasonable grounds for believing the statement true, with particulars of why. Or, where the governing law has a statutory claim, plead it under `[statutory provision]` in a short alternative paragraph.
8. Discovery of the truth and rescission: when the claimant learned the truth, and the letter (date) by which it rescinded, or the claim to rescind.
9. Loss and damage, with particulars: the price paid against the true value, sums lost, expenses incurred in reliance.
10. Interest.

**Particulars that must be there:** for every representation, the maker, the recipient, the date, the place or medium (identifying any document) and the content; for every representation, why it was false; for fraud, actual knowledge or recklessness and the primary facts that show it.

**Prayer:** a declaration that the contract was validly rescinded, or rescission; the return of the price paid; damages in deceit; or, in the alternative, damages for negligent misrepresentation or under `[statutory provision]`; interest; costs.

**US:** fraud is pleaded with particularity in every profile (FRCP 9(b); CPLR 3016(b) "stated in detail"; California requires the how, when, where and to whom of each representation and the means used, and for a company the speaker's name and authority). Delaware Superior Court also requires particularity for negligence. Negligent misrepresentation is a separate count with its own elements under the governing law.

**Flags:** a rolled-up plea that the maker "knew or ought to have known" the statement was false (it pleads constructive knowledge and will not carry fraud); representations defined in layers but then referred to inconsistently; a representation of intention or opinion pleaded as one of existing fact without the facts that make it so.

## 4. Fraud and dishonesty particulars

**Fits** every allegation of fraud, deceit, dishonesty, conspiracy to injure by unlawful means, dishonest assistance, knowing receipt, bad faith or concealment, in any module.

**Before drafting:** confirm that the user has clear instructions to allege fraud and material that a responsible advocate could base the allegation on. If either is missing, say so and do not draft the plea. A Hong Kong amendment that pleads fraud for the first time needs a supporting affirmation (see `amendment.md`).

**Sequence:**

1. A conclusory paragraph that charges dishonesty in terms nobody can mistake: "The Defendant acted dishonestly in that ...". Avoid equivocal words ("wilfully", "improperly", a bare "recklessly") where dishonesty is meant.
2. **PARTICULARS OF FRAUD** (or OF DISHONESTY, OF KNOWLEDGE): the primary facts, numbered, each with who, what, when, where and how. Facts that are equally consistent with honesty do not carry the plea; there must be facts that make dishonesty more likely than innocence or negligence.
3. Knowledge: what the defendant actually knew, and the grounds of that knowledge (the document they held and when, the meeting they attended, the account they controlled). Plead actual knowledge or recklessness. Never "knew or ought to have known", never "knew or should have known": those plead constructive knowledge. Where blind-eye knowledge is relied on, plead the facts that made the defendant suspicious and the deliberate decision not to inquire.
4. The inference: a short paragraph stating what the defendant must have known and intended, drawn from the facts above.
5. Where a conviction is relied on (HK O.18 r.7A): the conviction, its date, the court, and the issue in the action to which it is relevant. A foreign conviction is not pleaded under this rule.
6. For knowing receipt: the disposal of the claimant's assets in breach of trust or fiduciary duty, the defendant's beneficial receipt of assets traceable to them, and the defendant's knowledge. For dishonest assistance: the breach, each act of assistance, and the dishonesty with its particulars.
7. Bad faith: the conduct, and what the other party knew when it acted.

**Flags:** dishonesty alleged against a party with no primary facts; knowledge alleged with no grounds; "recklessly" used alone, without saying that the maker did not care whether the statement was true or false; fraud pleaded against several defendants without saying which acts were whose.

## 5. Breach of fiduciary duty and trust

**Fits** claims against a trustee, director, agent, partner or anyone who has undertaken to act for the claimant, and proprietary claims to property held by the defendant: resulting trust, common-intention constructive trust, proprietary estoppel, and purpose trusts. The sequence for each is given separately; where several are pleaded for one asset, plead the facts once and each route as an alternative.

### 5.1 Fiduciary relationship and breach

1. The relationship and the facts that create it: the office and its period (director from [date 1] to [date 2]), the agency or retainer and its terms, or the facts of the undertaking to act in the claimant's interest.
2. The duties relied on, stated specifically: to act in good faith in the claimant's interest, not to place himself in a position of conflict, not to profit from the position without consent, to account. Where a statute defines a director's duties, name it as `[statutory provision]` unless the user gives the section.
3. The property or opportunity received in that capacity: what, when, how much.
4. Each breach, separately, with **PARTICULARS OF BREACH OF FIDUCIARY DUTY** (or OF BREACH OF TRUST): the transaction, its date and amount, what was done with the money or asset, and the want of consent. Where the breach is a long run of payments, give their count and total in the paragraph and list each one (date, amount, paying account, receiving account) in a schedule.
5. Knowledge where it matters (a third party, or a breach that turns on what the fiduciary knew), with particulars (module 4).
6. Loss, or the profit made, quantified or to be found on an account; "in amounts presently unknown to the Plaintiff" where that is true.
7. The liability to account, and the claim to trace into identified assets.
8. Interest, with compound interest under the equitable jurisdiction pleaded expressly.

**Prayer:** a declaration of the breach; an account of profits, or an account on the footing of wilful default (only with its own particulars); payment of all sums found due; equitable compensation; a declaration of trust over traceable assets and an order for their transfer; an injunction preserving them; interest; costs.

### 5.2 Resulting trust (purchase in another's name)

1. The asset, fully described (`[property description]`), the date of purchase and the price.
2. The registered or legal owner.
3. Who paid what towards the purchase: each payment's date, sum, payer, payee and method. Stated in a schedule where there are many.
4. Who paid the outgoings and mortgage instalments, if relied on.
5. The absence of any intention to make a gift, and the facts that show it (the claimant kept the documents, the parties' discussions, the defendant's own conduct). Plead facts, not just the label.
6. The defendant's knowledge of, and consent to, the payments and the arrangement.
7. The conclusion: the defendant holds the asset on resulting trust for the claimant, in the share that matches the contribution "or in such share as the Court may determine".

### 5.3 Common-intention constructive trust

1. The asset and its legal ownership (as 5.2).
2. The common intention: the express discussions that formed it (what was said, by whom and to whom, and the date and place), or the conduct from which it is inferred. An oral agreement is pleaded with its terms, numbered.
3. Detrimental reliance: the acts the claimant did in reliance, each separately (payments, forgoing registration, contributions in money or work), with dates and sums.
4. That it would be unconscionable for the defendant to deny the claimant's interest.
5. The share claimed, and the fallback "or in such share as the Court may determine".

### 5.4 Proprietary estoppel

1. The assurance by the owner: its content, the words or conduct, who gave it, to whom, when and where. Give it a defined term ("the Assurance").
2. Reliance: that the claimant acted because of the Assurance.
3. Detriment: each act, with its date and value, under **PARTICULARS OF DETRIMENT**.
4. That it would be unconscionable for the owner to go back on the Assurance.
5. The relief that would satisfy the equity: the interest promised, a transfer, or a sum.

### 5.5 Purpose trust (money paid for a stated purpose)

1. The payment: date, sum, method, payee.
2. The agreed purpose, and that the money was not to become part of the recipient's general assets but was to be used only for that purpose (with its defined term, "the Purpose"), and returned if it was not.
3. What the recipient did with the money instead, and the want of consent.
4. The claim: the money is held on trust for the claimant, and its traceable proceeds with it.

**Particulars that must be there (all of 5):** each breach of trust or duty separately; each payment or contribution with date, sum and method; the content, maker and date of every assurance or discussion relied on; each act of detriment; the share claimed with the court-determined fallback; the defendant's knowledge of the payments or the arrangement where the claim relies on it.

**US:** breach of fiduciary duty is a separate count; in the Court of Chancery the complaint is verified (Rule 3(c)), and a derivative claim pleads demand or the reasons for not making one with particularity (Rule 23.1). Constructive trust and accounting are usually pleaded as remedies in the prayer, or as counts where the profile's law treats them as causes of action; ask the user.

**Flags:** every doctrine stacked on every asset without facts for each; "a trust" pleaded without its type, date and how it arose; a declaration sought in the body but missing from the prayer; the wrong party named as beneficiary in a cloned section ("on trust for the Defendant" in the plaintiff's claim).

## 6. Unjust enrichment and money had and received

**Fits** recovery of a payment or benefit where there is no contract to sue on: mistaken payments, failure of basis, payments induced by a third party's fraud, and the alternative count to a contract or account claim.

**Sequence:**

1. The payment or benefit: date, sum, payer, payee, method; for a non-money benefit, what it was, how it was conferred and its value.
2. The defendant's enrichment, and that it was at the claimant's expense. Where the enrichment came through a third party or a composite transaction, the facts that link the claimant's loss to the defendant's gain.
3. The unjust factor, named and with its facts: the mistake and what the claimant believed (lettered sub-paragraphs); or the basis on which the payment was made and how it failed; or the duress or other factor.
4. Where a third party's fraud procured the payment: that fraud, pleaded under module 4, including any conviction relied on.
5. Discovery and demand: when the claimant found out, the demand (date and form), and the failure to repay.
6. The entitlement: "the Defendant is liable to repay the sum of [sum] to the Plaintiff".
7. Further or alternatively, money had and received: the defendant received [sum] to the use of the claimant and is liable to repay it.
8. Where a proprietary claim is made: the facts that make the money or its proceeds identifiable, the claim that it is held on constructive trust for the claimant (check the beneficiary is the claimant), and the claim to trace.
9. Interest.

**Particulars that must be there:** the payment in full; facts for each element and the unjust factor (HK O.18 r.12 requires facts satisfying each element); the demand.

**Prayer:** the sum; a declaration of constructive trust over the sum and its traceable proceeds; a declaration of entitlement to trace; an injunction until the sum is paid over; delivery up; equitable compensation; interest; costs.

**US:** unjust enrichment and money had and received are pleaded as separate counts where the governing law recognises them, often in the alternative to a contract count; some laws bar the claim where a valid contract covers the subject, so plead it expressly in the alternative.

**Flags:** the unjust factor not named; enrichment "at the expense of" the claimant asserted where the money came from a third party, with no linking facts; change of position or good-faith purchase anticipated in the claim (answer it in the reply).

## 7. Loan

**Fits** recovery of money lent, including a series of advances, and the alternative to a gift alleged by the other side.

**Sequence:**

1. The agreement to lend: date, parties, oral or written (identifying the document), the sum or facility, the purpose if agreed, and the terms of repayment (on demand, by a date, by instalments) and of interest.
2. Each advance: date, sum, method, payee (where paid to a third party on the borrower's behalf, say so), and any currency conversion, giving the rate or the equivalent "at the material time".
3. The aggregate, stated once and used identically in the prayer.
4. Any repayment, with dates and sums, and the balance.
5. Demand: date and form, where repayment is on demand; or the due date passing.
6. Failure to repay: "the Defendant has failed to repay the said sum or any part of it", and the defendant "remains indebted to the Plaintiff in the sum of [sum]".
7. Further or alternatively, the payments were made for a purpose or on a basis that failed (module 6), or are held on a purpose trust (module 5.5), pleaded as express alternatives.
8. Interest: the agreed rate, or `[statutory provision]`.

**Particulars that must be there:** every advance with its date, sum and method; the repayment terms; the demand.

**Prayer:** the sum; interest; costs.

**Flags:** totals that do not add up to the listed advances; a currency equivalent with no rate or date; a loan pleaded with no repayment term (say what the parties agreed, or that it was repayable on demand, as the facts are).

## 8. Conversion

**Fits** wrongful interference with goods or other tangible property the claimant owns or has the right to possess: taking, keeping after demand, selling, destroying.

**Sequence:**

1. The goods, identified (`[property description]`: make, model, registration or serial number), and their value.
2. The claimant's title or immediate right to possession, and how it arose.
3. The defendant's act of conversion: what was done, when, and how it denied the claimant's title (refusal after a demand, with the demand's date and form; sale; disposal).
4. Loss: the value at the date of conversion, and any consequential loss with particulars.
5. Interest.

**Prayer:** delivery up of the goods, or their value; damages; interest; costs.

**Flag before drafting:** conversion lies for goods and tangible things. Money in a bank account is a debt owed by the bank, not a thing that can be converted, and many laws, including those of several US states, allow conversion of money only where it is a specific, identifiable fund. Do not plead conversion of a credit balance, or of "funds" paid out of an account, unless the user confirms that the governing law allows it. Raise it with the user instead, and offer the fiduciary, trust or restitution module that fits the facts.
