# Defence: defence, points of defence, answer

## What it is for

The defence tells the court and the plaintiff which facts are in dispute and why. In Hong Kong an allegation that is not traversed is admitted, a denial without reasons is not a traverse, and a defendant who pleads no version of its own cannot call evidence of one at trial. So the defence is built allegation by allegation from the claim, and the defendant's own case is pleaded in it. Affirmative answers (limitation, set-off, payment, estoppel and the rest) take the plaintiff by surprise unless pleaded, so they are pleaded on their own, with their facts.

## Hong Kong

### Before drafting: the response map

Take the claim paragraph by paragraph, and within each paragraph allegation by allegation. Split compound allegations ("entered into and performed the Agreement" is two). For each, record:

- **the answer**: admit, deny or not admit.
- **the reason**: for a denial, why it is wrong; for a non-admission, that the defendant does not know and cannot reasonably find out.
- **the version**: where the defendant's account differs, the facts of that account, or the paragraph where they are pleaded.
- **the affirmative matter**, if the allegation is true but answered by something else (confess and avoid).

Admit what is plainly true and not in the defendant's interest or power to disprove; unreasonable non-admissions cost money. Non-admission is honest only where the defendant genuinely does not know; it is not a way of avoiding a decision on a fact within its knowledge.

### Structure

1. **Title.** "DEFENCE", or "DEFENCE OF THE 2ND DEFENDANT" where defendants plead separately. No writ-issue line.
2. **Conventions.** Two short paragraphs: the abbreviations adopted from the claim, and what paragraph references mean (`formulae.md` section 9).
3. **An opening position, where it saves repetition.** One paragraph stating the defendant's case on the claim as a whole (for example, that the Agreement is admitted but no sum is due because of the payment pleaded below). Optional, factual, never argument.
4. **The traverse, in the claim's order.** One defence paragraph per claim paragraph or range ("As to paragraphs 7 to 9:-"). Use the claim's section headings, or none; do not invent new ones. Each answer uses the forms in `formulae.md` section 10:
   - **Admit**, adding a helpful fact where one exists ("Paragraph 1 is admitted. The Defendant further avers that ...").
   - **Admit with a correction**: "Save that [sum] was received on [date], paragraph 5 is admitted." The correct figure goes inside the answer.
   - **Deny with reasons and the version**: "Paragraph 9 is denied. The true position is that ...", followed by lettered sub-paragraphs of short facts, or a cross-reference to the paragraph where the version is pleaded.
   - **Not admit** with the want of knowledge, adding "and the Plaintiff is put to strict proof thereof" only as an addition.
   - **No answer required** where a paragraph pleads only law or no fact against the defendant. Do not traverse law. A point of law the defendant will take is pleaded expressly in its own paragraph (O.18 r.11), never in a form that reads as a traverse.
   Deny the substance, not the words: add "whether as alleged or at all", "or any part thereof", "or any other sum", so the denial does not track the allegation word for word. A denial of a negative that implies a positive fact pleads that fact.
5. **The defendant's version.** Where it is short, it sits under the answer to the first claim paragraph it concerns, and later answers refer back to it. Where it runs to more than a few paragraphs, give it its own section at that point and cross-refer to it from each answer ("For the reasons pleaded at paragraphs 12 to 18 above, paragraph 21 is denied."). Either way it is pleaded as fact in date order, with defined terms.
6. **Affirmative matter, each in its own paragraphs (O.18 r.8).** After the traverse, under headings that name each matter so the reader can find it ("PAYMENT", "SET-OFF", "LIMITATION", "CHANGE OF POSITION"). Each with its facts and particulars:
   - **limitation**: for each cause of action separately, the accrual date, the expiry date and the provision, `[statutory provision]` unless the user names it.
   - **set-off** (O.18 r.17): the cross-claim with particularity, and that it is set off "in diminution or extinction" of the claim; if it is also a counterclaim, use the `counterclaim` variant.
   - **payment, release, accord, performance**: dates, sums, documents.
   - **estoppel, waiver**: the representation, reliance and detriment.
   - **change of position, good-faith receipt, bona fide purchase**: what was received, when, in what belief, and what the defendant did in reliance.
   - **illegality, want of authority, duress, undue influence, fraud** as a defence: with the particulars the claim module would need (`causes-of-action.md` module 4 for fraud).
   - **quantum and mitigation**: a positive case on the figures, with particulars; a bare denial of loss is not enough.
   - **tender**: only with payment into court (O.18 r.16).
   Never nest these inside the traverse of a claim paragraph; answer the paragraph and point to the section.
7. **Several defendants.** Where a claim paragraph does not concern the pleading defendant, say so factually: it does not know the matters pleaded and makes no admission as to them, and, where true, it did not take part in them and did not know of them at the time. Use one form and apply it consistently.
8. **Relief.** A closing paragraph denying that the plaintiff is entitled to each head of relief claimed, named, or to any relief.
9. **General traverse.** The last paragraph of the body, as a backstop for immaterial allegations only. Every essential allegation is answered specifically above it. Leave it out where nothing is denied.
10. **Formal parts.** The dated line, counsel and solicitors "for the Defendant", the statement of truth naming "this Defence" (or the exact title), any certificate, and the backsheet with Dated, Filed and Served.

### What a defence never does

Join issue (that is for a reply); answer "is not denied" (it admits nothing and denies nothing); intensify a denial ("strongly", "firmly"); mention a payment into court or a sanctioned offer; threaten to strike out, to seek costs or to complain to a professional body; argue; or plead a version inconsistent with what the defendant has said in an earlier pleading without an explanation.

### Points of defence (family proceedings)

The same engine with the Family Court caption and roles. Where the party admits the claim, each admission may be followed by "The Petitioner further avers that ..." with the facts that matter to the party's own position; there is no general traverse where nothing is denied. The statement of truth speaks of "this cause"; a certificate follows where the verifier does not read English.

## United States: what changes

- **Name.** "Answer", usually "Answer and Affirmative Defenses"; "Answer, Affirmative Defenses and Counterclaims" where there is a counterclaim (use the `counterclaim` variant).
- **Grammar.** Every numbered paragraph answered: admit, deny, or want of knowledge in the profile's words (`formulae.md` section 16). Partial admissions admit the true part and deny the rest (FRCP 8(b)(4)). A denial must meet the substance (8(b)(2)). No reasons or version are required inside a denial; the defendant's positive case goes in the affirmative defenses or a counterclaim. Do not answer "the document speaks for itself": admit what the document says and deny the characterisation.
- **General denial.** Federal and Washington practice allow "except as expressly admitted, denied" where the defendant intends in good faith to deny the rest. California allows a bare general denial against an unverified complaint (CCP 431.30(d)), and requires specific denials, positively or on information and belief, and a verified answer against a verified one. The Court of Chancery forbids general denials (Rule 8(b)(3)). The Hong Kong forms, "save and except" and "traversed seriatim", are never used.
- **Format of responses.** The Court of Chancery repeats each allegation verbatim and answers beneath it (Rule 7(a)(3)): set the repeated allegation as an indented quotation under its number, and label the response "RESPONSE:" (not "ANSWER:", which reads as a question-and-answer catechism). The New York Commercial Division requires the answer to interlineate each allegation with its response, keeping the complaint's numbering (Rule 6(d)); ask for the complaint in its word-processing format. Elsewhere, answer by paragraph number, and answer the headings, unnumbered paragraphs and prayer too.
- **Affirmative defenses.** Under their own heading, each separately numbered and titled, each with the facts that support it (Chancery requires a short and plain statement of the basis, Rule 8(c)(3)). California states each defense separately, says which causes of action it answers (CCP 431.30(g)), and heads it with its number and nature. The lists in FRCP 8(c)(1), CPLR 3018(b) and the state rules are not exhaustive: plead anything that would otherwise surprise the plaintiff.
- **Verification.** A New York answer to a verified complaint is verified (CPLR 3020(a)), and some answers must be verified whatever the complaint (3020(b)). California: verified answer to a verified complaint (CCP 446).
- **Delaware Superior Court.** The existence of a corporation, signatures on an instrument filed with the complaint and certain agency are admitted unless denied by affidavit served with the answer (Rule 8(dd)); a complaint noted under 10 Del. C. § 3901 must be answered by affidavit of defense. Check the complaint's face.
- **Defenses raised by motion.** Pre-answer motions (FRCP 12(b), CPLR 3211, a California demurrer) are separate papers. Defenses that are waived unless raised (personal jurisdiction, service, in New York the 3211(a) grounds listed in 3211(e)) must be in the answer if no motion was made.
- **Compulsory counterclaims.** A same-transaction claim is lost if left out in federal court, California and Washington. Ask before filing an answer without one.

## Skeleton: US answer (federal; state changes noted above)

```
{caption as on the complaint}

          DEFENDANT [name 2]'S ANSWER AND AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSES

Defendant [name 2] ("Defendant"), by its undersigned attorneys, answers the
Complaint of Plaintiff [name 1] ("Plaintiff") as follows:

The headings of the Complaint are repeated below for convenience only. To the
extent they contain allegations, Defendant denies them.

                          NATURE OF THE ACTION

1. Defendant denies the allegations of paragraph 1.

                              THE PARTIES

2. Defendant lacks knowledge or information sufficient to form a belief about
   the truth of the allegations of paragraph 2, and therefore denies them.

3. Defendant admits the allegations of paragraph 3.

                         JURISDICTION AND VENUE

4. Paragraph 4 states conclusions of law to which no response is required.
   To the extent a response is required, Defendant admits that this Court has
   subject-matter jurisdiction and otherwise denies the allegations of
   paragraph 4.

                          FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS

7. Defendant admits that it entered into the Agreement on [date 1], refers to
   the Agreement for its contents, and denies any characterization of it
   inconsistent with its terms.

10. Defendant admits that it did not pay [sum 1] on [date 2]. Defendant
    otherwise denies the allegations of paragraph 10.

                   RESPONSE TO THE PRAYER FOR RELIEF

15. Defendant denies that Plaintiff is entitled to the relief requested in
    the Prayer for Relief or to any relief.

                            GENERAL DENIAL

16. Except as expressly admitted above, Defendant denies each and every
    allegation of the Complaint, including any allegation in its headings,
    unnumbered paragraphs and Prayer for Relief. {omit in the Court of
    Chancery}

                         AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSES

Defendant asserts the following defenses without assuming any burden of proof
that it would not otherwise bear.

                        FIRST AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSE
                        (Statute of Limitations)

17. Plaintiff's claims are barred, in whole or in part, by [statutory
    provision] because {the claim accrued on [date 3] and this action was
    filed on [date 4]}.

                        SECOND AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSE
                               (Payment)

18. Plaintiff's claims are barred, in whole or in part, by payment: on
    [date 5], Defendant paid Plaintiff [sum 2] by {method}.

WHEREFORE, Defendant requests that the Court dismiss the Complaint with
prejudice, enter judgment in favor of Defendant, award Defendant its costs,
and grant such other and further relief as the Court deems just and proper.

Dated: [date 6]                         Respectfully submitted,

                                        [solicitors-firm]

                                        By: /s/ [counsel-name]
                                        [counsel-name] ([bar number])
                                        [address]
                                        Attorneys for Defendant [name 2]
```

Every paragraph of the complaint gets a response, and the answer's own paragraphs are numbered continuously to the end; the skeleton shows one of each kind.

## Formulae

`formulae.md` sections 9, 10 and 12 (Hong Kong) and 16 (US).

## Checklist

The checker tests:
- numbered paragraphs and some traverse language (admitted, denied, not admitted; admits, denies).
- Hong Kong: no joinder of issue in a defence; no "is not denied"; a statement of truth; a "Served" line on the backsheet.
- US: no "save and except" or "traversed seriatim"; an affirmative defenses heading (soft); a bar number (soft).
- no "knew or ought to have known" outside quotation marks; no procedural threats; no intensified denials.
- the shared checks: placeholders and the FIELDS table, citations, dashes and tells.

Check by eye:
- every paragraph and every compound allegation of the claim answered, in order.
- every denial with its reason; the defendant's version pleaded wherever it differs.
- every affirmative matter in its own paragraphs with its facts, none nested in the traverse.
- limitation pleaded per cause of action with accrual and expiry dates.
- a positive case on quantum and mitigation where the figures are disputed.
- the denial of relief naming each head claimed; the general traverse only as a backstop, or omitted where nothing is denied.
- nothing inconsistent with the defendant's earlier pleadings or correspondence without an explanation the user has approved.
- US: every complaint paragraph, heading and the prayer answered; affirmative defenses with facts; compulsory counterclaims considered.
