# Counterclaim: defence and counterclaim, counterclaim, cross-complaint

## What it is for

A counterclaim is the defendant's own action against the plaintiff (and, where needed, others), brought inside the plaintiff's action so that both are tried together. It is pleaded as a statement of claim in everything but its position: every element of each cause, with particulars and specific relief. It survives even if the claim is discontinued or fails, which is what distinguishes it from a set-off.

## Hong Kong

### Forms

- **Defence and counterclaim.** One document, titled "DEFENCE AND COUNTERCLAIM", in two headed sections, "DEFENCE" and "COUNTERCLAIM", with paragraphs numbered continuously. All the counterclaim's facts are in numbered paragraphs under its heading; facts already pleaded in the defence are brought in by reference (18/18/1).
- **Counterclaim against the plaintiff and another person.** The other person is added as a defendant to the counterclaim (O.15 r.3), the title of the action is extended to show them, and they are served with the pleading. Ask the user for the added party's details and whether leave or other steps are needed under the profile.
- **Set-off and counterclaim together.** Where the cross-claim both reduces the claim and stands on its own, plead it as a set-off in the defence and as a counterclaim, so that it survives if the claim is discontinued.

### Structure

1. **Title.** "DEFENCE AND COUNTERCLAIM" (spelled correctly on the face and the backsheet; a typo in the title is a defect).
2. **DEFENCE.** As `defence.md`: conventions, the traverse in the claim's order with reasons and the defendant's version, affirmative matter in its own paragraphs, then:
   - where a set-off is relied on, a paragraph that if (which is denied) the defendant is liable in any sum, it will set off the sums counterclaimed against that sum in diminution or extinction of it.
   - the denial of relief.
   - the general traverse.
3. **COUNTERCLAIM.** A fresh heading; numbering continues.
   - **Repetition.** "The Defendant repeats paragraphs [number 1] to [number 2] of the Defence." Name the paragraphs that carry the facts the counterclaim needs; do not repeat the conventions blindly, and never repeat the general traverse.
   - **The claim.** Each cause of action stated separately, using the modules in `causes-of-action.md`: the agreement or relationship, the terms or duties, the breach with particulars, the loss with particulars. The rules for a claim apply: material facts only, every element, particulars of fraud and states of mind, interest pleaded specifically (O.18 rr.8(4), 12, 15(1) through r.18(a)).
   - **Interest.** The basis, rate, sum and period, repeated exactly in the counter-prayer.
   - **Counter-prayer.** "AND THE DEFENDANT COUNTERCLAIMS AGAINST THE PLAINTIFF FOR:-", heads numbered from (1), in the order of `formulae.md` section 8, with the tail. Each relief must follow from pleaded facts; a declaration sought must be prayed.
4. **Formal parts.** The dated line; counsel and solicitors "for the Defendant"; the statement of truth naming "this Defence and Counterclaim" (or the exact title); any certificate; the backsheet with the same title and Dated, Filed and Served.

### Rules to hold

- The counterclaim gives the plaintiff something to answer, so it must be specific enough to traverse: one allegation to a paragraph, in date order.
- Facts that arose after the action began are pleaded as such.
- No procedural threats, no argument and no characterisation of the plaintiff's conduct in the counterclaim either; the temperature of a counterclaim is the temperature of a claim.
- The plaintiff will answer in a reply and defence to counterclaim (`reply.md`), which must traverse every allegation; plead so that each allegation can be admitted or denied on its own.

## United States: what changes

- **Name and place.** Federal, New York, Washington and Delaware: the counterclaims follow the affirmative defenses in one pleading, "Answer, Affirmative Defenses and Counterclaims", under a heading "COUNTERCLAIMS", and must be designated as counterclaims to require an answer (FRCP 7(a)(3); CPLR 3011). The parties take dual labels: "Defendant and Counterclaim-Plaintiff", "Plaintiff and Counterclaim-Defendant".
- **California** has no counterclaim: the defendant files a separate **cross-complaint** (CCP 428.10), with its own caption and the labels "Cross-Complainant" and "Cross-Defendant". Affirmative relief cannot be claimed in the answer (CCP 431.30(c)). A cross-complaint may also bring in third parties on related claims.
- **Compulsory or permissive.** A claim arising out of the same transaction or occurrence is compulsory, and lost if omitted, in federal court (FRCP 13(a)), California (CCP 426.30) and Washington (CR 13(a)); Delaware's rules follow the federal model. New York's CPLR 3019 is permissive on its face. Ask the user about every related claim before the answer is filed.
- **Jurisdiction (federal).** Plead the basis of jurisdiction over any counterclaim that is not compulsory, and over any added party, as `[statutory provision]` unless the user gives it.
- **Other parties.** A claim against a co-party is a crossclaim (FRCP 13(g); Chancery Rule 13(e); CPLR 3019(b)); against a non-party liable over, a third-party complaint (FRCP 14, which needs leave more than 14 days after the original answer).
- **Content.** The counterclaims are pleaded like a complaint: parties, jurisdiction where needed, facts (in full, or adopting answer paragraphs by number under FRCP 10(c) or CPLR 3014), separately numbered counts, a WHEREFORE clause, and a jury demand where wanted. Fraud with particularity; the standards of the profile apply.
- **Verification.** Court of Chancery counterclaims are verified (Rule 3(c)). In New York, a counterclaim is verified where the complaint was, and may be verified separately where it was not (CPLR 3020(a)).
- **No statement of truth and no backsheet.**

## Checklist

The checker tests:
- numbered paragraphs.
- a COUNTERCLAIM (or CROSS-COMPLAINT) heading.
- a counter-prayer: "AND THE ... COUNTERCLAIMS" in Hong Kong; PRAYER FOR RELIEF or WHEREFORE in the US.
- Hong Kong: no joinder of issue; a statement of truth; a "Served" line on the backsheet.
- US: no "save and except" or "traversed seriatim"; an affirmative defenses heading where the answer is in the same pleading (soft); a bar number (soft); a verification in the Court of Chancery (soft).
- no "knew or ought to have known" outside quotation marks; no procedural threats; no intensified denials.
- the shared checks: placeholders and the FIELDS table, citations, dashes and tells.

Check by eye:
- the defence part meets every point in the `defence.md` checklist.
- each cause of action in the counterclaim has every element and its particulars.
- the repetition paragraph names the defence paragraphs that carry the counterclaim's facts, and only those.
- interest, sums and periods identical between the counterclaim body and the counter-prayer.
- every relief in the counter-prayer supported by pleaded facts.
- the title identical on the face, in the statement of truth and on the backsheet.
- US: every related claim considered as compulsory; parties' dual labels used consistently; California relief kept out of the answer.
