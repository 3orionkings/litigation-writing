# Reply: reply, reply and defence to counterclaim, reply or answer to a counterclaim

## What it is for

A reply answers the defence where the plaintiff has something to say that the rules require it to plead: an affirmative answer to new matter (an acknowledgment that defeats limitation, the facts that defeat an estoppel or a change of position, an answer to a set-off). It is not a second statement of claim and it does not deny the defence for the sake of it. A defence to counterclaim is different in kind: it answers a claim, so it follows every rule of a defence.

## Hong Kong

### When each part is needed

- **Reply.** Only where needed to plead a matter O.18 r.8 requires, or where the plaintiff wants its answer to the defendant's new facts on the record (O.18 r.3(1)). Without a reply, issue is joined on the defence automatically, and that joinder operates as a non-admission of every material allegation in it (O.18 r.14(1), (4)).
- **Defence to counterclaim.** Required if the plaintiff intends to defend the counterclaim (O.18 r.3(2)). There is no joinder of issue on a counterclaim, express or implied (O.18 r.14(3)), so every allegation in it that is not admitted must be specifically traversed or it is deemed admitted.
- **Both together.** One document, titled "REPLY AND DEFENCE TO COUNTERCLAIM", in two headed sections, "REPLY" and "DEFENCE TO COUNTERCLAIM", with paragraphs numbered continuously across both (O.18 r.3(3)). Served within 28 days of the defence or counterclaim (O.18 r.3(4)) unless extended.
- **Where the defence was amended,** answer the amended pleading by its title and date, and say so in the conventions.

### What a reply may not do

- Raise a new cause of action, or a claim not founded on the facts in the writ. The remedy is to amend the claim (`amendment.md`).
- Plead anything inconsistent with the statement of claim (O.18 r.10). The plaintiff may add facts and answers; it may not change its case.
- Restate the claim. Where the defence's version simply contradicts the claim, a one-line reference back to the claim paragraphs answers it.

### Structure

1. **Title** as above, or "REPLY" alone.
2. **Conventions.** The abbreviations adopted from the claim and, so far as consistent with it, from the defence; that paragraph references are to the defence (`formulae.md` section 9).
3. **Joinder** (optional, reply only): "The Plaintiff joins issue with the [Defendant] upon its Defence save in so far as the same consists of admissions." An express joinder may also list allegations that are excepted from it and admitted (O.18 r.14(4)). Many Hong Kong replies also open with a preliminary general denial, with or without the joinder: "Save as expressly admitted or not admitted in the Statement of Claim or below, each and every allegation in the Defence is denied." Include it unless the user's practice is otherwise; it does not replace the specific answers that follow.
4. **Answers, in the order of the defence.** Answer the paragraphs that need it; where the defence pleads a full competing narrative, answer each paragraph of it. Use the traverse forms of `formulae.md` section 10:
   - admit, with any fact the plaintiff needs on the record.
   - deny with the reason, and the plaintiff's version where the defence pleads a new one; where the version is already in the claim, "Paragraph [number] is denied. The Plaintiff repeats paragraphs [number 1] to [number 2] of the Statement of Claim."
   - not admit, where the defence pleads matters the plaintiff does not know.
   - where an allegation is too vague to answer, say so in one sentence, make no admission, and serve a request for further and better particulars separately (`litigation-correspondence`); do not threaten one in the pleading.
5. **Affirmative answers, each in its own paragraph,** after the answer to the paragraph that raises the point, or in a short section of their own:
   - to **limitation**: the acknowledgment or part payment (date, document, signatory), the later date of knowledge, or the concealment, with the provision as `[statutory provision]`.
   - to **change of position or good-faith receipt**: what the defendant knew and when (module 4 of `causes-of-action.md`), or that the change came before the receipt or was not made in reliance on it, or that the transaction was void so nothing was given for value.
   - to **estoppel, waiver, laches or acquiescence**: the absence of the representation, of reliance or of detriment, or the facts that explain the delay.
   - to **set-off**: why the cross-claim is not due, not mutual or not connected.
   - to **gift, release or settlement**: the facts that show there was no intention to give or release, or that the release was not authorised.
   Law is not argued: the reply pleads the facts that make the answer good and, where a statute is relied on, names it.
6. **Defence to counterclaim.** A fresh heading. Then:
   - a paragraph repeating the reply paragraphs that also answer the counterclaim ("The Plaintiff repeats paragraphs [number 1] to [number 2] above.").
   - a conventions line saying references in this section are to the paragraphs of the counterclaim.
   - every allegation of the counterclaim answered specifically, in order, with reasons and the plaintiff's version, exactly as in a defence (`defence.md`).
   - affirmative matter in its own paragraphs (limitation, set-off, tender with payment in, O.18 r.18(b)).
   - a denial that the defendant is entitled to the relief counterclaimed, naming each head, or to any relief.
   - the general traverse of the counterclaim as a backstop.
7. **Formal parts.** The dated line; counsel and solicitors "for the Plaintiff"; a statement of truth naming the pleading by the title on its face; any certificate; the backsheet with the same title and Dated, Filed and Served.

## United States: what changes

- **No reply to an answer** unless the court orders one (FRCP 7(a)(7); CPLR 3011; Delaware Superior Court Rule 7(a)). Affirmative defenses in an answer are treated as denied without one (FRCP 8(b)(6); CPLR 3018(a); CCP 431.20(b)). Do not draft a reply to affirmative defenses unless there is an order; say so to the user.
- **The response to a counterclaim** is required where the counterclaim is designated as such: "Answer to Counterclaims" in federal court (FRCP 7(a)(3)), "Reply to Counterclaims" in New York (CPLR 3011) and the Delaware Superior Court, "Answer to Cross-Complaint" in California. A New York cross-claim needs an answer only if it demands one.
- **Grammar.** That of an answer (`defence.md` and `formulae.md` section 16): every numbered paragraph of the counterclaim admitted, denied or met with want of knowledge in the profile's words; affirmative defenses to the counterclaim under their own heading, with facts. Court of Chancery: repeat each counterclaim allegation as an indented quotation and put the response beneath it, labelled "RESPONSE:"; no general denial. New York Commercial Division: interlineate.
- **Verification.** In New York, a reply to a verified counterclaim is verified (CPLR 3020(a)); in California an answer to a verified cross-complaint is verified (CCP 446).
- **No new claims** in the response; a new claim against the counterclaimant is pleaded by amending the complaint.

## Formulae

`formulae.md` sections 9, 10 and 12 (Hong Kong) and 16 and 17 (US).

## Checklist

The checker tests:
- numbered paragraphs and some traverse or joinder language.
- Hong Kong: a statement of truth and a "Served" line on the backsheet.
- US: no "save and except" or "traversed seriatim"; that the document answers a counterclaim or a court order (soft); a bar number (soft).
- no "knew or ought to have known" outside quotation marks; no procedural threats; no "is not denied" in Hong Kong.
- the shared checks: placeholders and the FIELDS table, citations, dashes and tells.

Check by eye:
- the joinder appears in the reply section only; the defence to counterclaim traverses every allegation specifically.
- nothing in the reply is a new cause of action or inconsistent with the claim.
- every affirmative defence in the defence that needs an answer has one, with facts, in its own paragraph.
- the answered pleading is identified by its exact title and date, including "Amended" where it was.
- paragraph numbering runs continuously from the reply into the defence to counterclaim.
- the statement of truth and the backsheet carry the title exactly as on the face.
- US: a reply to an answer only if the court ordered one.
