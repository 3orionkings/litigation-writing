# Deponent intake

Evidence is the witness's account, so the draft can only be as good as what is known about the witness. Settle the points below before drafting the body. Ask them in the same message as the missing fields, grouped as here, and skip any that the matter file or the conversation already answers. If the user tells you to go ahead without some answers, draft with placeholders and list the open points above the draft.

Keep the answers in a deponent note (at the end of this file). It governs every sentence: what the deponent may say flat, what must carry a source, and what stays out.

## 1. Who the deponent is

- Their name as it will appear in the title and the jurat (`[deponent]` until given), and the address for the opening words.
- Their role in the proceedings: a party, an officer or employee of a party, the solicitor with conduct, or a non-party. For a non-party, their occupation and their relationship to each party.
- The number of this affirmation for this deponent in this action, and the earlier ones they have made (dates, exhibit codes). A wrong ordinal breaks every later cross-reference and exhibit code.

## 2. Capacity and authority

- **An individual party:** acting in person or through solicitors? A party in person gets a plain voice and no solicitors' phrasing.
- **A company:** the office the deponent holds; their authority to depose for the company (a board resolution, or the user's confirmation); whether they speak from their own dealings, from the company's records, or both. Authority goes in the first paragraph.
- **The solicitor with conduct:** why the party is not giving the evidence; which facts come from the client and from whom; what must stay privileged. A solicitor's account of what the client did or thought is information and belief and must name its source.
- **Family proceedings:** how any child is to be referred to (`[the Child]` unless the user decides otherwise), and how much feeling the deponent will want to express.

## 3. What they know, and how

Sort every fact the evidence needs into one of four kinds. The kind decides the wording.

| Kind | Meaning | How the draft says it |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge | Seen, done or heard first-hand | Stated flat |
| Document | From a document they hold or have read | Stated with the document, which is exhibited at that point |
| Informant | Told by someone | "I am informed by [name], {role}, and verily believe that …" (Hong Kong); "[name] told me, and I believed, that …" (witness statement); with when, where possible |
| Unknown | Not known to them | "I do not know …"; then decide whether another witness, a document or disclosure can supply it |

Ask specifically: who told the deponent each informant fact, in what role and when; whether that informant could give the evidence directly instead; and whether any "fact" in the instructions is really the lawyer's inference rather than something the deponent knows.

## 4. Documents to exhibit

- The list: for each document, its date, a short description, its author and recipient, its page count, and who holds the original.
- The exhibit code scheme, if the user has one (for example the deponent's initials, the affirmation number and a running number, or one paginated bundle per affirmation cited by page). Otherwise use `[exhibit]` placeholders, numbered `[exhibit 1]`, `[exhibit 2]` in order of first mention.
- Documents already exhibited to an earlier affirmation: refer to them by their existing code rather than exhibiting them again, unless the user wants a self-contained set.
- Undated documents, screenshots and recordings: what identifies each one (a date range, a timestamp, the source or device).
- Documents in another language: whether translations are needed, and who will certify them.

## 5. Language

- Does the deponent read English well enough to read the whole document and its exhibits before affirming?
- If not: the language or dialect; who will interpret (name and firm, often filled in later); whether the document will be read over orally or translated in writing.
- **Hong Kong.** An interpreter's jurat follows the deponent's jurat (`formulae.md` section 11). For a witness statement, the interpreter's or translator's certificate in the profile.
- **New York.** A paper in a foreign language goes with an English translation and the translator's affidavit or affirmation of qualifications and accuracy (CPLR 2101(b)).
- **California.** Foreign-language exhibits need an English translation certified under oath by a qualified interpreter (CRC 3.1110(g)).
- **Other US profiles.** No rule is recorded; ask the user for the form their court uses.
- Whatever the language, write in plain words the deponent will recognise when the document is read back to them.

## 6. What the deponent is not to depose to

- Privileged legal advice and communications with lawyers. Once in the evidence, a waiver cannot be undone.
- Without prejudice negotiations and offers.
- Matters under a confidentiality obligation, an order or an undertaking.
- Identity-card numbers, medical details and details of children beyond what the application needs.
- Topics left to another witness, or that the user wants left out.
- Opinion, other than the deponent's own lay observation.

## 7. Where and how it will be made

- Affirmation or oath: the deponent's choice. It changes the opening words and the jurat.
- Where it will be made: in Hong Kong (before a commissioner for oaths, usually at a solicitors' office) or abroad (before a notary public; check what the court requires for evidence made outside the jurisdiction).
- In the US: where the declarant will sign, because some forms change with the place (federal, California); Delaware may need a notary.
- The jurat details (place, date, commissioner) are filled in later.

## 8. The voice

- How the deponent speaks: formal or plain, technical in their own trade, a second-language speaker? Words they would not use?
- Anything they feel strongly about and will want said, and whether it belongs in the evidence at all.
- Other witnesses giving evidence about the same events, so that sums, dates and defined terms can be matched across all of them.

```
Deponent:       [deponent], {ordinal} affirmation in this action

Documents:      {documents to exhibit; code scheme or [exhibit] placeholders}

Unknown:        {gaps, and who or what could fill them}

```
