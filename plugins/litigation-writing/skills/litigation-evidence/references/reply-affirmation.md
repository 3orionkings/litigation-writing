# Evidence in reply

Variant id: `reply-affirmation`. Square brackets are placeholders from `references/fields.md`; text in braces is a drafting note to be replaced or deleted.

## What it is for

Answering the other side's evidence: an affirmation in reply to evidence in opposition, and in further support of the application, or a further round where the rules or a direction allow one. The judge reads it beside the evidence it answers, so its structure follows that evidence.

Reply evidence answers; it does not start again. Matter that belonged in the first affirmation invites an objection or another round, and may need leave. If the user wants new facts in, say so in one line above the draft and leave the decision to them.

## Structure: Hong Kong

1. **Caption and title** ("[number] AFFIRMATION OF [deponent]").
2. **Opening words, capacity, knowledge.**
3. **Reading conventions.** Define the evidence being answered once ("[name]'s Affirmation" means the [number] Affirmation of [name] made on [date]); adopt the abbreviations of the deponent's own earlier affirmation.
4. **Purpose.** "I make this affirmation in reply to [name]'s Affirmation and in further support of the Summons." Where a cross-summons is answered at the same time, add "and in opposition to the [Defendant]'s Summons dated [date]".
5. **General traverse.** "Save as expressly admitted below, I deny …", and "Where I do not respond to a particular paragraph … that is not to be taken as an admission." A company's officer can traverse for the company and leave matters of law to its counsel in the same paragraph.
6. **Overview.** A few paragraphs that fix the story and the deponent's answer to the other side's main point, as facts. Refer to the earlier affirmation by paragraph instead of repeating it.
7. **The answer, in the other side's order.** Headings or lead-ins keyed to its paragraph numbers ("Paragraphs 5 to 8 of [name]'s Affirmation"), so that the judge can read the two together. Group paragraphs that raise one point; say once that a run of paragraphs is irrelevant and why, instead of answering each.
8. **Law, parked**, as in `affirmation.md`.
9. **Prayer.**
10. **Jurat**, filing line, interpreter's jurat if needed, exhibit cover and index, backsheet.

## The unit of rebuttal

Each answer has three parts:

(a) a verdict sentence in plain words: "That is not correct." "No such meeting took place." "The [Defendant] did not raise this at the time.";
(b) what shows it: the document with its exhibit code or page, or the deponent's own first-hand account;
(c) a one-line consequence, as fact: "The payment was therefore made before the meeting he describes."

Where the other side has changed its case, use four steps: what it first said (by pleading or affirmation paragraph); what it says now; the explanation it gives for the change; and why the deponent does not accept the explanation, with the document that shows it.

Quote the other side briefly, a few words with the paragraph number. Answer the facts, not the adjectives.

An "even if" argument ("even if [name]'s account were accepted (which is denied)") belongs to counsel. If one must appear, it goes inside an "I am advised and verily believe that" sentence and is handed over.

## Temperature

Reply evidence tends to run hot. Keep the heat in the documents. A deponent may say a statement is untrue and show why. Labels such as "sham" or "fabrication" are counsel's to deploy; in the deponent's mouth they invite cross-examination about motive. If the user wants one, tie it to a paragraph and an exhibit.

## Differences in the US profiles

- **The instrument.** A reply declaration "in further support of" the motion, in the profile's declaration form.
- **Reply papers only reply.** California summary judgment reply papers may not include new evidentiary matter (CCP 437c(b)(4)); New York bars sur-replies without permission in advance (22 NYCRR 202.8-c), and limits reply attorney affirmations under 202.8-b. Check the profile and the judge's practices.
- **No traverse.** A US declaration does not "deny" the paragraphs of the other side's declaration. It states the true facts, paragraph by paragraph if that helps, and the reply memorandum argues.
- **No law, no prayer.**

## Skeleton: Hong Kong

```
{caption and title as in affirmation.md}

I, [deponent], of [address], do hereby solemnly, sincerely and truly affirm and say as follows:

1.  I am {capacity}. This is my [number] affirmation in this action.

2.  The facts and matters deposed to herein are, unless otherwise stated, within my own knowledge and are true to the best of my knowledge, information and belief.

3.  I refer to my [number] Affirmation made on [date 1] (my "[number] Affirmation") and adopt the abbreviations used in it. In this affirmation, "[name]'s Affirmation" means the [number] Affirmation of [name] made on [date 2].

4.  I make this affirmation in reply to [name]'s Affirmation and in further support of the Summons.

5.  Save as expressly admitted below, I deny the allegations in [name]'s Affirmation. Where I do not respond to a particular paragraph of [name]'s Affirmation, that is not to be taken as an admission of its contents.

6.  {Overview: the deponent's answer to the main point, as facts, in a few paragraphs.}

Paragraphs [number] to [number] of [name]'s Affirmation

7.  At paragraph [number], [name] says that "{a few words}". That is not correct. {What shows it, with the exhibit or page.} {One-line consequence.}

8.  {Change of case: what was first said, at paragraph [number] of the [document title]; what is said now; the explanation given; why it is not accepted, with the document.}

Paragraph [number] of [name]'s Affirmation

9.  {Next unit.}

10. I am advised and verily believe that {the legal conclusion}. That is a matter of law, which I leave to counsel.

CONCLUSION

11. In the circumstances, I respectfully ask the Court for an order in terms of the Summons {and to dismiss the [Defendant]'s Summons dated [date 3]}.

{Jurat, filing line, interpreter's jurat, exhibits and backsheet as in affirmation.md.}
```

## Checklist

The checker tests: everything listed in `affirmation.md`, plus a reply purpose ("in reply", "in further support") near the start, and (softly) references to the paragraphs of the evidence answered.

Check by eye:

- Every paragraph of the other side's evidence that matters is answered, in its order, and nothing is left that could be taken as admitted.
- No new case: every fact either answers a point raised or is flagged to the user as new matter.
- Each verdict has its document or first-hand basis; no label without a paragraph and an exhibit.
- Quotations of the other side's words are accurate and short, with paragraph numbers.
- Defined terms and exhibit codes continue from the deponent's earlier affirmation.
