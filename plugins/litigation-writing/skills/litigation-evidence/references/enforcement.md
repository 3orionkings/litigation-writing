# Enforcement evidence

Variant id: `enforcement`. Square brackets are placeholders from `references/fields.md`; text in braces is a drafting note to be replaced or deleted.

## What it is for

The evidence for a post-judgment remedy when the judgment is unpaid: an order for the examination of the judgment debtor, a charging order over land, or a garnishee order against a bank or another person who owes the judgment debtor money. The application is often made without notice, so full and frank disclosure applies. The court needs a short, exact record: the judgment, what is unpaid, who the debtor is, what has been demanded, and, for the remedy, each matter the rule requires.

The draft is a common preamble plus one module. Where the creditor pursues several remedies, each affirmation repeats the preamble with a new "as at" date and updated sums, and adds its own module.

## The rules (Hong Kong)

Name a rule in the evidence only as the user gives it. For orientation: garnishee proceedings are under O.49, charging orders under O.50, and examination of a judgment debtor under O.49B (as the profile records) or O.48, depending on the relief sought; the choice is the user's. Read the rule for the matters the affidavit or affirmation must state, and make sure each one is in the evidence. The evidence supplies those matters; it does not quote or discuss the rule.

## The preamble (every module)

In this order, without headings in a short affirmation:

1. **Capacity and knowledge.** For a company creditor, the director's authority. A "crave leave to refer" sentence for the pleading, if useful.
2. **The judgment.** Date, amount, interest terms and costs, with the defined terms "Judgment", "Judgment Creditor", "Judgment Debtor" and "Judgment Sum". Exhibit it.
3. **Any costs order.** Date and amount, or the basis of assessment. Exhibit it.
4. **The debtor.** Full name as in the judgment; last known address, and where that address comes from.
5. **The demand.** Date; each mode of sending; to whom, including the debtor's solicitors; the amount; the deadline; then non-payment. Exhibit the letter and the proof of sending.
6. **The sums due, as at a stated date,** itemised: (a) the judgment sum; (b) interest on it to that date; (c) costs; (d) interest on costs; then the total, defined as the "Judgment Debt". Put the calculation in a schedule if it is not obvious.
7. **Entitlement.** One sentence that the debt may now be enforced. Any belief that the debtor will not pay voluntarily rests on the facts above, and is said in those terms.

Once defined, use "Judgment Creditor" and "Judgment Debtor" throughout, never "the Plaintiff" or "the Defendant". The caption shows the labels after the party roles: "[Plaintiff] (Judgment Creditor)".

## Module A: examination of the judgment debtor

- **Enforcement so far.** Each step already taken, with its date, the tribunal and the outcome (a charging order made; a garnishee order that attached nothing).
- **The assets believed to exist,** one paragraph each, in three parts: (i) what the document or the informant shows, with the exhibit; (ii) "My belief is based on …"; (iii) "I do not know …" (whether it is still held, encumbered, let or sold). The gap is what the examination is for.
- **Timing,** where it matters: list the dates (the judgment, service of an order, the debtor's act) and place them side by side. Then concede what the timing does not prove, and say in measured words what it leads the deponent to fear.
- **The documents** the debtor should produce, listed specifically.
- **Prayer:** attendance for examination and production, at the earliest date.

A long examination affirmation reads better with lettered headings: the judgment and the debt; non-payment and enforcement so far; the assets and the need for examination.

## Module B: charging order

- **Other creditors.** "To the best of my knowledge, information and belief, the Judgment Debtor has no creditors other than …", naming each mortgagee or chargee with the date of the instrument and its registration particulars from the land search.
- **The property.** The Land Registry long-form description and the postal address, defined as "the Property".
- **The land search.** Exhibited; the registered owner; the encumbrances.
- **Beneficial ownership.** The deponent's belief that the debtor is beneficially entitled, subject to the encumbrances, and its basis. If a co-owner or a spouse may claim an interest, say so: that is part of the disclosure.
- **Prayer:** a charging order over the debtor's interest in the Property, with the costs of and occasioned by the application.

No headings; about a dozen paragraphs.

## Module C: garnishee order

- **How the account or debt was identified.** Each source in its own paragraph, exhibited: a document naming the account, an informant by name and role, the deponent's own record of a payment into it.
- **The belief paragraph.** The garnishee is within the jurisdiction and owes the debtor money; then "The grounds of my belief are …".
- **The identifiers.** The garnishee's full legal name, the account number, the name in which the account stands, the branch and its code.
- **Other accounts.** If the deponent believes there are others (for example because the garnishee holds a mortgage over the debtor's property), say why, say plainly what is not known, and ask that the order extend to every account.
- **Prayer:** an order attaching all debts due or accruing due from the garnishee to the debtor, including the identified account and any other, with the costs of and occasioned by the application, paid out of the monies attached if the Court so directs.
- **Caption.** Name the garnishee in the title as the rules and the profile require.

## Temperature

Nil. No word about the debtor's character. Inferences rest on dates and documents, and are conceded where they fall short of proof.

## Differences in the US profiles

The profiles do not cover post-judgment remedies. Draft the factual content (the judgment, the sums due, the debtor's identity, the demand, the assets and the sources of belief) as a declaration in the profile's form, and write `[rule]` for the enforcement procedure. Ask the user which device applies and what it requires, and mark every formal requirement for the user to check: whether a declaration or a sworn affidavit is needed, whether the application may be made without notice, any prescribed court form, and the names of the papers. "Judgment creditor" and "judgment debtor" are common to both systems; the names of the remedies are not.

10.  {Asset}: {what the document or informant shows}. There is now produced and shown to me marked "[exhibit 5]" a copy of the [document title]. My belief is based on {the sources}. I do not know {whether it is still held, encumbered, let or sold}.

In the circumstances, I respectfully ask the Court to order the Judgment Debtor to attend before {the Court or an officer of the Court} and be orally examined as to whether any and what debts are owing to {him}, and whether {he} has any and what other property or means of satisfying the Judgment Debt, and to produce {the documents listed}.

{Module B: charging order}

9.   To the best of my knowledge, information and belief, the Judgment Debtor has no creditors other than {each mortgagee or chargee, with the date and registration particulars}.

10.  ALL THAT piece or parcel of ground registered in the Land Registry as [property description] And of and in the messuages erections and buildings thereon known as [address] (the "Property").

11.  A land search of the Property made on [date 6] shows the Judgment Debtor as the only registered owner. There is now produced and shown to me marked "[exhibit 5]" a copy of the land search. I verily believe that {he} alone is beneficially entitled to the Property, subject to {the registered encumbrances}.

12.  In the circumstances, I respectfully ask the Court to make a charging order over the Judgment Debtor's interest in the Property, and that the costs of and occasioned by this application be paid by the Judgment Debtor.

{Module C: garnishee order}

9.   {Source 1, exhibited.} {Source 2: I am informed by [name], {role}, and verily believe that …} {Source 3, exhibited.}

10.  To the best of my knowledge, information and belief, [Garnishee] (the "Garnishee") is within the jurisdiction and is indebted to the Judgment Debtor in respect of monies standing to the credit of account no. [account number] in the name of the Judgment Debtor, maintained at its branch at [address] (branch code [number]). The grounds of my belief are the matters set out in paragraph 9 above.

11.  {Other accounts: why the deponent believes they exist; I do not know their particulars.}

12.  In the circumstances, I respectfully ask the Court to make a garnishee order attaching all debts due or accruing due from the Garnishee to the Judgment Debtor, including the monies standing to the credit of account no. ## Checklist

The checker tests: everything in `affirmation.md`, plus "Judgment Debtor" in the text; softly, a statement of unpaid sums, a stated basis of belief, and no comment on the debtor's character.

Check by eye:

- The judgment, the costs order and the sums match the sealed documents; the (a) to (d) items add up to the total; the "as at" date is the same throughout.
- Each matter the rule requires for this remedy is in the evidence.
- Every asset or account has its source and its "I do not know" sentence.
- Charging order: the long-form description matches the land search; all other creditors are disclosed.
- Garnishee order: full legal name, account number, account name and branch code are right; the order extends to other accounts only with a stated reason.
- Disclosure: anything that tells against the order (a co-owner, a payment proposal, a pending appeal or stay) is in the evidence.
