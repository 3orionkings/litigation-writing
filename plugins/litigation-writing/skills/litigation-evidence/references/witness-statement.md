# Witness statement for trial

Variant id: `witness-statement`. Square brackets are placeholders from `references/fields.md`; text in braces is a drafting note to be replaced or deleted.

## What it is for

The witness's evidence-in-chief, exchanged before a trial or a preliminary issue under the court's directions, and usually ordered to stand as that evidence. The witness will be cross-examined on it, so every sentence must survive a question. It tells the witness's story in the witness's words; the closing submissions will do the arguing.

## The grammar (Hong Kong)

A witness statement is not an affirmation, and its grammar differs:

- **Opening:** "I, [witness], of [address], do make this statement and say as follows:".
- **Paragraph 1** joins capacity and purpose: who the witness is, and the directions under which the statement is made ("I make this witness statement in accordance with the directions given by the Court on [date]").
- **No knowledge sentence** is needed. Where a fact comes from elsewhere, say so where it appears.
- **No "I am advised" and no "verily believe".** What another person said: "[name] told me, and I believed, that …". What a document or a judgment says: "It is my understanding that …", "I understand from the [document title] that …". Never "my understanding" for the witness's own acts.
- **Documents:** refer to the trial bundle (`[Bundle ref]`) once it exists. Before then, annex copies ("annexed hereto marked "Annexure [number]"") or follow the directions if they provide for exhibits.
- **Evaluation**, if any, only in a short closing section ("Concluding Remarks"): the witness's own view in plain words, never law and never a summary of the evidence.
- **Statement of truth** at the end, signed and dated, then the interpreter's or translator's certificate where needed.
- **Prayer.** Some practitioners close a party's statement with one sentence asking for the relief claimed in the pleading. It is optional; follow the user's practice.

## Structure (Hong Kong)

1. Caption; title "WITNESS STATEMENT OF [witness]".
2. Opening words; paragraph 1 (capacity and directions).
3. **Background.** Who the witness is: occupation, relationships, roles in the events.
4. **The story,** in date order, by episode under topic headings. One event a paragraph. For each conversation: who was there, when and where, what was said as best the witness recalls it, and what the witness did next.
5. **Agreements.** The terms in lettered sub-paragraphs, as the witness understood them at the time. Where there was an oral and a written version, give each in turn, then say whether the parties' conduct followed them.
6. **Knowledge trail.** Where the case turns on what someone knew, say at each episode who was told what and when, rather than in a separate section at the end.
7. **Procedural history and earlier judgments,** only as far as the witness knows them, as "my understanding", by date and paragraph.
8. **The issues.** Where the issues list or the pleadings frame the factors the court will weigh, the later sections can follow them as headings, with the witness's facts under each. Never state the law behind them.
9. **Concluding Remarks** (optional, short).
10. Statement of truth, signature, date; certificate where needed; backsheet if filed.

## Points to watch

- **The witness's words.** A translated statement keeps the witness's plain wording; do not polish it into lawyer's English.
- **Only what this witness can speak to.** Leave the rest to the witness who knows it, or to the documents.
- **Reconcile before exchange.** Sums, dates and the terms of any agreement must match the other witnesses on the same side. Flag any difference to the user; never smooth it over.
- **Sub-paragraphs** for terms and lists, (a), (b), then (i), (ii); never bullets.
- **No reply in the body.** Do not argue with the other side's statements unless the directions allow supplemental statements; then follow the reply discipline in `reply-affirmation.md`.

## Differences in the US profiles

There is no general practice of exchanged trial witness statements. Some judges take direct testimony in a bench trial by declaration or affidavit; that requirement, and its form, come from the judge's individual practices, which the profiles do not cover. Ask the user for them. Where it applies:

- use the profile's declaration form, with the personal-knowledge sentence;
- no statement of truth: the declaration words close it;
- documents by trial exhibit number (`[exhibit]`);
- facts only, in numbered paragraphs; second-hand matter is governed by the forum's hearsay rules, which are for the user.

## Checklist

The checker tests: numbered paragraphs; the first person; in Hong Kong, the opening "make this statement" and the statement of truth; in the US, the profile's declaration words; no "I am advised" (hard) and no "verily believe" or exhibit formula (soft); no "It is submitted", case citation, "a fortiori" or "the Court is invited".

Check by eye:

- Every second-hand fact names who said it, or is framed as the witness's understanding of a document.
- Evaluation appears only in the closing section, and it is the witness's own view.
- The story is in date order and each episode is in the witness's own words.
- Figures and terms match the other witnesses and the documents.
- The statement of truth is in the current form and the certificate is there if the witness does not read English.
