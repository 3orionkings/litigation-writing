# Evidence of a non-party

Variant id: `non-party`. Square brackets are placeholders from `references/fields.md`; text in braces is a drafting note to be replaced or deleted.

## What it is for

Evidence from someone who is not a party: a relative who lent money, a former employee who saw a document signed, an accountant who prepared a set of accounts, a bank officer speaking from the bank's records. Usually it corroborates one specific part of a party's evidence. Its value is its independence, so it says what the witness did, saw and holds, and stops there.

## Structure: Hong Kong

1. **Caption and title.** "AFFIRMATION OF [deponent]", with an ordinal from the second affirmation on.
2. **Opening words,** with the occupation after the name (`formulae.md` section 1).
3. **Purpose first.** "I make this affirmation in support of the [Petitioner]'s {claim or application} in relation to {the specific matter}." The purpose comes before the knowledge sentence, because the court first needs to know why a stranger to the action is giving evidence.
4. **Knowledge,** in the sourced form for a non-party (section 3).
5. **Relationship.** Who the witness is to each party, and how they come to know the facts. One plain sentence saves the court guessing.
6. **What they did,** in date order: the occasion on which they learned of the matter, what they did and why, where their reasons explain the act (a loan rather than a gift, payment by cheque rather than transfer).
7. **The document they hold,** exhibited. Usually one or a few, and their own, not the party's.
8. **Nothing more.** No comment on the merits, on the other party's conduct or on the law. No "I am advised". No prayer.
9. **Jurat,** the interpreter's jurat where needed, "This Affirmation is filed on behalf of the [Petitioner].", exhibit cover, backsheet.

## Points to watch

- **Match the party's evidence exactly** on dates, sums and terms; any difference will be put to both witnesses. If the instructions differ, flag it to the user; do not smooth it.
- **Records witnesses.** A bank or company officer speaks from records: say which records, kept by whom and in the ordinary course of business, what they show, and exhibit the extract.
- **Neutral witnesses.** State the facts and let them stand. Do not have the witness volunteer support for either side.
- **The witness owns the words.** The party's lawyers usually draft, and the witness must be free to correct the draft. Put questions for the witness or the client in the note above the draft, never in its text.
- **Temperature:** none.

## Differences in the US profiles

- The profile's declaration form, with the personal-knowledge and competence sentence. The structure is the same without the Hong Kong formulae: purpose, relationship, what the witness did, the document, the declaration words.
- A records custodian's declaration lays the foundation for business records; what the forum's evidence rules require for it is not in the profiles. Ask the user for the form their court expects.

```

                 AFFIRMATION OF [deponent]

I, [deponent], {occupation}, of [address], do hereby solemnly, sincerely and truly affirm and say as follows:

1.  I make this affirmation in support of the [Petitioner]'s {claim or application} in relation to {the specific matter}.

2.  Save where otherwise stated, the facts deposed to herein are within my own knowledge. Where they are not, I identify the source of my information, and I believe it to be true.

3.  I am not a party to these proceedings. {Relationship to each party, and how the deponent knows the facts.}

4.  In or around [date 1], {the occasion on which the deponent learned of the matter}.

5.  On [date 2], {what the deponent did, and why}.
```

## Checklist

The checker tests: numbered paragraphs; the first person; the Hong Kong opening formula and jurat, or the profile's declaration words; softly, a purpose sentence, a statement of the relationship, and no prayer, no "I am advised" and no comment on the merits; and the forbidden phrases common to all evidence.

Check by eye:

- Every date, sum and term matches the party's evidence on the same point.
- The witness says only what they did, saw or hold; no inference about the dispute.
- The document exhibited is the witness's own.
- The interpreter's jurat is there if the witness does not read English.
