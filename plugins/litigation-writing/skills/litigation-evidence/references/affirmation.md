# Affirmation in support or opposition

Variant id: `affirmation`. Square brackets are placeholders from `references/fields.md`; text in braces is a drafting note to be replaced or deleted.

## What it is for

The evidence on which an application is decided, filed with the summons or in answer to it: summary judgment, strike-out, security for costs, an injunction, maintenance, an unless order, a stay, and the rest. The court reads it before the hearing, and counsel's skeleton cites it by paragraph and exhibit page. Its job is to put on the record every fact counsel will need, each with its source, in the deponent's voice, and nothing counsel would have to explain away.

An affidavit has the same structure. Only the opening words and the jurat change (sworn rather than affirmed).

## Structure: Hong Kong

1. **Caption.** Court, case number and parties as the profile sets them.
2. **Title.** "[number] AFFIRMATION OF [deponent]", the ordinal counting this deponent's affirmations in the action. Some practitioners leave "1st" off the first; using it from the start keeps exhibit codes and later references unambiguous.
3. **Opening words** (`formulae.md` section 1).
4. **Capacity.** Who the deponent is in the proceedings; for a company, the office and the authority to depose.
5. **Knowledge.** The knowledge sentence (section 3).
6. **Reading conventions.** The abbreviations adopted from a pleading, a "crave leave to refer" sentence, and any earlier affirmation relied on.
7. **Purpose.** "I make this affirmation in support of" (or "in opposition to") the summons, named by party and date, with the orders sought listed as (a), (b), (c), copied word for word from the summons. With no summons (ex parte), name the application and, if the user gives it, the rule.
8. **Overview.** In contested evidence, two to five paragraphs saying what the evidence shows, as facts: the agreement, the default, the demand, the silence. No law and no adjectives.
9. **Body.** Under headings that state a point. Facts in date order, one event a paragraph, each closed by its exhibit. Correspondence as a ladder: one paragraph per letter (date, what it asked, the reply or the silence, the exhibit). Where the court weighs known factors (for security for costs, for example: where the plaintiff resides, what assets it has within the jurisdiction, whether a judgment could be enforced against it, the likely costs), the factors can be the headings.
10. **The other side's case.** In opposition, take the other side's evidence or pleading by its paragraph numbers, in its order, using the unit of rebuttal in `reply-affirmation.md`.
11. **Law, parked.** Where a legal conclusion must appear, one "I am advised and verily believe that …" sentence states it, and a short sentence leaves it to counsel. A few at most in the whole affirmation.
12. **Alternatives.** If orders are sought in the alternative, bridge each with "If the Court is not minded to …": the deponent asks for less without conceding.
13. **Prayer.** One paragraph asking for an order in terms of the summons. Restate the orders in full under a "Conclusion" heading only where alternatives make that clearer.
14. **Jurat**, then "This Affirmation is filed on behalf of the [Plaintiff].", then the interpreter's jurat if the deponent does not read English.
15. **Exhibit cover sheet(s) and index.**
16. **Backsheet.**

**Without notice.** On an ex parte application the deponent owes the court full and frank disclosure. Set out, under its own heading, the points the absent party would make and anything else that tells against the order. Mark the document for an ex parte application if local practice expects it.

**Temperature.** Commercial applications: measured; a strong word only where a document uses it. Family applications: the deponent's own feeling, briefly, is acceptable; attacks on the other party are not. A party in person: plain words, with no sign of the drafter's polish.

## Differences in the US profiles

- **The instrument.** A declaration under penalty of perjury: 28 U.S.C. § 1746 (federal), CCP 2015.5 (California), RCW 5.50.050 and GR 13 (Washington). New York: an affirmation under CPLR 2106, which any person may make wherever they are. Delaware: the profiles show affidavits on personal knowledge (Superior Court Civil Rule 56(e); Chancery Rule 56(c)(4)); whether a declaration is accepted is for the user to check. No commissioner and, outside Delaware, no notary.
- **Title and caption.** "DECLARATION OF [deponent] IN SUPPORT OF [Plaintiff]'S [document title]". California requires the caption to name the declarant and identify the motion it supports or opposes (CRC 3.1115), on pleading paper. Follow the profile for the court's paper format.
- **Opening.** "I, [deponent], declare as follows:", then a capacity paragraph stating personal knowledge and competence.
- **No list of orders, no prayer.** One sentence says which motion the declaration supports. The relief is in the notice of motion and the memorandum.
- **No law.** No "I am advised" sentence: the memorandum carries the law. Facts only, without conclusions or argument; N.D. Cal. Civil L.R. 7-5(b) says so expressly and requires the basis of any statement on information or belief.
- **Exhibits.** "Attached as Exhibit [exhibit] is a true and correct copy of …", lettered or numbered as local practice requires. In King County, documents go to a declaration that identifies and authenticates them, and attachments above 25 pages are paginated consecutively. California exhibits in a foreign language need a certified translation (CRC 3.1110(g)).
- **Length.** New York Commercial Division Rule 17 limits affidavits and affirmations to 7,000 words, with counsel's word-count certificate; elsewhere in the Supreme Court, 22 NYCRR 202.8-b limits attorney affirmations but not lay or expert evidence.
- **Close.** The profile's declaration words, the date (and the place where the profile requires it), the signature and the printed name.

## Skeleton: Hong Kong

```
                                                        [case number]
IN THE [court] OF THE
HONG KONG SPECIAL ADMINISTRATIVE REGION
{division line, and the long-form action number, as the profile sets them}
==========================================================
BETWEEN
[name 1]                                              [Plaintiff]
                              and
[name 2]                                              [Defendant]
==========================================================
                 [number] AFFIRMATION OF [deponent]
==========================================================

I, [deponent], of [address], do hereby solemnly, sincerely and truly affirm and say as follows:

1.  I am {capacity}. {Company: I am duly authorised by the [Plaintiff] to make this affirmation on its behalf.}

2.  The facts and matters deposed to herein are, unless otherwise stated, within my own knowledge and are true. Where they are not within my own knowledge, I state their source, and they are true to the best of my knowledge, information and belief.

3.  Unless otherwise stated, I adopt the abbreviations used in the [document title] dated [date 1].

4.  I make this affirmation in support of the [Plaintiff]'s Summons dated [date 2] (the "Summons") for the following orders:
    (a) {order, word for word from the Summons};
    (b) {order}; and
    (c) {costs, as the Summons words them}.

5.  {Overview, in contested evidence: what the evidence shows, as facts, in two to five paragraphs.}

A.  {HEADING THAT STATES A POINT}

6.  On [date 3], {event}. There is now produced and shown to me marked "[exhibit 1]" a copy of the [document title] dated [date 3].

7.  {One paragraph per letter: its date, what it asked, the reply or the silence, the exhibit.}

B.  {HEADING NAMING A FACTOR THE COURT WEIGHS}

8.  {Facts under the factor, with dates side by side and the other side's words quoted briefly with the paragraph.}

9.  My belief is based on {the sources}. I do not know {the gap}.

10. I am advised and verily believe that {the legal conclusion, in one sentence}. That is a matter of law, which I leave to counsel.

C.  {HEADING FOR THE OTHER SIDE'S CASE}

11. At paragraph [number] of the [document title], the [Defendant] says that "{a few words}". {Verdict sentence.} {The document, with its exhibit or page.} {One-line consequence.}

D.  CONCLUSION

12. In the circumstances, I respectfully ask the Court for an order in terms of the Summons.

AFFIRMED at the office of Messrs. [solicitors-firm],   )
[address], Hong Kong                                   )
this [date 4]                                          )    ......................
                                                       )    [deponent]
Before me,                                             )

......................
{Commissioner for Oaths / Solicitor, Hong Kong SAR}

This Affirmation is filed on behalf of the [Plaintiff].

{Interpreter's jurat, if the deponent does not read English: formulae.md section 11.}
```

## Checklist

The checker tests: numbered paragraphs; the first person; the Hong Kong opening formula, a jurat and "Before me", and (softly) the filing line; the profile's declaration words in the US; a purpose paragraph near the start; a prayer in Hong Kong; no "It is submitted", "I submit that", case citation, "a fortiori" or "the Court is invited"; soft notes for a stated legal test, "as a matter of law" outside an advised sentence, information with no named source, and the solicitors' voice.

Check by eye:

- The orders in the purpose paragraph match the summons word for word, and the prayer matches both.
- Every fact has its source, and every exhibit sentence follows its fact.
- The voice is this deponent's, and nothing in it is privileged or outside what the deponent may depose to.
- On an ex parte application, the points against the order are disclosed.
- Sums, dates and defined terms agree with the summons, the pleadings and any other witness.
- The interpreter's jurat is there if needed; the jurat uses the right form (affirmed or sworn) and the right place.
