# Evidence for a procedural application

Variant id: `procedural`. Square brackets are placeholders from `references/fields.md`; text in braces is a drafting note to be replaced or deleted.

## What it is for

Evidence in support of (or against) an application about the conduct of the proceedings rather than their merits: an extension of time, leave to file or serve a document late, leave to amend, relief from the consequences of a missed deadline, an adjournment, leave to appeal out of time, and similar applications for leave. The court is exercising a discretion. The evidence gives it the facts that discretion turns on: what the deadline was and how far it was missed; why; what has been done since; whether the step has substance; and who is prejudiced, either way.

Take the governing rule, the time limit and the test from the user. Do not supply them.

## Who gives it

Often the solicitor with conduct, because the facts are on the file. Two cautions follow.

- **Sources.** A solicitor's evidence of what the client did, knew or intended is information and belief, and names its source ("I am informed by [name], {role} of the [Plaintiff], and verily believe that …").
- **Privilege.** Reasons for delay can stray into legal advice. Describe the steps taken and their dates, not the advice given, unless the client has decided to waive privilege. Ask the user if in doubt.

Where the reason for the delay is personal to the party (illness, absence abroad, a change of representation), the party's own evidence is usually stronger. Ask the user who is to give it.

## Structure: Hong Kong

1. **Caption, title, opening words, capacity, knowledge.**
2. **Purpose.** The summons and the order sought: for example, time for [document title] extended to [date]; leave to {take the step}.
3. **The timetable.** The order or rule that fixed the deadline, when it was made and served, what it required, when time expired, and the length of the delay in days. Put the dates side by side. The arithmetic is the deponent's; the characterisation of the delay is counsel's.
4. **The reasons.** A dated account of the whole period from the start of the delay to the application, with no unexplained gap. Each step with its date and, where possible, its document. If something went wrong on the applicant's side, say what it was, plainly and once. An apology, where one is due, is a short sentence in the deponent's own words; it does not take the place of reasons.
5. **Once the problem was known.** When the deponent learned of it, what was done, and the date of this application. Promptness at this stage often matters as much as the original reason.
6. **Substance, briefly.** Where the court will look at the merits of the step (a defence, an amendment, an appeal), exhibit the draft document and give the deponent's belief in one or two sentences, in the advised form. Counsel argues the merits; the evidence identifies them.
7. **Prejudice.** To the other side: what the delay or the step does to it, with dates (any hearing or trial date, the steps still to come), and whether costs or a revised timetable would cure it. To the applicant: what is lost if the order is refused. Facts, not adjectives.
8. **Terms offered.** A new date, payment of the costs thrown away, an undertaking: consistent with the draft order and the summons.
9. **Prayer, jurat, filing line, exhibits, backsheet.**

## Notes by application

- **Extension sought before time expires.** Shorter: why the step cannot be done in time, how much more time is needed, and why that time will be enough.
- **Extension after time has expired, or relief from a missed deadline.** The full account in steps 3 to 7, with any earlier extensions and the record of compliance, stated frankly.
- **Leave to amend.** What the amendment does; when and how the facts behind it came to light; why it was not made earlier; the draft amended document exhibited; the effect on the timetable and on evidence already filed.
- **Leave to file evidence late.** What the evidence is, why it is late, why it is needed, and that the other side can answer it in time.
- **Leave to appeal out of time.** The decision and its date, the time limit, the delay and its reasons, the draft grounds or notice exhibited, a brief advised belief on prospects, and prejudice. The appeal court's own rules govern.

## Language

Plain and specific. "Between [date 1] and [date 2], I was {doing what}", not "owing to various circumstances". Dates and documents carry the account. Keep conclusions out of the deponent's mouth: words such as "inadvertent", "minimal", "short" or "excusable" are what counsel will argue the facts show, so use them only if the deponent would naturally say them.

## Differences in the US profiles

- **The instrument.** The profile's declaration form. Declarations on procedural motions are often by counsel; keep them to facts within counsel's knowledge and the documents.
- **Leave to amend.** California requires a separate declaration stating the effect of the amendment, why it is necessary and proper, when the facts giving rise to it were discovered, and why the request was not made earlier (CRC 3.1324(b)). New York requires the proposed pleading "clearly showing the changes" (CPLR 3025(b)); Washington, a copy of the proposed amended pleading marked "proposed" and unsigned (CR 15(a)); federal district courts commonly require a redline under local rules (for example S.D.N.Y. Local Civil Rule 15.1). Exhibit what the rule requires.
- **Extensions and relief from default.** The governing rule and its standard are not in the profiles. Write `[rule]`, ask the user, and do not supply the test.
- **No law, no prayer** in the declaration.

2.  I make this affirmation in support of the [Plaintiff]'s Summons dated [date 1] (the "Summons") for an order that {the order sought, word for word from the Summons}.

The Timetable

3.  By an Order dated [date 2] (the "Order"), the [Plaintiff] was to {the step} by [date 3]. The Order was served on [date 4]. There is now produced and shown to me marked "[exhibit 1]" a copy of the Order.

4.  The [Plaintiff] {took the step / has not yet taken the step} on [date 5], [number] days after time expired.

Reasons for the Delay

5.  Between [date 3] and [date 6], {what happened, with documents}.

6.  On [date 6], {the next step}. {Continue until the application, with no unexplained gap.}

7.  {Where something went wrong on the applicant's side: what it was, plainly, once; a short apology in the deponent's words where one is due.}

Steps Taken Since

8.  I learned of {the problem} on [date 7]. On [date 8], {what was done}. The Summons was issued on [date 1].

The Proposed {Document}

9.  There is now produced and shown to me marked "[exhibit 2]" a copy of the draft [document title]. I am advised and verily believe that {it raises a matter fit for the Court's consideration}. That is a matter for counsel.

Prejudice

10. The [Defendant] {has / has not} taken any step in reliance on the deadline. {Any hearing or trial date: [date 9].} {Whether costs or a revised timetable would meet any prejudice.}

11. If the order is refused, {what the applicant loses}.

12. The [Plaintiff] is willing to {terms offered}.

13. In the circumstances, I respectfully ask the Court for an order in terms of the Summons.

{Jurat, filing line, interpreter's jurat, exhibits and backsheet as in affirmation.md.}

## Checklist

The checker tests: everything in `affirmation.md`; softly, that prejudice and the reasons or delay are addressed.

Check by eye:

- The dates are right and the day count is correct; the whole period is accounted for.
- The deadline's source (order or rule) is identified, and the rule and its test came from the user.
- No privileged advice; every fact from the client names its source.
- The draft document the application concerns is exhibited.
- Prejudice is dealt with on both sides, with dates; any terms offered match the draft order.
- The tone is candid and plain; no conclusion the deponent would not say in their own words.
