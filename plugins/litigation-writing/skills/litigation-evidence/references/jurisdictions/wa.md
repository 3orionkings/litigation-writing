# Washington

This profile covers civil actions in the Washington Superior Court, with King County as the example of local practice. The governing rules are the Superior Court Civil Rules ("CR"), which keep the pre-2007 wording of the federal rules with Washington additions; the General Rules, in particular GR 13 (unsworn statements) and GR 14 (format, with Appendix 1, the Reporter of Decisions Style Sheet effective September 1, 2026); chapter 5.50 RCW (the Uniform Unsworn Declarations Act); and the King County Superior Court Local Civil Rules ("LCR") 7, 10 and 56. RCW 9A.72.085, still cited in older forms, was repealed effective July 1, 2021. Researched October 2026. Check the assigned judge's practices and the current LCR before filing.

## Document names

| Document type | Washington name |
|---|---|
| Parties | Plaintiff and Defendant |
| Claim pleading | Complaint |
| Defence | Answer; with affirmative matter and a counterclaim, titled for example "Answer, Affirmative Defenses and Counterclaim" |
| Reply | A reply to a counterclaim denominated as such, within 20 days (CR 7(a), 12(a)); a reply to an answer only if the court orders |
| Counterclaim and others | Counterclaim (compulsory under CR 13(a)); cross claim; third party complaint |
| Amendment | Amended complaint (or answer); on a motion, the proposed amended pleading "denominated 'proposed' and unsigned" (CR 15(a)) |
| Evidence instrument | Declaration under RCW 5.50.050 and GR 13; affidavits are also used |
| Witness statement | No separate form; evidence on a motion is a declaration |
| Skeleton | In King County the motion is itself the brief: one document, no separate memorandum (LCR 7(b)(5)(B)); trial brief at trial |
| Hearing note | No equivalent |
| Application papers | The motion (in the King County single-brief format), declarations with exhibits, a proposed order, a Notice of Court Date, working copies |
| List of authorities | Tables of contents and authorities are optional ("if any") and excluded from the word count |
| Request for particulars | None. A motion for a more definite statement (CR 12(e)), broader than its federal counterpart, is the nearest device |
| Attacks on a pleading | CR 12(b) motion; motion to strike (CR 12(f)). Demurrers are abolished (CR 7(c)) |

## Pleading standard

CR 8(a): a pleading which sets forth a claim for relief "shall contain (1) a short and plain statement of the claim showing that the pleader is entitled to relief and (2) a demand for judgment for the relief to which the pleader deems the pleader is entitled. Relief in the alternative or of several different types may be demanded."

- **Notice pleading.** The least demanding of the US profiles. Washington has declined the federal plausibility standard; a complaint survives CR 12(b)(6) unless no state of facts consistent with it would entitle the plaintiff to relief (see "Background"). There is no jurisdictional-statement element.
- **Form.** Each averment "simple, concise, and direct" (CR 8(e)(1)); pleadings construed to do substantial justice (CR 8(f)). Numbered paragraphs, each limited as far as practicable to a single set of circumstances; separate counts for separate transactions where that helps (CR 10(b)). An exhibit is part of the pleading (CR 10(c)).
- **Alternatives.** Alternative or hypothetical statements, and inconsistent claims, are allowed, subject to CR 11 (CR 8(e)(2)).
- **Time and place.** Averments of time and place are material for testing sufficiency (CR 9(f)).
- **Conditions precedent.** May be averred generally (CR 9(c)).
- **Foreign law.** A party who intends to raise the law of a jurisdiction outside the United States gives notice in the pleading of that jurisdiction; the substance need not be pleaded (CR 9(k)(2)). Without that notice the court applies Washington law unless that would be manifestly unjust (CR 9(k)(4)).
- **Relief.** Special damage is specifically stated (CR 9(g)). In a personal injury action the complaint states no figure but prays for damages "as shall be determined"; on request the plaintiff serves a statement of special and general damages within 15 days (RCW 4.28.360).
- **Unknown names.** A defendant whose name is unknown may be designated by any name, and the pleading amended when the true name is discovered (CR 10(a)(2)).

## Answering a pleading

CR 8(b): "A party shall state in short and plain terms the defenses to each claim asserted and shall admit or deny the averments upon which the adverse party relies. If a party is without knowledge or information sufficient to form a belief as to the truth of an averment, the party shall so state and this has the effect of a denial. Denials shall fairly meet the substance of the averments denied."

- **Admit, deny, or without knowledge.** Admit the part that is true and material and deny the rest. No reasons and no own version are required inside a denial.
- **General denial with admissions is allowed.** Unless the pleader means to controvert everything, denials may be specific, or the pleader "may generally deny all the averments except such designated averments or paragraphs as the pleader expressly admits"; a general denial of everything is subject to CR 11 (CR 8(b)). This is not the Hong Kong traverse: it carries no reasons and no positive case. Paragraph-by-paragraph answers remain the clearer course.
- **Effect of silence.** Averments other than those as to the amount of damage are admitted if not denied (CR 8(d)).
- **Affirmative defenses (CR 8(c)).** Set forth affirmatively: accord and satisfaction, arbitration and award, assumption of risk, contributory negligence, discharge in bankruptcy, duress, estoppel, failure of consideration, fault of a nonparty, fraud, illegality, injury by fellow servant, laches, license, payment, release, res judicata, statute of frauds, statute of limitation, waiver, "and any other matter constituting an avoidance or affirmative defense".
- **Nonparty at fault.** A claim that a nonparty is at fault is an affirmative defense, and the nonparty's identity, if known, is pleaded too (CR 12(i)).
- **Capacity and conditions precedent.** An issue as to a party's legal existence or capacity is raised by specific negative averment with the particulars within the pleader's knowledge (CR 9(a)). A denial of performance of a condition precedent is made specifically and with particularity (CR 9(c)).
- **Counterclaims.** A claim arising out of the same transaction or occurrence is compulsory (CR 13(a)); an omitted counterclaim needs leave (CR 13(f)). Set-off against an assignee is preserved (CR 13(j)).

Answer formulae:
- "Defendant admits the allegations of paragraph [number]."
- "Defendant denies the allegations of paragraph [number]."
- "Defendant is without knowledge or information sufficient to form a belief as to the truth of the allegations of paragraph [number] and therefore denies them."

## Particulars

- **CR 9(b):** "In all averments of fraud or mistake, the circumstances constituting fraud or mistake shall be stated with particularity. Malice, intent, knowledge, and other condition of mind of a person may be averred generally."
- **No particulars procedure.** No request for further and better particulars and no bill of particulars; detail comes through discovery, so fraud is particularised in the complaint.
- **More definite statement (CR 12(e)).** Available where a pleading is so vague or ambiguous that a party cannot reasonably be required to frame a response, "or if more particularity in that pleading will further the efficient economical disposition of the action". It is a motion, made before the responsive pleading, pointing out the defects and the details desired; an order not obeyed within 10 days may lead to the pleading being struck.
- **Special damage** (CR 9(g)) and the personal injury damages statement (RCW 4.28.360) are the other sources of detail.

## Verification and evidence

- **Signature and certificate (CR 11(a)).** Every pleading, motion and legal memorandum of a represented party is dated and signed by at least one attorney of record, with the attorney's address and Washington State Bar Association number. The signature certifies that the signer has read the paper and that, after reasonable inquiry, it is well grounded in fact, warranted by law or a good faith argument for changing it, not interposed for an improper purpose, and that denials are warranted. Sanctions may be imposed on motion or on the court's own initiative; there is no 21-day safe harbor.
- **Verification.** Petitions for dissolution of marriage, separation, validity of marriage, custody and modification of those decrees are verified; other pleadings need not be, but may be (CR 11(a)).
- **Unsworn statement under GR 13.** Wherever a matter may be supported by affidavit, an unsworn written statement may be used instead, signed in accordance with GR 30, stating that it is certified or declared true under penalty of perjury, the date and place of execution, and that it is so certified or declared under the laws of the state of Washington. The form in the rule: "I certify (or declare) under penalty of perjury under the laws of the State of Washington that the foregoing is true and correct:" followed by (Date and Place) and (Signature). The rule does not apply to writings requiring an acknowledgment, oaths of office, or oaths required before a special official other than a notary.
- **Unsworn declaration under RCW 5.50.050.** The statutory form:

> I declare under penalty of perjury under the law of Washington that the foregoing is true and correct.
>
> Signed on the . . . . day of . . . . . ., . . . ., (date) (month) (year) at . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . (city or other location, and state or country)
>
> . . . . . . . . . (printed name)
>
> . . . . . . . . . (signature)

- **Scope of chapter 5.50.** The chapter applies to a declarant inside or outside the United States (RCW 5.50.020), and an unsworn declaration has the effect of a sworn one, except for a deposition, an oath of office, an oath required before a specified official other than a notary, and a declaration to be recorded under Title 64 or 65 RCW (RCW 5.50.030).
- **Use both.** To meet GR 13 (date and place) and RCW 5.50.050 (date, city and state or country, printed name), use the RCW 5.50.050 words with the place filled in, for a declarant in Hong Kong as anywhere else. No notary is needed.
- **Summary judgment evidence (CR 56(e)).** Affidavits and declarations are made on personal knowledge, set out admissible facts, and show the affiant's competence; sworn or certified copies of papers referred to are attached. The adverse party may not rest on its pleadings.
- **Interpreters and translations.** No Washington rule was found.

## Amendment

- **As a matter of course (CR 15(a)).** Once, at any time before a responsive pleading is served; or, where no responsive pleading is permitted and the action is not on the trial calendar, within 20 days after service.
- **Otherwise.** By leave of court or the adverse party's written consent; "leave shall be freely given when justice so requires".
- **The motion.** "If a party moves to amend a pleading, a copy of the proposed amended pleading, denominated "proposed" and unsigned, shall be attached to the motion." On grant, the moving party files the signed amended pleading and serves it. A party responds within the time left for the original or 10 days after service, whichever is longer.
- **Relation back and supplemental pleadings.** CR 15(c) and (d), in the older federal wording.
- **No interlineation.** No amendment by erasing or adding words to the pleading on file without leave (CR 15(e)).
- **How changes are shown.** CR 15 has no redline rule, and King County has no LCR 15. GR 14(a) forbids colour and highlighting in filed papers, so any comparison copy supplied uses underlining for additions and strike-through for deletions, in black. No Hong Kong colour conventions. (The federal court in the same state, the Western District of Washington, does require a marked copy: see `us-federal.md`.)

## Formal rules for papers

- **GR 14(a), mandatory.** Letter-size paper (8½ by 11 inches), written on one side; first page top margin at least three inches, bottom one inch, sides one inch; later pages one-inch margins. "Papers filed shall not include any colored pages, highlighting or other colored markings." The rule extends to attachments unless compliance is impractical.
- **CR 10(a), caption.** The name of the court, the title of the action, the file number if known, and the nature of the pleading or paper; the complaint names all parties. King County requires all known parties' names in the initiating documents; initials only with a motion filed and noted with them (LCR 10(a)(1)(B)).
- **CR 10(e), recommended format.** Space at the top of the first page for the clerk's stamp and receipt of copies; below the docket number a title showing the paper's purpose and the party presenting it ("Defendant's Motion to Dismiss", never just "Motion"); at the bottom left of each page an abbreviated title and the page number; at the bottom right of the first page the preparing attorney's or firm's name, mailing address and telephone; typed names under signatures; headings; numbered paper.
- **Signature.** Dated, with the WSBA number (CR 11(a)).
- **Word limits, King County motions (LCR 7(b)(5)(B)(vi)).** Motion and response 4,200 words; reply 1,750 words. The count includes headings and footnotes and excludes the caption, any tables and the signature block. The signature block certifies, substantially: "I certify that this brief contains [number] words, in compliance with the Local Civil Rules."
- **Word limits, summary judgment (LCR 56(c)(3)).** Moving and opposing memoranda 8,400 words; reply 1,750 words; the same inclusions and exclusions; certification: "I certify that this memorandum contains [number] words, in compliance with the Local Civil Rules."
- **Attachments.** Attachments or exhibits above 25 pages are numbered consecutively at the bottom centre or right, and briefs cite those numbers (LCR 7(b)(5)(C)(ii)).

## Applications and motions

- **Timing (King County LCR 7(b)(4)).** Motion papers filed and served by 4:30 p.m. nine judicial days before the date the motion is to be considered; responses by 4:30 p.m. four judicial days before; replies by 4:30 p.m. two judicial days before. Working copies go to the judge no later than the day of service. A Notice of Court Date is filed with the motion (LCR 7(b)(5)(A)).
- **Oral argument (LCR 7(b)(3)).** Motions are decided without argument except summary judgment, temporary restraining orders and preliminary injunctions, and the other listed kinds; any party may ask for argument by putting "ORAL ARGUMENT REQUESTED" prominently on the first page.
- **The single brief (LCR 7(b)(5)(B)).** Motion, response and reply each follow this order (the reply may omit the first four): (i) Relief Requested, consistent with the proposed order; (ii) Statement of Facts; (iii) Statement of Issues; (iv) Evidence Relied Upon, specified with particularity; (v) Argument, citing authority. Authority relied on that is neither case law nor Washington law is cited with a public web address or a Westlaw citation, or supplied with the working papers.
- **Evidence (LCR 7(b)(5)(C)(i)).** Documents are attached to the brief, if no authentication is needed, or to authenticating declarations; cited portions are marked. The rule mentions highlighting or boxes; GR 14(a) bans highlighting, so use boxes.
- **Proposed orders (LCR 7(b)(5)(D)).** Each party submits one; where working copies are e-submitted, an editable version too.
- **Summary judgment (CR 56(c)).** The motion and supporting papers are filed and served at least 28 calendar days before the hearing; the opposition at least 11; rebuttal at least 5. Heard more than 14 days before trial unless leave is given. No separate statement of facts is required. The order designates the documents and evidence the court considered (CR 56(h)). In King County summary judgment is decided after oral argument unless waived or struck (LCR 56(c)(1)), and objections to admissibility go in the responsive brief (LCR 56(e)).
- **Motions to dismiss and strike.** The CR 12(b) defenses (including (6), failure to state a claim) may be made by motion before pleading; matters outside the pleading turn a (b)(6) motion into one for summary judgment. Motion to strike within 20 days where no responsive pleading is permitted (CR 12(f)). Personal jurisdiction, venue, process and service defenses are waived if omitted from the motion or the answer (CR 12(h)(1)).
- **Meet and confer.** No general requirement for these motions was found.

## Citation style

GR 14(d) and the Reporter of Decisions Style Sheet (effective September 1, 2026): the Bluebook (the sheet names the 20th edition) with Washington exceptions; cite official sources. The bracketed items show the shape of a citation only; in a draft every authority comes from the user (see `authorities.md`).

- Supreme Court: `[Case name], [volume] Wn.2d [page], [pin] ([year])`; the third series is "Wn.3d".
- Court of Appeals: `[Case name], [volume] Wn. App. 2d [page], [pin] ([year])`.
- Common practice adds the Pacific Reporter after the official cite: `[Case name], [volume] Wn.2d [page], [pin], [volume] P.3d [page] ([year])`.
- Washington abbreviations replace the Bluebook's: "Wn.2d", "Wn. App.", not "Wash. 2d" or "Wash. App."
- Case names are italicised wherever they appear.
- Washington cases: official reports first, regional reporter only where no official report exists; non-Washington cases: the regional reporter first.
- Statutes: "RCW 5.50.050", with no year for a current provision; regulations "WAC [number]"; session laws "Laws of [year], ch. [number], § [number]".
- Rules: "CR 12(b)(6)", "GR 14", "LCR 7(b)(5)(B)".

## Background: where the standards come from

Background only. Cite these in a draft only if the user supplies or verifies them.

- *McCurry v. Chevy Chase Bank, FSB*, 169 Wn.2d 96, 101 (2010): a complaint states a claim under CR 12(b)(6) if it is possible that facts could be established to support its allegations; the court declined to adopt the federal plausibility standard.
- *Young v. Key Pharmaceuticals, Inc.*, 112 Wn.2d 216, 225 (1989): the moving party's initial burden on summary judgment and the shift to the party with the burden of proof at trial, following the federal approach.
