# Hong Kong

This profile covers civil actions in the Court of First Instance and the District Court, the pleaded disputes heard in the Family Court (points of claim and points of defence), and applications in the Court of Appeal. The governing rules are the Rules of the High Court (Cap 4A, "RHC"): Order 18 (pleadings), Order 20 (amendment) and Order 41A (statements of truth), with Practice Direction 19.1 on filing and amending pleadings. The Rules of the District Court (Cap 336H, "RDC") are taken to follow the same order and rule numbers on these points; that was not checked (see "Not verified"). References in the form 18/12/18 are to paragraphs of the commentary in Hong Kong Civil Procedure (the White Book). Researched October 2026. Rules and practice directions change: check the current text, and any direction of the court or the judge, before filing.

## Document names

| Document type | Hong Kong name |
|---|---|
| Parties | Plaintiff and Defendant, never "Claimant". Family Court: Petitioner, Respondent, Intervener. Appeal: Appellant, Respondent. After judgment: Judgment Creditor, Judgment Debtor |
| Claim pleading | Statement of Claim, indorsed on the writ of summons or served separately. Never "Particulars of Claim" or "statement of case". Family Court: Points of Claim |
| Defence | Defence. Family Court: Points of Defence |
| Reply | Reply. With a defence to counterclaim it is one document, "Reply and Defence to Counterclaim" (O.18 r.3(3)) |
| Counterclaim | One document with the defence, "Defence and Counterclaim", in two headed sections |
| Amendment | Amended Statement of Claim, then Re-Amended, then Re-Re-Amended. Leave is sought by summons with the draft amended pleading annexed |
| Evidence instrument | Affirmation (affirmed) or affidavit (sworn), titled by ordinal: "2nd AFFIRMATION OF [deponent]" |
| Witness statement | Witness Statement of [witness], for trial under the court's directions |
| Skeleton | Skeleton Submissions; a reply skeleton answers the other side's |
| Hearing note | Hearing note for a call-over or case management hearing |
| Application papers | Summons (inter partes or ex parte), a supporting affirmation, a draft order |
| List of authorities | List of Authorities, with the caption of the paper it accompanies |
| Request for particulars | Request for Further and Better Particulars; the answer is a formal document bearing the title of the action and a description |
| Forms of this system | Sanctioned offer or sanctioned payment (O.22); Scott Schedule |

English CPR vocabulary does not belong in a Hong Kong draft: no "claimant", "particulars of claim", "statement of case", "Part 18 request", "Part 20 claim" or pre-action protocol sentence. Use the RHC terms above.

## Pleading standard

O.18 r.7(1): every pleading must contain, and contain only, a statement in summary form of the material facts on which the party relies, but not the evidence by which they are to be proved, and it must be as brief as the nature of the case admits.

- **Material facts.** Plead every fact needed to complete each cause of action, and nothing immaterial (18/7/6-18/7/7). A material fact left out cannot be proved at trial.
- **Facts, not evidence or law.** Plead the fact relied on, not the facts that prove it, and not conclusions of law (18/7/4-18/7/5). Put the facts in chronological order and call each person and thing by one name throughout (18/7/3). An implied contract is alleged as a fact, with the letters or conversations described generally.
- **Documents and conversations.** State their effect briefly; quote the precise words only where the words themselves are material, as in defamation (r.7(2)).
- **Do not plead the other side's burden.** Facts presumed by law, matters the opponent must prove and performance of conditions precedent (implied by r.7(4)) are not pleaded; do not anticipate the defence (18/15/9). Facts of an acknowledgment or part payment that take a claim out of limitation may be pleaded.
- **Shape of a statement of claim.** The parties and the background, then the body disclosing a cause of action against each defendant, then the prayer (18/15/1).
- **Relief.** O.18 r.15(1): the relief claimed must be stated specifically; costs need not be. The prayer opens "AND THE PLAINTIFF CLAIMS" (or "AND THE [Plaintiff] CLAIMS AGAINST THE [Defendant]") and lists each relief in numbered heads. Relief in the writ but left out of the prayer is treated as abandoned; "further or other relief" does not carry a remedy that was not claimed; a declaration must be prayed for (18/15/2, 18/15/5).
- **Scope.** No cause of action that is not mentioned in the writ or does not arise from the same facts (r.15(2)). Matters arising after the writ are introduced as such: "The matters hereinafter pleaded have arisen since the issue of the writ." (r.9; 18/9/1). No allegation inconsistent with the party's own earlier pleading (r.10).
- **Matters to be specifically pleaded.** In any pleading after the statement of claim, every matter that makes the other side's case not maintainable, might take it by surprise, or raises new issues of fact: performance, release, limitation, fraud, illegality and the like (r.8(1)). Exemplary and provisional damages are claimed in the body with their facts (r.8(3)).
- **Interest.** O.18 r.8(4) requires interest to be pleaded specifically. Plead the basis (`[statutory provision]` or the contractual term), the rate and the period, claim compound interest expressly with its basis, and keep body and prayer consistent (18/8/22). Which provision applies is for the user.
- **Damages.** Special damage is claimed explicitly with particulars; loss that is not the necessary and immediate consequence of the wrong, and any calculated loss, is pleaded with the facts behind it (18/8/35, 18/12/11, 18/12/44). In personal injury actions the medical report and statement of special damages are served with the statement of claim (r.12(1A)).
- **Alternatives.** Inconsistent factual allegations only with reasonable grounds, expressly in the alternative, each in its own paragraph, with the primary case identified, and never on matters within the pleader's own knowledge (r.12A; 18/12A/1-18/12A/5).
- **Points of law and convictions.** A point of law may be raised (r.11), clearly and never in a form that reads as a traverse. A party relying on a Hong Kong conviction states that intention in the pleading, with the conviction and its date, the court, and the issue it goes to; the other side denies it, says it was wrong, or denies its relevance in its own pleading (r.7A).
- **Strike-out exposure.** O.18 r.19(1): no reasonable cause of action or defence; scandalous, frivolous or vexatious; may prejudice, embarrass or delay the fair trial; otherwise an abuse of process. No evidence is admissible on ground (a) (r.19(2)). The test is stated under "Background".

## Answering a pleading

The grammar is specific traverse, with reasons and the defendant's own version.

- **Every allegation dealt with.** An allegation of fact not traversed is deemed admitted (r.13(1)). Traverse by denial or non-admission (r.13(2)). Every allegation in a statement of claim or counterclaim that is not admitted must be specifically traversed; a general denial or general non-admission is not enough (r.13(3)).
- **Reasons and own version.** A denial states its reasons and, where the defendant's account differs, the defendant's own version, pleaded as a positive case in the defence itself (r.13(5); 18/13/10-18/13/12). A defendant who sets out no positive case is taken only to require proof (r.13(6)) and cannot call contrary evidence.
- **Non-admission.** Puts the other side to proof and sets up no case. Use it for matters outside the defendant's knowledge and say so. "Put to strict proof" is never the only answer to an allegation the defendant can deny (18/13/5).
- **Split and widen.** Split a compound allegation and answer each part; turn "and" into "or" and "all" into "any"; add "or any other sum", "or at all", "whether as alleged or at all", so the denial is not evasive (18/13/6-18/13/7).
- **Admit what is plain.** Admit facts that cannot sensibly be disputed (18/13/2). Where an allegation is true but answerable, admit it and plead the matter that avoids it (confess and avoid, 18/8/2).
- **Affirmative matter in its own paragraphs.** Limitation, set-off, estoppel, illegality, release and the other r.8 matters are pleaded separately from the traverses, each with its facts (18/8/2). Limitation is pleaded cause of action by cause of action, with the date of accrual and the date of expiry (18/8/26). A set-off states the debt or matter set off with particularity (r.17). Tender is pleaded with the payment into court (r.16).
- **Do not traverse** matters of law, matters the plaintiff has not raised, or the reply in advance (18/13/6).
- **General traverse.** The closing general traverse is at most a convenience for immaterial allegations; it never answers an essential one (18/13/6).
- **Joinder of issue, in a reply only.** If there is no reply there is an implied joinder of issue on the defence (r.14(1)), operating as a non-admission (r.14(4)). There is no joinder of issue on a statement of claim or a counterclaim (r.14(3)), so a defence to counterclaim traverses specifically.
- **Reply.** Served only where needed to plead an affirmative answer under r.8 (r.3(1); 18/3/2); it raises no new cause of action and nothing inconsistent with the statement of claim. A reply and a defence to counterclaim go in one document with two headed sections and continuous numbering (r.3(3); 18/3/1). No pleading after a reply without leave (r.4).
- **Times.** Defence within 28 days after the time for acknowledging service or service of the statement of claim, whichever is later (r.2(1)); reply and defence to counterclaim within 28 days (r.3(4)). Pleadings close 14 days after the reply or defence to counterclaim, or 28 days after the defence if neither is served (r.20).

Traverse formulae (keep verbatim):
- "Paragraph [number] of the Statement of Claim is admitted." / "is denied." / "is not admitted."
- "Save and except that …, paragraph [number] is admitted." (the qualification, the part not admitted, goes where the ellipsis is)
- "The [Defendant] avers that:" (the reasons and own version follow)
- "and the [Plaintiff] is put to strict proof thereof."
- "Save as hereinbefore expressly admitted or not admitted, the [Defendant] denies each and every allegation contained in the Statement of Claim as though the same were herein set forth and traversed seriatim."
- In a reply: "The [Plaintiff] joins issue with the [Defendant] upon its Defence save in so far as it contains admissions."

## Particulars

O.18 r.12(1) requires the necessary particulars of every claim, defence or other matter pleaded, and in particular: (a) of any misrepresentation, fraud, breach of trust, wilful default or undue influence; (b) of the facts behind any alleged condition of mind (disorder, malice, fraudulent intention and so on), knowledge excepted; (c) of facts relied on in mitigation of, or otherwise about, the amount of damages. Particulars of debt, expenses or damages longer than 3 folios (216 words, a figure or date counting as one word) go in a separate document referred to in the pleading, which says whether it has been or will be served (r.12(2); 18/12/57). Particulars cannot supply a missing material fact (18/12/1).

The cause-of-action checklist (18/8/5-18/8/39, 18/12/2-18/12/53), in short:
- **Fraud and dishonesty.** Plead fraud only on clear instructions and credible material. Allege it distinctly: who did what, when and how, on facts that are not consistent with innocence. Allege actual knowledge; "knew or ought to have known" does not allege it, and words like "wilfully and recklessly" do not make a plea of fraud (18/8/19, 18/12/18). Bad faith is particularised and the opponent's knowledge alleged (18/12/12).
- **Misrepresentation.** Each representation: its nature and extent, who made it and to whom, whether oral or written (identifying the document), and whether express or implied. An implied representation cannot be run on a plea of an express one (18/12/28).
- **Agreement.** Date, parties, oral or written (who made it, or which document), the terms relied on and the consideration if not under seal (18/12/4, 18/12/8, 18/8/37).
- **Implied terms.** The term and the facts from which it arises; a bare label such as "necessity and/or business efficacy" is not enough (18/12/19). A novel legal duty is pleaded expressly (18/8/25).
- **Breach of trust and wilful default.** Breach by breach (18/12/48). **Dishonest assistance:** the acts of assistance. **Knowing receipt:** disposal in breach of trust or fiduciary duty, beneficial receipt of traceable assets, knowledge (18/12/12). **Fiduciary duty:** the relationship it arises from and an actual conflict with loss (18/12/13, 18/8/17).
- **Undue influence and duress.** Undue influence: its character, the acts and their dates (18/12/51). Duress: where, when, by whom, over whom and how (18/8/12).
- **Conspiracy.** Overt acts showing each conspirator joined the agreement, that it was carried out, and that carrying it out caused the loss (18/12/9).
- **Negligence and contributory negligence.** Facts founding the duty, the duty, each breach, then injury and loss (18/12/30); contributory negligence pleaded as a defence with particulars (18/8/8, 18/12/10).
- **Estoppel.** Representation or assurance, reliance, detriment (18/8/15).
- **Unjust enrichment.** Facts for each element and the unjust factor (18/8/38).
- **Defamation.** The precise words, the publication, its date and recipients; natural meaning and innuendo in separate paragraphs, with the extrinsic facts and who knew them (18/12/20, 18/12/25).
- **Negotiable instrument.** Date, amount, parties, notice of dishonour or the facts excusing it (18/12/31).
- **Knowledge, notice, intention, malice.** Allege expressly where material; particulars of knowledge or notice may be ordered (r.12(4); 18/12/24, 18/12/32).
- **Quantum and mitigation (defence).** A positive case with particulars; a non-admission of loss is not enough (r.12(1)(c); 18/8/28, 18/8/30).

Requests and orders: ask by letter first, or the court may refuse an order (r.12(6)). The court orders particulars only if necessary for fair disposal or saving costs (r.12(3)-(3B)), and not before the defence unless needed to plead (r.12(5)). The answer sets each item of the request or order immediately above the particulars given, identified by paragraph and sub-paragraph (r.12(7); 18/12/71). Particulars are verified by a statement of truth (r.20A) and bind the party (18/12/68).

## Verification and evidence

**Statement of truth.** Every pleading, every amendment and every set of particulars (voluntary, requested or ordered) is verified by a statement of truth under O.41A (O.18 r.20A; O.20 r.13). Every paragraph must be covered by a verifier who can honestly speak to it; several verifiers may be used, and each alternative under r.12A must be capable of honest verification (18/20A/3-18/20A/4). An unverified pleading may be struck out; a false statement made without honest belief is a contempt. Forms (keep verbatim):
- Individual: "I, [name], believe that the facts stated in this [document title] are true." Family Court: "I, [name], the [Petitioner] in this cause believe that the facts stated in this [document title] are true."
- Director for a company: "I, [name], director of the [Plaintiff], believe that the facts stated in this [document title] are true.", then signature and "For and on behalf of the [Plaintiff]".
- Company by an authorised signatory: "The [Plaintiff] believes that the facts stated in this [document title] are true." and "I am duly authorised by the [Plaintiff] to sign this statement of truth.", then Signed / Name / Title / Date.
- The writ form gives the pattern "I/ The plaintiff believe(s) that the facts stated in this statement of claim are true."
- Witness statement (as used in practice): "I, [witness], believe that the facts stated in this Witness Statement are true and the opinion expressed in it is honestly held."

On amendment the statement of truth names the amended document ("this Amended Statement of Claim") and is signed afresh.

**Interpreter's or translator's certificate** for a signatory who does not read English, placed after the statement of truth (keep verbatim; say "interpreted" where the document was read over to the signatory and "translated" where a written translation was given):

> I certify that I, [name], solicitor of [solicitors-firm], of [address], well understand the English language and [language], and that I have truly, distinctly and audibly interpreted the contents of this document and the statement of truth to the person signing the statement of truth, who appeared to understand (a) the document and approved its content as accurate and (b) the statement of truth and the consequences of making a false statement, and made his signature in my presence.

The language, the verb and the pronoun ("his" or "her") are fields; the wrong pronoun is a common slip.

**Affirmation.** First person; the order is caption, title with ordinal, opening, capacity, knowledge sentence, purpose, body, prayer, jurat, filing line, backsheet, exhibits.
- Opening: "I, [deponent], of [address], do hereby solemnly, sincerely and truly affirm and say as follows:"
- Capacity: "I am the [Plaintiff] in this Action." / "I am a director of the [Plaintiff] and I am duly authorised to make this affirmation on its behalf."
- Knowledge: a sentence that the facts are within the deponent's own knowledge unless stated otherwise, and that other facts are true to the best of the deponent's knowledge, information and belief, with their source given where they appear.
- Purpose: "I make this affirmation in support of the [Plaintiff]'s Summons dated [date] for the following orders:"
- Belief and law: "I am advised and verily believe that …"; "I am informed by [name] and verily believe that …". Law appears only in this form; no case or test is stated.
- Exhibits: "There is now produced and shown to me marked "[exhibit]" a copy of [document title]." placed after the fact it proves. Codes are the deponent's initials, the affirmation ordinal and a running number, or one paginated bundle per affirmation cited by page. Each exhibit has a cover sheet: "This is the exhibit marked "[exhibit]" referred to in the Affirmation of [deponent] affirmed on [date]." with "Before me," and the commissioner's signature. An index lists Exhibit, Date, Description, No. of pages.
- Jurat and filing line:

```
AFFIRMED at [address]                 )
this [date]                           )   [deponent]
                                      )
Before me,

Commissioner for Oaths

This Affirmation is filed on behalf of the [Plaintiff].
```

- Interpreter's jurat where the deponent does not read English (keep verbatim): "I, [name], of [address], do solemnly and sincerely affirm and say that I well understand [language] and the English language and that I have truly, distinctly and audibly interpreted the contents of this document to the Affirmant." Older forms say "[language]".
- Who takes it: the jurat is taken before a commissioner for oaths (in practice at a solicitors' office); outside Hong Kong, before a notary public. The statutory basis was not researched.
- An affidavit is sworn rather than affirmed; its opening and jurat use the oath form, which was not researched.

**Witness statement.** Opening "I, [witness], of [address] do make this statement and say as follows:". No knowledge sentence, no "I am advised"; second-hand matter is "my understanding"; documents are annexures ("annexed hereto marked "Annexure [number]""), not exhibits; evaluation, if any, in a short closing section; signed with the statement of truth.

## Amendment

**Without leave (O.20 r.3).** A party may amend a statement of claim, defence, counterclaim, defence to counterclaim or reply once, before the pleadings close, and must serve it. Not separately served particulars, not a re-amendment, not after close of pleadings (20/4/2). The other side may apply within 14 days to disallow it (r.4), and an amendment that would have been refused on an application for leave is struck out. The receiving party may amend in response within 14 days, confined to consequential changes; otherwise its pleading stands and issue is joined on the new allegations (r.3(2)-(3), (6); 20/4/7-20/4/8). A writ is amended without leave once before close of pleadings, and after service not to change parties, capacity or causes of action (r.1).

**With leave (O.20 r.5, r.8).** By summons, before trial to the master. The court amends to determine the real question in controversy (r.8(1)), and only if the amendment is necessary for disposing fairly of the matter or for saving costs (r.8(1A)). The amendments must be exactly formulated, in the summons or "as set forth in red ink in the draft [document title] annexed hereto" (20/8/4). An affirmation is needed where fraud or another serious allegation is pleaded for the first time, where lateness needs explaining, or after limitation (20/8/4, 20/8/10). After limitation, only the three r.5 cases: a genuine misnomer, a capacity held at commencement or since acquired, or a new cause of action on the same or substantially the same facts (r.5(2)-(5)). The usual term is that the amending party pays the costs of and occasioned by the amendment (20/8/51). An order to amend lapses if not acted on within the time it sets or 14 days (r.9).

**By agreement (O.20 r.12).** Written agreement, at any stage, but never to add, omit or substitute a party.

**How the change is shown (O.20 r.10; PD 19.1 Part II).**
- Every amended document is indorsed with a statement that it is amended, the date, and either the judge or master and the date of the order, or the rule relied on (r.10(2)). The legend goes at the head of the document, above the caption, with a line for the Registrar. Legends (keep verbatim):
  - Without leave: "Amended and underlined in red the [date] pursuant to RHC, Order 20, Rule 3" (Rule 1 for a writ).
  - With leave: "Amended pursuant to the Order of [judge] dated the [date]".
  - By agreement: "Amended pursuant to RHC, Order 20, Rule 12".
  - Fresh document: "Replaced by Amendment made the [date] pursuant to O.20 r.3" (or "pursuant to the order dated the [date]").
- Colours by round: first amendment red; re-amendment green; re-re-amendment violet; a fourth round yellow. Insertions are underlined in the colour of their round; deletions are struck through in that colour, never erased. Each round has its own legend, stacked at the head. In a word-processed pleading use coloured underline and coloured strike-through.
- The title on the face and on the backsheet moves with the round: Amended, Re-Amended, Re-Re-Amended. The backsheet gains a "Re-filed" line.
- New paragraphs take letter or decimal suffixes (11A, 11B, 12.1); existing paragraphs keep their numbers.
- Where alterations would make the document hard to read, prepare a fresh document (r.10(1)); a reissued writ is marked "Reissued on amendment" and the original "Superseded by amendment".
- Update together: the legend, the title on face and backsheet, the dated signature block, the statement of truth (r.13), and every figure that recurs in the body and the prayer, interest included. Where the amendment departs from allegations already verified, explain why in the application (20/13/1).

## Formal rules for papers

- **Face of every pleading (O.18 r.6(1)).** The year the writ was issued and the action number; the title of the action exactly as on the writ (18/6/2); the description of the pleading; the date on which it was served.
- **Statement of claim.** Also bears a statement of the date on which the writ was issued (r.15(3)), for example "(Writ of Summons issued on the [date])".
- **Paragraphs and figures.** Consecutively numbered paragraphs, one allegation in each so far as convenient (r.6(2)). Dates, sums and other numbers in figures, not words, and not figures followed by words (r.6(3); 18/6/4).
- **Indorsement and signature.** The solicitors' firm name and business address, or the party's name and address if in person (r.6(4)); signed by counsel if settled by counsel, otherwise by the solicitor or the party (r.6(5)). Block: "Dated the [date]", [counsel-name], "Counsel for the [Plaintiff]", [solicitors-firm], "Solicitors for the [Plaintiff]".
- **Combined pleadings.** "Defence and Counterclaim" and "Reply and Defence to Counterclaim" have separately headed sections and continuous numbering; the counterclaim's facts sit under the heading "Counterclaim" and may incorporate the defence by reference (18/3/1, 18/18/1).
- **Caption.** Short action number at top right; the court in capitals ("IN THE HIGH COURT OF THE HONG KONG SPECIAL ADMINISTRATIVE REGION", "COURT OF FIRST INSTANCE", the action number line); "BETWEEN"; the parties with their roles at the right margin; the title between rules.
- **Backsheet.** The action number, the court heading, the parties, the title between rules, then three slots that are always present:

```
Dated the [date 1]
Filed the [date 2]
Served the [date 3]
```

followed by [solicitors-firm], "Solicitors for the [Plaintiff]", the address, telephone, fax and reference. The "Served" slot carries the r.6(1)(e) date of service and is the slot most often left out. PD 19.1 Part I requires the filed copy to show the date of service.
- **Affirmation.** Title with ordinal; filing line after the jurat; exhibit cover sheets and index.
- **Skeleton submissions.** Counsel's name, "Counsel for the [Plaintiff]" and the date at the end; a "To:" distribution block may follow; the list of authorities comes after it, repeats the caption, numbers each authority by its first appearance, and carries its own signature.
- **Page, word and font limits.** None was found for skeletons or pleadings (see "Not verified").
- **Colour.** Required for amendments (above); no ban.

## Applications and motions

- **The papers.** A summons; a supporting affirmation (or affidavit); skeleton submissions for the hearing; a draft order; a list of authorities.
- **Summons formal parts (keep verbatim).** A margin note naming the rules relied on ("Order [number] rule [number] of the Rules of the High Court (Cap 4A)"); "LET ALL PARTIES CONCERNED ATTEND before [judge] in Chambers (open to public) of the Court of First Instance at the High Court of Hong Kong, [address] on [date] at [time], on the hearing of an application on the part of the [Plaintiff] that:"; the orders in numbered paragraphs, with the costs order last ("The costs of this Summons be to the [Plaintiff]."); "Dated the [date]"; "Registrar"; "THIS SUMMONS WAS TAKEN OUT BY [solicitors-firm 1], Solicitors for the [Plaintiff]."; "Time estimate: [period]"; "To: [solicitors-firm 2], Solicitors for the [Defendant]". A summons for summary judgment (O.14 r.1) carries the notice that a party intending to oppose should send a copy of any affidavit intended to be used to the applicant's solicitors before the hearing.
- **Supporting affirmation.** States the summons and the orders sought in its purpose paragraph and ends with a prayer for an order in terms of the summons.
- **Leave to amend.** Summons under O.20 r.5 or r.8 with the draft amended pleading annexed in colour; consequential directions for the other side's amended pleading; an affirmation where needed (above); the costs term chosen by the user, the default being that the amending party pays the costs of and occasioned by the amendment.
- **Strike-out (O.18 r.19).** Say precisely what is attacked and on which limb; PD 19.1 para 5 requires five clear working days' written notice of the grounds of an application under r.19(1)(a). Apply promptly, normally before close of pleadings (18/19/3); consider an alternative under O.14A or O.33.
- **Particulars (O.18 r.12(3)).** Only after a letter request (r.12(6)).
- **Other applications.** Summary judgment (O.14 r.1) and judgment on admissions (O.27 r.3); setting aside service or jurisdiction (O.12 r.8); enforcement by examination of a judgment debtor (O.49B), garnishee order (O.49) and charging order (O.50).
- **Length limits.** None was found.

## Citation style

The bracketed items below show the shape of a citation only. In a draft, an authority comes from the user (see `authorities.md`); never fill these from memory.

- Neutral citation: `[Case name] [year] HKCFI [number] at §[number]`; the court codes include HKCFA, HKCA, HKCFI, HKDC and HKFC.
- Reported cases: `[Case name] ([year]) [volume] HKCFAR [page] at §[number]`; `[Case name] [year] [volume] HKLRD [page] at §[number]`; `[Case name] [year] [volume] HKC [page]`; `[Case name] [year] HKEC [number]`.
- Pinpoints use "§": "at §[number]", "§§[number]-[number]". Give the full citation at first mention, then one short name.
- Ordinances: by name and chapter, with the section: "[statutory provision]" in the form "s.[number] of the [name] Ordinance (Cap [number])". In a pleading, cite a statute only where the claim depends on it.
- Rules: "O.18 r.19(1)(a)" in submissions; "Order 14, rule 1 of the Rules of the High Court (Cap 4A)" in summonses and affirmations; "RHC" once defined.
- White Book commentary is cited by paragraph number (for example 18/19/4); no set citation form or edition was researched.

## Forms that exist only here

- **Request for Further and Better Particulars.** A letter request first (r.12(6)); then a formal request identifying each paragraph and sub-paragraph and saying exactly what is sought; the answer repeats each item and gives the particulars beneath it (r.12(7)), is a formal document with the title of the action, and is verified.
- **Statement of truth on pleadings and particulars**, with the interpreter's or translator's certificate.
- **Coloured amendments** with stacked legends, and the "Replaced by Amendment" fresh document.
- **The backsheet** with "Dated / Filed / Served" slots.
- **Defence and Counterclaim; Reply and Defence to Counterclaim** as single documents.
- **Sanctioned offers and sanctioned payments (O.22).** Not researched here; take its form and consequences from O.22 itself.
- **Scott Schedule** for multi-item disputes: item number, description, each party's position on liability and amount, a column for the court; the party challenging an item states the sum it says is reasonable (18/12/41).
- **Personal injury.** Medical report and statement of special damages served with the statement of claim (r.12(1A)).
- **Family Court points of claim and points of defence**, with Petitioner, Respondent and Intervener.
- **Hearing notes** for call-overs and case management hearings.

## Background: where the standards come from

No case is named here. The standards are stated by rule and White Book paragraph:
- Strike-out (O.18 r.19; 18/19/4-18/19/5): only in plain and obvious cases, where the pleading is unarguably bad on its face; the facts pleaded are assumed true and disputed facts resolved for the party attacked; the applicant bears the burden; a weak case or sparse detail is not enough; difficult or developing law is not decided on a strike-out. A pleading short only of particulars is met by a r.12 request, and the court usually gives leave to amend a curable defect.
- What pleadings are for (18/0/2-18/0/3): they define the issues, and the issues define the evidence; an unpleaded issue cannot come in through the evidence (18/8/3-18/8/4).
- The functions of particulars (18/12/1).
- Leave to amend (20/8/6-20/8/6A, 20/8/7-20/8/8, 20/8/10-20/8/12): the real question in controversy; necessity under r.8(1A); the limitation stages; serious allegations first raised late; amendment at trial.
