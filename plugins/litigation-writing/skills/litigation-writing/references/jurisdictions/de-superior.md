# Delaware Superior Court

This profile covers civil actions in the Superior Court of the State of Delaware. The governing rules are the Superior Court Civil Rules ("Super. Ct. Civ. R." or "Rule"), which keep the numbering of the federal rules but the older, pre-2007 federal wording, with Delaware additions; chapter 39 of title 10 of the Delaware Code, in particular 10 Del. C. § 3901 on affidavits of defense; and, for citation, the Bluebook as Rule 107(d)(4) applies it, with the Delaware Uniform Citation (2008 edition, keyed to the 18th edition of the Bluebook) as the Delaware overlay. Researched October 2026. The Court of Chancery has its own rules and profile (`de-chancery.md`); the federal court in Delaware is covered in `us-federal.md`.

## Document names

| Document type | Superior Court name |
|---|---|
| Parties | Plaintiff and Defendant |
| Claim pleading | Complaint |
| Defence | Answer; in an action within 10 Del. C. § 3901, an affidavit of defense where the complaint demands one |
| Reply | A reply to a counterclaim denominated as such; a reply to an answer only if the court orders (Rule 7(a)) |
| Counterclaim and others | Counterclaim; cross-claim; third-party complaint and third-party answer |
| Amendment | Amended complaint (or answer), indicating plainly how it differs from the pleading amended (Rule 15(aa)) |
| Evidence instrument | Affidavit (Rule 56(e)) |
| Witness statement | No separate form |
| Skeleton | Opening brief, answering brief, reply brief (Rule 107) |
| Hearing note | No equivalent |
| Application papers | Motion, signed under Rule 11 (Rule 7(b)(3)), with briefs under Rule 107 where briefing is called for |
| List of authorities | Table of citations in the brief (Rule 107(e)) |
| Request for particulars | None. A motion for a more definite statement (Rule 12(e)); a written request for the amount of unliquidated damages claimed (Rule 9(g)) |
| Abolished | "Demurrers, pleas, and exceptions for insufficiency of a pleading shall not be used." (Rule 7(c)) |

## Pleading standard

Rule 8(a): a claim for relief contains "(1) a short and plain statement of the claim showing that the pleader is entitled to relief and (2) a demand for judgment for the relief to which the party deems itself entitled."

- **No jurisdictional statement** is required, unlike the federal Rule 8(a)(1).
- **The motion-to-dismiss standard** in Delaware's state courts is reasonable conceivability, lower than federal plausibility (see "Background"; not verified from a primary source).
- **Negligence with particularity.** Rule 9(b) diverges from the federal rule: "In all averments of fraud, negligence or mistake, the circumstances constituting fraud, negligence or mistake shall be stated with particularity." Plead the acts and omissions said to be negligent, not a bare allegation of negligence.
- **No sum for unliquidated damages (Rule 9(g)).** A pleading praying for unliquidated money damages demands damages generally without specifying the amount; items of special damage are specifically stated. On written request the pleader serves, within 10 days, a statement of the amount claimed, which is not filed except on order.
- **Form (Rule 10(a)-(c)).** Caption, numbered paragraphs each limited as far as practicable to one set of circumstances, separate counts, adoption by reference; an exhibit is part of the pleading. Papers are "plainly written or printed and backed" (Rule 10(d)). Filing under a pseudonym needs the court's approval (Rule 10(e)).
- **Alternatives** are permitted (Rule 8(e)(2)).
- **Conditions precedent** may be pleaded generally; a denial is made with particularity (Rule 9(c)).

## Answering a pleading

- **Admit, deny, or lack knowledge.** Rule 8(b) follows the older federal wording: admit or deny the averments, state lack of knowledge or information sufficient to form a belief (which operates as a denial), and admit the true part of a partly true averment. A general denial is "subject to the obligations set forth in Rule 11". No reasons and no own version are required; there is no Hong Kong "save and except" traverse. Answer each numbered paragraph.
- **Effect of silence.** Averments not denied are admitted, save as to the amount of damage (Rule 8(d)).
- **Affirmative defenses (Rule 8(c)).** The federal list with "discharge in bankruptcy" added; plead each separately.
- **Deemed admissions unless denied by affidavit (Rule 8(dd)).** The existence of a corporation or partnership, the signatures on an instrument sued on whose copy is filed with the complaint as the statute requires, and the agency of the operator of a motor vehicle are admitted unless denied by affidavit served with the answer.
- **Affidavit of defense (10 Del. C. § 3901; Rules 3(b), 12(a)).** In actions on bills, notes, bonds or other instruments for the payment of money, book accounts, foreign judgments, and scire facias on recognizances, judgments or mortgages, the plaintiff may, by a notation on the face of the complaint, require specified allegations to be answered by an affidavit setting forth the specific nature and character of the defense and its factual basis.
- **Time.** Answer within 20 days after service of process, complaint and any affidavit (Rule 12(a)).
- **Counterclaims.** Compulsory and permissive counterclaims, omitted counterclaims by leave, and cross-claims follow the older federal wording (Rule 13(a)-(c), (f), (g)).

Answer formulae:
- "Defendant admits the averments of paragraph [number]."
- "Defendant denies the averments of paragraph [number]."
- "Defendant is without knowledge or information sufficient to form a belief as to the truth of the averments of paragraph [number], and they are therefore denied."

## Particulars

- **Rule 9(b)** requires particularity for fraud, negligence and mistake (above).
- **Rule 9(g)** gives the defendant a written request for the amount of unliquidated damages claimed.
- **More definite statement (Rule 12(e)).** A motion before the responsive pleading; an order must be obeyed within 10 days.
- The Superior Court has no counterpart of the Hong Kong request for particulars; anything more is obtained in discovery.

## Verification and evidence

- **No statement of truth.** "Except when otherwise specifically provided by statute or rule, pleadings need not be verified or accompanied by affidavit." (Rule 11(a)). The two exceptions found are the affidavit of defense under 10 Del. C. § 3901 and the affidavit denials under Rule 8(dd).
- **The signature certifies (Rule 11(b)).** Rule 11 follows the 1993 federal version: the signature certifies proper purpose, warranted legal contentions, evidentiary support and warranted denials; a sanctions motion is subject to a 21-day safe harbor (Rule 11(c)(1)(A)).
- **Affidavits on summary judgment (Rule 56(e)).** Made on personal knowledge, setting forth facts that would be admissible in evidence, and showing affirmatively that the affiant is competent to testify. The adverse party "may not rest upon the mere allegations or denials" of its pleading.
- **Unsworn declarations.** Whether Delaware law allows an unsworn declaration in place of an affidavit in the Superior Court, and in what words, was not researched. Do not substitute the federal 28 U.S.C. § 1746 form without checking.
- **Who may take an affidavit, and interpreter or translation requirements,** were not researched.

## Amendment

- **As of course (Rule 15(a)).** Once, "at any time before a responsive pleading is served" (not the federal 21-day window).
- **Otherwise,** "by leave of court or by written consent of the adverse party; and leave shall be freely given when justice so requires". Respond to an amended pleading within 10 days.
- **How changes are shown (Rule 15(aa)).** "A party serving an amended pleading shall indicate plainly in the amended pleading in what respect the amendment differs from the pleading which it amends." Serve a complete amended pleading in which the changes are marked. The rule does not prescribe the marking; underlining additions and striking through or bracketing deletions, the method the federal court in Delaware requires (D. Del. LR 15.1), meets it. No Hong Kong colour conventions.
- **Relation back and supplemental pleadings** (Rule 15(c), (d)).

## Formal rules for papers

- **Briefs (Rule 107(b)).** Double-spaced; Times New Roman 14-point; two spaces between sentences; case names italicised or underlined; footnotes single-spaced in Times New Roman 14-point.
- **Length (Rule 107(h)).** Without leave, an opening or answering brief may not exceed 8,000 words and a reply brief 5,500 words, exclusive of the appendix. A certificate of compliance stating the word count is required (Rule 107(h)(2)).
- **Contents (Rule 107(e)).** A table of contents; a table of citations; a statement of the case; a statement of the questions involved; the argument under headings.
- **Pleadings.** Caption, numbered paragraphs and backing as above (Rule 10).
- **Signature.** By counsel under Rule 11.

## Applications and motions

- **Motions** are signed in accordance with Rule 11 (Rule 7(b)(3)) and briefed under Rule 107.
- **Rule 12 defenses.** The seven defenses (including failure to state a claim) may be raised by motion; judgment on the pleadings under Rule 12(c). The court "may order stricken from any pleading any insufficient defense or any redundant, immaterial, impertinent or scandalous matter", on a motion within 20 days where no responsive pleading is permitted (Rule 12(f)).
- **Summary judgment (Rule 56).** The older federal structure: judgment "shall be rendered forthwith if the pleadings, depositions, answers to interrogatories, and admissions on file, together with the affidavits, if any, show that there is no genuine issue as to any material fact and that the moving party is entitled to a judgment as a matter of law" (Rule 56(c)). Cross-motions for summary judgment are deemed a stipulation for decision on the record (Rule 56(h)).
- **Meet and confer** and proposed-order practice were not researched.

## Citation style

Rule 107(d)(4): the Bluebook, "with no reference to State Reporter Systems or other parallel citations"; the older Delaware style is also permitted. The Delaware Uniform Citation gives the Delaware forms. The bracketed items show the shape of a citation only; in a draft every authority comes from the user (see `authorities.md`).

- Reported cases: the Atlantic Reporter. `[Case name], [volume] A.3d [page], [pin] (Del. [year])` for the Supreme Court; for this court `([court] [year])` with "Del. Super. Ct." in Bluebook form (as Rule 107's own example uses) or "Del. Super." in the Delaware Uniform Citation form. Chancery is "Del. Ch.".
- Unreported cases, online database first: `[Case name], [year] WL [number], at *[pin] (Del. Super. [date])`; the Delaware form drops the docket number. Then a memorandum copy, then a journal.
- Older Delaware style, still permitted: `[Case name], Del. Super., [volume] A.2d [page], [pin] ([year])`.
- Short forms: `[Case name], [volume] A.3d at [pin].`; `Id. at [pin].`; for a database cite `[Case name], [year] WL [number], at *[pin].`
- Statutes: "10 Del. C. § 3901". Rules: "Super. Ct. Civ. R. [number]", with no date for the current rule.

## Forms that exist only here

- **The affidavit of defense** under 10 Del. C. § 3901, triggered by a notation on the face of the complaint.
- **Affidavit denials** of corporate existence, signatures and agency under Rule 8(dd).
- **Negligence pleaded with particularity** (Rule 9(b)).
- **The general damages demand** with no sum, and the unfiled statement of the amount served on request (Rule 9(g)).
- **The Rule 107 brief**: Times New Roman 14-point, two spaces between sentences, 8,000 and 5,500 words with a certificate.

## Background: where the standards come from

Background only. Cite these in a draft only if the user supplies or verifies them.

- *Central Mortgage Co. v. Morgan Stanley Mortgage Capital Holdings LLC*, 27 A.3d 531 (Del. 2011): the Delaware Supreme Court's statement that the pleading standard on a motion to dismiss in Delaware's courts is reasonable conceivability, not federal plausibility. The citation was confirmed through a search index; the opinion text and the formulation were not read from a primary source.
